## How it is built \{#how-it-is-built\}

- **The figures are an [auto grid](/components/auto-grid).** `auto-grid` with `--auto-grid-min: 12rem` holds as many tiles as fit at 12rem or wider: four across a wide sheet, two on a tablet, one or two on a phone. The grid counts the columns itself.
- **The panels are a three-column grid inside a container.** The main sheet is `container-inline`. The grid is one column until the sheet reaches 56rem, then `grid-cols(3)@container(--4xl)`; the wide panel takes two of them with `grid-col-span(2)@container(--4xl)`. When the sidebar leaves, the sheet gets wider and the panels may go back to three columns: that is the point of measuring the container and not the screen.
- **Each tile is a surface from the library.** A figure is a [stat](/components/stat) on a flat [sheet](/components/surface#sheet); a panel is a [card](/components/card). On a sheet, a tile is `sheet-flat` or `card-flat`: one contour and no shadow, since a shadow on a raised surface would stack two elevations.
- **A wide table scrolls in its panel.** The table stands in a [scroll area](/components/scroll-area) with `scroll-area-inline`, which keeps a narrow panel from widening the page and fades the edge where columns continue. The region has `tabindex="0"` and a name, so the keyboard can scroll it.

## Density \{#density\}

One gap, `gap-md`, between every tile and panel keeps the page one grid. Resist a second gap for "groups": if two panels belong together, make them one panel.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Each panel is a `<section>` named by its title with `aria-labelledby`, so the panels can be reached as regions.
- A figure's change says its direction in words for assistive technology; see [stat](/components/stat#accessibility).
- A meter carries its value as text inside the element and a visible label beside it.
