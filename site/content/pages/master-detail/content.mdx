## How it is built \{#how-it-is-built\}

- **`container-inline` on the frame** makes it a size container. Every `@container(…)` utility inside measures the frame, not the screen.
- **A row with a fixed height.** `flex h(440)` gives both panes the same height; `overflow-y-auto` on the list and on the detail makes each scroll alone, and `min-h(0)` lets the list shrink under its header, which stays. In a full-height app, take the height from a [fixed shell](/pages/app-shell#fixed) instead of a number.
- **The list is fixed and the detail is fluid.** The list is `w-full` until the frame reaches 48rem, then `w(300)@container(--3xl)` with `shrink(0)`. The detail is `grow(1) basis(0) min-w(0)`: it takes the rest and never pushes the list.
- **Below 48rem there is one pane.** The detail is `hidden` and returns with `flex@container(--3xl)`. A narrow screen shows the list; choosing a row goes to the detail on its own page. That is navigation, so it belongs to your router, not to the layout.
- **The rows stand inside the pane.** The list is `p-sm`, so the current row's sheet keeps clear of the pane's edges and of the line under the header. The header's title is `pxs(10)`, the rows' `--list-inset`, so it starts where the rows' words do.
- **The line between the panes** is `bxe(1)@container(--3xl) bxe-solid@container(--3xl)`: it exists only while there are two panes.

The current row is marked with `aria-current="true"` on a `list-row` in a [`list-interactive`](/components/list).

- **The way back is the first thing in the page.** One [link](/components/link) over the title, named for where it goes: API keys. The pane lost the list when it became a page, and this link is what gives it back, so it stands where a reader looks first and where the keyboard arrives first after the header.
- **The header is a [page header](/blocks/page-header).** The name is the page's `<h1>` with its status beside it, and the two actions are a split that wraps, `flex flex-wrap items-end justify-between`. The name's column is `grow(1) basis(320) min-w(0)`: a long name wraps beside the actions while it has 320px, and under that the actions drop below it and keep their order.
- **A grid of thirds inside a container.** The main region is `container-inline`. The grid is `grid-cols(1)` until the region gives it 48rem, then `grid-cols(3)@container(--3xl)`: the key and the activity take two of them with `grid-col-span(2)@container(--3xl)` and the details take the third, about 19rem at the full measure. In a column that narrow the [data list](/components/data-list) sets each term over its value by itself.
- **Three sections, in reading order.** The source is the key, its details, its activity. In two columns the details stand beside both of the others, `grid-row-span(2)@container(--3xl)`, and `items-start` keeps each section at its own height. In one column the same order stands as it is written: what the key is and may do, the facts about it, then the record, which is the longest and the least urgent.
- **One measure and one gutter.** The main region is `max-w(1040) mx-auto px-lg`. The navbar runs the full width, so its padding follows the measure: `--navbar-padding-x: max(1.5rem, calc(50% - 31rem))` is 1.5rem on a narrow screen and the measure's own edge on a wide one. The brand, the way back, the title and the first words of each section start on one line at every width.

Where the link goes, and what Rotate key and Revoke do, belongs to your application: here they are places, not behavior.

## Three ways to build it \{#three-ways\}

| When | Use | The reader gets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The panes are a region of a page | Utilities and a container, [as above](#how-it-is-built) | The list and one item at once, from 48rem of room |
| The reader resizes the panes | A [splitter](/components/splitter) | The same, shared as they choose |
| The panes are the whole app | A [fixed shell](/pages/app-shell#fixed), with the list as its sidebar | The same, as tall as the viewport |
| There is no room for two panes, or the item is linked to from elsewhere | A [standalone](#standalone) detail on a route of its own | One item at any width, at its own address |

Two panes read at once keep the list's place: the reader goes down the rows and compares one item with the next without leaving. They need the width for both, and the choice is either a selected state the page holds or a part of one address. A detail route has an address, a place in the history and a title of its own, and it works at any width; it loses the list, so it gives a way back, and the list should return to the row the reader left. Most applications want both: two panes where there is room, and the same detail as a page where there is not, at one address so that a link opens whichever fits.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The list is a list of links, each to a detail. `aria-current="true"` marks the one on show.
- Label the frame, and give the detail a heading that names what was chosen, so a reader who lands in it knows where they are.
- A pane that scrolls and holds nothing focusable needs `tabindex="0"`, so the keyboard can scroll it.
- When the detail is hidden on a narrow screen it is `display: none`, out of the accessibility tree; do not leave a control that points at it.
- A standalone detail has the landmarks of a [page](/pages/page): a skip link, one `<header>` with the navigation, one `<main>`. Its three sections are each named by their heading, so they can be reached as regions.
- Its way back is a link whose name is the list's, "API keys"; the arrow before it is `aria-hidden`. Do not use `history.back()` for it: a reader who arrived by a link has no list behind them.
- The item's name is the page's one `<h1>`, the sections are `<h2>` and the scopes an `<h3>`. In the panes the same name is an `<h3>` under the list's `<h2>`: the level follows where the detail stands, so set it when the detail moves.
- `aria-current="true"` belongs to the row in the list, and there is no list on this page: only the header's link to the section it is in carries `aria-current="page"`. In the timeline, `aria-current="true"` marks the newest event.
- After the route changes, move focus to the main region or the `<h1>` and update the document's title, so the new page is announced.
