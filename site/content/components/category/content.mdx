A category is which piece of data something belongs to: a project, a calendar, a team, a label. Master UI tells a category by one of the preset's 23 named palettes, through four roles each palette gives: a tinted **surface**, the **text** that reads on it, a decorative **line** and a **mark** for a dot or a bar. A `category-<name>` class sets the four roles for itself and for what it holds, and the parts that opt in read them.

## Chrome, status, category, content \{#roles-of-color\}

Neutral chrome is the default; status colors say how something is, category colors say what it belongs to, content colors show the data as it is, and the brand color keeps its one moment.

- **Status** is a tone of a component, such as `badge-success`: done, failing, at risk. It always comes with words that say the same.
- **Category** is a class around the data, such as `category-blue`: a project, a calendar, a tag. Red here names a category, never a danger, and a badge is one or the other, never both.
- **Content** is a color the data is: a paint, a theme's accent, a chart's series. A [swatch](/components/swatch) shows it as it is.

A button, a field, a dialog or any other chrome inside a category keeps its neutral face: only `badge-category`, `chip-category`, `schedule-event-category` and `icon-frame-category` read the roles.

## The palettes \{#palettes\}

Every palette sits at one lightness per role, so no category weighs more than another, and each role clears its contrast in Moonstone, Obsidian and Paper: the text 4.5:1 and the mark 3:1, on its own surface and on the base and raised faces. The hue and the chroma are the palette's own; a yellow's text leans toward amber, as the preset's darker steps do, instead of turning olive.

## Tell it in words too \{#words\}

A color is never the only way a category is told. Write its name in the badge or the chip, or beside the dot; a reader who cannot tell teal from green, a printout in grey and a screen reader all need the words. A category color chosen by a user is not a role of the theme and has not been checked for contrast: map it to the nearest named palette, or show it as content, in a swatch.

In forced colors the system colors replace the tints: the words, the edges and a chosen filter keep their system colors, and only a swatch, whose color is its content, keeps its own.

## Retune a palette \{#retune\}

The roles are tokens of the theme, `--color-category-<name>-surface`, `-text`, `-line` and `-mark`, so a theme retunes a palette as it retunes any color: set the four in a theme of its own and keep their contrast. To give a scope a palette of its own, set the four public properties on it.

```css name=theme.css
@theme {
  --color-category-teal-surface: light-dark(oklch(96% .03 190), oklch(28% .04 190));
  --color-category-teal-text: light-dark(oklch(45% .08 190), oklch(87% .09 190));
}
```
