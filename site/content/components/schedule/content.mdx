A schedule shows a day as hours down a rail, each event as tall as it lasts: a calendar's day view, the rooms of a conference, a clinic's appointments. The events are an ordered list, and each says its title and its time in words; the stylesheet places them on a scale of hours with a hairline at every hour and a label on each. Events that overlap stand side by side. There is no script and nothing is measured.

What lasts whole days stands in a band over the hours. A month shows each day's events in lanes, and an agenda lists them by day. A [schedule view](#schedule-view) is the schedule a reader works, by the day, the week, the month or as an agenda, driven by the `schedule` behavior.

## How an event gets its place \{#how-an-event-gets-its-place\}

The hours run from `--schedule-from` to `--schedule-to`, set on the `schedule`, and each hour is `--schedule-hour` tall, 48px unless you say otherwise. Each `schedule-event` sets `--start` and `--end` in hours of the same clock, so half past ten is 10.5 and a quarter to two in the afternoon is 13.75. The event begins and ends at those hours, a pixel clear of the hairline over it and the one under it, so two events that meet keep a line between them.

`schedule-rail` holds one label for each hour from `--schedule-from` to `--schedule-to`, the last one included, and stands each on the middle of its hairline. Write the labels the way your readers tell time; the rail is 48px wide (`--schedule-rail`).

A whole day at 48px an hour is over 1,100 pixels tall. Show the hours that matter, or put the schedule in a [scroll area](/components/scroll-area) and let it scroll.

## Events that overlap \{#events-that-overlap\}

Events that share time share the width. Give each of them `--lanes`, how many stand side by side there, and `--lane`, its place among them from 0 at the start. Above, the design review and the interview are two lanes of one stretch. Working out the lanes is a question of the data, not the drawing: whatever renders the list decides them, and the stylesheet only places.

## Short events \{#short-events\}

An event is a size container, so it reads its own height and its words follow:

- From an hour at the default size, the title stands over the time.
- Shorter, the title and the time share one line, centered in the event, and the time is left out of sight when the two do not fit.
- With no room for a line, as for a quarter of an hour at the default size, the event is its tinted sheet alone. Its words stay in the list for assistive technology, and its place on the rail says when it is.

## Now and the current event \{#now\}

`schedule-now` on the schedule draws an ink line at `--now`, in hours like the events, with a dot where it meets the start of the day. The line passes under the events, so it never strikes through their words.

The event marked `aria-current="true"` is an ink sheet: the one in progress, or the one open beside the schedule.

## Several days \{#several-days\}

For a few days side by side, put each day's `schedule-events` in a `schedule-day` with a `schedule-heading` over it. The days share the width and the rail, and their headings and hours line up across them. Mark today's column `aria-current="date"`: its heading takes the strong ink, and the line of now runs through it with its dot, and through the other days as a hairline.

## Events that open something \{#events-that-open-something\}

When an event opens its details, put `schedule-event` on a link or a button inside the list item instead of on the item. It is placed the same way, takes a step firmer tint under the pointer and draws the focus ring for the keyboard.

```html
<li>
  <a class="schedule-event" href="/events/design-review" style="--start: 10; --end: 11.5">
    <span class="schedule-title">Design review</span>
    <span class="schedule-time"><time datetime="10:00">10:00</time> – <time datetime="11:30">11:30</time></span>
  </a>
</li>
```

## Whole days \{#whole-days\}

An event that lasts a day or more stands in `schedule-allday`, a band under the headings and over the hours, with its name, such as "All day", in the rail's column. Each event in it is a `schedule-event-lane`: it covers `--span` days from day `--column`, on lane `--lane`. The band runs across the days, so each `schedule-day` names its column with `--column` and the schedule says how many there are in `--schedule-days`.

## A month \{#month\}

`schedule-month` is a month as weeks of seven days. Each `schedule-week` writes a day, then the events that start on it, then its more button, so a screen reader meets them in the order of the days; the stylesheet places each in its column and lane. The day's number, `schedule-date-number`, is a round button that opens the day, ink for today. A day shows `--lanes` events, three by default, and a `schedule-more` button for the rest, which opens a [popover](/components/popover) that lists them. A short event in a month is plain, `schedule-event-plain`: a dot, its time and its title, without a sheet.

In a room narrower than 36rem the lanes give way: each day shows a dot for each of its events, three at most, under its number, which opens the day. Nothing is squeezed into seven narrow columns of text.

`schedule-agenda` lists the same events by day, the date at the start of each row and its events in a `schedule-list` beside it, or over them in a narrow room. It suits a phone, a printout and a reader who would rather read than look.

The month and the agenda are written from the events, so they come from the behavior or a framework component, as in the view below.

### The events \{#the-events\}

Each event has a stable `id`, a `title`, a `start` and an `end`. An all-day event has `allDay: true` and days for its ends, `YYYY-MM-DD`; another has local times, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm`. The `end` is exclusive: an all-day event on the 13th alone ends on the 14th, and an event that ends at midnight ends with the day before. `category` names a palette, such as `blue` for `category-blue`, and `href` makes the event a link; without one it is a button whose `value` is its id, which the page listens to.

Times are already in the zone the schedule is shown in, and a repeating event arrives as its occurrences: time zones and repeats are the application's to resolve. Days are counted with the calendar, kept at noon, so a daylight-saving change never moves an all-day event to the day before. The view does not edit, move or store events; it shows them and says which one was pressed.

### The behavior \{#behavior\}

```js name=app.js
import { schedule } from '@master/ui/behaviors/schedule.js'

const team = schedule(document.querySelector('.schedule-view'), { events, today: '2026-11-13' })
team.value // { date: '2026-11-13', view: 'month' }
```

| Option | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `events` | `[]` | The events, as above |
| `value` | today's month | The day and the view to show first: `{ date, view }`, the view one of `day`, `week`, `month` or `agenda` |
| `today` | the browser's | Today, as `YYYY-MM-DD`; give it when a server renders the schedule, so both sides agree |
| `locale` | the page's `lang` | A BCP 47 tag for the names of days and months |
| `weekStart` | the locale's | The first day of a week, 0 for Sunday to 6 for Saturday |
| `lanes` | `3` | How many events a day of a month shows before the rest go behind its more button |
| `from`, `to` | `8`, `18` | The hours a day or a week shows at least; they reach further for an earlier or a later event |
| `messages` | English | The band's name, the more button's words and name, and the empty agenda's sentence |
| `draw` | none | Takes over writing the title and the body, for a renderer that writes them itself |

The reader's moves dispatch `input` and `change` from the root; the function it returns has a `value` to read and to set, and setting it dispatches nothing. `scheduleLayout(value, options)` returns what a view shows as data, the weeks with each event's lane, the hours with each event's place, the band and the agenda's days, so a server, a framework and the behavior lay out the same events the same way.

## Schedule, gantt or calendar \{#schedule-gantt-or-calendar\}

A schedule is for events placed in time: the hours of a day read down, or the days of a month. Spans of a plan on one shared scale, one row to each thing, are a [gantt](/components/gantt). A month to pick a day from, or a range of days, is a [calendar](/components/calendar), and moments in the order they happened are a [timeline](/components/timeline).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Write the events as an `<ol>` in order of time, and name it: with `aria-label` for one day, or with the day's heading through `aria-labelledby` on its `schedule-day`.
- Each event says its title and its time in words, with `<time datetime>` for the hours, so it reads "Design review, 10:00 to 11:30" without the drawing. The rail is a picture of the same scale: hide it with `aria-hidden="true"`.
- The current event and today are marked with `aria-current`, which a screen reader announces. The line of now is drawn by the stylesheet; say the time in words where it matters.
- In forced colors each event is outlined, the current one is filled with the highlight color, and the hairlines and the line of now keep the system's colors.
- A month is not a date grid: its days are numbers that open a day and its events are links or buttons, each named with its title and when it is. A more button says how many events it lists and on which day. A category's color is never the only way an event is told apart.
- The view's controls keep focus as they move; a day's number, which leaves with the month, hands focus to the name of the day it opened.
- Name the time zone the hours are in near the title, such as "Times in Taipei (GMT+8)", wherever a reader may be in another zone: the rail and the events say only the time on the clock.

## React, Svelte and Vue \{#frameworks\}

The `schedule` component writes the period from `scheduleLayout()`, so the first HTML a server sends holds it, and attaches the behavior, which answers the header and the day numbers. Its value is `{ date, view }`, and pressing an event without an `href` is heard as `onEventClick`, or `eventClick` in Vue. Give `today` and `locale` when it is rendered on a server. Copy it into the project with `npx master-ui add schedule`; [React, Svelte and Vue](/guide/frameworks) says how the components are built and used.
