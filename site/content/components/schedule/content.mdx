A schedule shows a day as hours down a rail, each event as tall as it lasts: a calendar's day view, the rooms of a conference, a clinic's appointments. The events are an ordered list, and each says its title and its time in words; the stylesheet places them on a scale of hours with a hairline at every hour and a label on each. Events that overlap stand side by side. There is no script and nothing is measured.

## How an event gets its place \{#how-an-event-gets-its-place\}

The hours run from `--schedule-from` to `--schedule-to`, set on the `schedule`, and each hour is `--schedule-hour` tall, 48px unless you say otherwise. Each `schedule-event` sets `--start` and `--end` in hours of the same clock, so half past ten is 10.5 and a quarter to two in the afternoon is 13.75. The event begins and ends at those hours, a pixel clear of the hairline over it and the one under it, so two events that meet keep a line between them.

`schedule-rail` holds one label for each hour from `--schedule-from` to `--schedule-to`, the last one included, and stands each on the middle of its hairline. Write the labels the way your readers tell time; the rail is 48px wide (`--schedule-rail`).

A whole day at 48px an hour is over 1,100 pixels tall. Show the hours that matter, or put the schedule in a [scroll area](/components/scroll-area) and let it scroll.

## Events that overlap \{#events-that-overlap\}

Events that share time share the width. Give each of them `--lanes`, how many stand side by side there, and `--lane`, its place among them from 0 at the start. Above, the design review and the interview are two lanes of one stretch. Working out the lanes is a question of the data, not the drawing: whatever renders the list decides them, and the stylesheet only places.

## Short events \{#short-events\}

An event is a size container, so it reads its own height and its words follow:

- From an hour at the default size, the title stands over the time.
- Shorter, the title and the time share one line, centered in the event, and the time is left out of sight when the two do not fit.
- With no room for a line, as for a quarter of an hour at the default size, the event is its tinted sheet alone. Its words stay in the list for assistive technology, and its place on the rail says when it is.

## Now and the current event \{#now\}

`schedule-now` on the schedule draws an ink line at `--now`, in hours like the events, with a dot where it meets the start of the day. The line passes under the events, so it never strikes through their words.

The event marked `aria-current="true"` is an ink sheet: the one in progress, or the one open beside the schedule.

## Several days \{#several-days\}

For a few days side by side, put each day's `schedule-events` in a `schedule-day` with a `schedule-heading` over it. The days share the width and the rail, and their headings and hours line up across them. Mark today's column `aria-current="date"`: its heading takes the strong ink, and the line of now runs through it with its dot, and through the other days as a hairline.

## Events that open something \{#events-that-open-something\}

When an event opens its details, put `schedule-event` on a link or a button inside the list item instead of on the item. It is placed the same way, takes a step firmer tint under the pointer and draws the focus ring for the keyboard.

```html
<li>
  <a class="schedule-event" href="/events/design-review" style="--start: 10; --end: 11.5">
    <span class="schedule-title">Design review</span>
    <span class="schedule-time"><time datetime="10:00">10:00</time> – <time datetime="11:30">11:30</time></span>
  </a>
</li>
```

## Schedule, gantt or calendar \{#schedule-gantt-or-calendar\}

A schedule is for the hours of a day, read down. Spans of a plan on one shared scale, one row to each thing, are a [gantt](/components/gantt). A month to pick a day from is a [calendar](/components/calendar), and moments in the order they happened are a [timeline](/components/timeline).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Write the events as an `<ol>` in order of time, and name it: with `aria-label` for one day, or with the day's heading through `aria-labelledby` on its `schedule-day`.
- Each event says its title and its time in words, with `<time datetime>` for the hours, so it reads "Design review, 10:00 to 11:30" without the drawing. The rail is a picture of the same scale: hide it with `aria-hidden="true"`.
- The current event and today are marked with `aria-current`, which a screen reader announces. The line of now is drawn by the stylesheet; say the time in words where it matters.
- In forced colors each event is outlined, the current one is filled with the highlight color, and the hairlines and the line of now keep the system's colors.
