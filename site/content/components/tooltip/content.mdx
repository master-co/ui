A tooltip is a short label in ink that names a control or shows its shortcut. It is a hint popover: put `popover="hint"` and `.tooltip` on the label, and `interestfor` on the control. The browser shows it after a short delay on hover and on keyboard focus, and on a long press on a touch screen; it hides it on <kbd>Esc</kbd>, and never closes a menu that is already open. CSS anchor positioning centers it above the control.

## Placement \{#placement\}

A tooltip sits 6px above its control and flips below when there is no room. `tooltip-bottom` prefers the space below, for controls in a top bar.

<div id="tip-deploy" popover="hint" class="tooltip tooltip-bottom">Ships the main branch to production</div>`} />

## Content \{#content\}

Keep a tooltip to a few words. It wraps at 16rem and balances its lines. A [`kbd`](/components/kbd) inside reads on the ink surface without a variant, because the tooltip retunes the tokens the keycap uses. For anything a reader has to act on, such as a link or a form, use a [popover](/components/popover).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- A tooltip adds to a control's name; it does not replace it. Keep `aria-label` on an icon-only button, since touch screens show a tooltip only on a long press.
- The browser connects the tooltip to its control, so assistive technology announces it as a description.
- Interest invokers ship in Chrome and Edge. In a browser without them the tooltip does not appear and the control keeps its accessible name. To show it there as well, call `showPopover()` and `hidePopover()` from pointer and focus handlers; the stylesheet is the same.
