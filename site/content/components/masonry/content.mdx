Masonry packs tiles of their own heights into lanes of one width. Each tile goes where the lanes end highest, so a short picture leaves no row of empty space beside a tall one, and every picture keeps the shape it was taken in. It is a layout: it draws no surface; the tiles are [figures](/components/figure), [cards](/components/card) and [sheets](/components/surface).

Like an [auto grid](/components/auto-grid), it counts its own lanes from the room it is given, none narrower than `--masonry-min`, and needs no query. Where the browser has grid lanes, the grid does the packing; elsewhere a multi-column box does the same with one difference, in the order the tiles are read, described [below](#fallback).

## How it is built \{#how-it-is-built\}

- **Lanes counted from the room.** `masonry` makes as many equal lanes as fit at `--masonry-min` or wider, and never one wider than itself, so a phone does not overflow. The gallery sets `--masonry-min` to 10rem, which keeps two lanes on a phone.
- **Packing by the platform.** Where `display: grid-lanes` is supported, the lanes are grid columns and the browser places each tile in the lane that ends highest. Elsewhere they are the columns of a multi-column box, which fills one column before the next and balances their heights.
- **Each tile whole.** `masonry-item` keeps a tile from breaking across two columns, and keeps the gap above it. The fallback needs a class on the tile for both, so every tile has one.
- **Tiles at their own shape.** Each picture is a [figure](/components/figure) with `--figure-ratio` set to its shape, so the lane holds its place before the picture loads and nothing moves when it arrives.

## Lanes \{#lanes\}

`--masonry-min` is the narrowest a lane may be, 14rem by default. A smaller minimum makes more, narrower lanes: 9 or 10rem for pictures, which keeps two lanes on a phone; 16rem or more for cards with paragraphs. `--masonry-gap` is the space between lanes and between the tiles in a lane, 1rem.

Tiles need not be pictures. Anything that is as tall as what it holds can share the lanes: a note, a quote, a figure with a longer caption.

## Grid lanes and the fallback \{#fallback\}

Masonry is written once and laid out by whichever the browser has.

| | With grid lanes | Without them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The lanes | The columns of `display: grid-lanes` | The columns of a multi-column box, `columns: var(--masonry-min)` |
| Where a tile goes | The lane that ends highest, in the order of the markup | Down the first column until it is full, then the next |
| The order seen | Across the lanes: the first tiles make the top row | Down each column: the first tiles make the first column |
| The gap between tiles | The grid's gap | A margin, which the top of a column cuts off |

Grid lanes are new, and not every engine has them yet; [browser support](/guide/browser-support#layout) lists the layouts that use them. Without them nothing is lost but the order: every tile is packed, whole and at its shape. Follow the numbers below to see which one your browser used.

Choose masonry when that difference does not matter: a gallery, a board of notes, a wall of quotes, read in any order. When the order does matter, as in a feed with the newest first, use an [auto grid](/components/auto-grid), whose rows always read across.

## When to use it \{#when\}

| The tiles | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Each as tall as what it holds, read in any order | Masonry |
| Of one size, or meant to line up in rows | An [auto grid](/components/auto-grid) |
| Of a few sizes, arranged once, some meaning more | A [bento](/components/bento) |
| On one row, scrolled through | A [carousel](/components/carousel) |

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The order of the markup is the reading order and the order of the keyboard in both layouts. With grid lanes the order seen follows it across the lanes; in the fallback it follows it down each column. Neither moves a tile out of its place in the sequence.
- Use a list, `<ul class="masonry">`, for a set of like things, so their number is announced. The tiles are its items.
- A picture that says something needs `alt` text even when it has a caption: the caption adds to the picture, and does not stand in for it.
