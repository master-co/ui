A coachmark is one step of a tour: a small card of ink that points at a control and says what it does. It is a popover, so the browser holds it in the top layer and moves focus into it, and CSS anchor positioning places it against the element that opened it, with an arrow that points there. A beacon, a dot of ink that sends out a ring now and then, marks the place on the page and is the button that opens it. A tour opens several in turn, with the `tour` behavior.

The card is ink on moonstone and pearl on obsidian, the inverse surface of [sheet-inverse](/components/surface) laid flat, so it stands apart from the sheets of ordinary popovers and from the page under it, and its arrow joins it without a seam. A default [button](/components/button) on it reads as the action; a quiet one takes the card's ink, and a tint of it under the pointer.

## Anatomy \{#anatomy\}

Each part is optional except the title.

| Class | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `coachmark-count` | Where the step stands in the tour, such as 2 of 4, or a word such as New, in a quieter ink |
| `coachmark-title` | The heading of the step; give it an id and label the coachmark with it |
| `coachmark-text` | One or two sentences that say what the control does |
| `coachmark-actions` | Small buttons along the foot. The first of several stands at the start, such as Skip; the others line up at the end, such as Back and Next |

The actions sit 12px from the card's edges, so a small button's corner nests in the card's: the card's corner is the button's corner plus that inset.

## Placement \{#placement\}

A coachmark opens centered under the element that opened it, with the arrow's point 6px from that element, and flips above when there is no room below. `coachmark-top` prefers the space above, for a control near the foot of the screen or a beacon on a control's top corner, as in the first example.

The arrow points at the element right before the coachmark in the markup, so put the coachmark right after its beacon or the control it explains; in a tour, it points at the tour's spotlight, which stands over the step's target. While the coachmark is open, that element is named as an anchor, and the arrow follows its middle: it stays on it when an edge of the viewport pushes the card aside, keeps clear of the card's rounded corners, and moves to the other edge of the card when the card flips. One coachmark is open at a time, as in a tour.

## Beacon \{#beacon\}

`coachmark-beacon` is a 10px dot on a button, with a rim of the surface it stands on and a target of 24px, 44px on a touch screen. It opens its coachmark with `popovertarget` and needs an `aria-label` that says what it opens. The page places it: on the top corner of the control it marks, with `absolute` utilities inside a `relative` wrapper, or beside it in a row.

Its ring asks to be noticed. It rests as a halo while the beacon is under the pointer or has focus, while its coachmark is open, and with reduced motion. Remove the beacon once the reader has seen its step.

## A tour \{#tour\}

A tour is a few coachmarks opened one after another, each against the control it explains. Hold its steps in a `coachmark-tour`, in the order they are taken, each with `popover="manual"`, so a click on the page, such as trying the control a step points at, does not end the tour. A step names its target with a selector in `data-target`; without one, it points at the element right before it in the markup. Its buttons say what they do in their `value`: `next`, `back`, `skip`, and `done` on the last step.

The `tour` [behavior](/guide/behaviors) does the rest. A button with `command="--start"` and `commandfor` naming the tour starts it; the behavior opens each step against its target with `showPopover({ source })`, so the browser anchors the card there and CSS places it, and moves focus into it, to Next or Done. A `coachmark-spotlight` in the tour is opened over the target first: the step's arrow points at it, wherever the step stands in the markup.

The tour may hold the region it explains, as here, or only its steps and its spotlight: a step finds its target anywhere in the document. Closed, the steps and the spotlight take no room, so the tour can be the row it stands in.

A single coachmark opened by its beacon needs none of this. It is `popover="auto"`: the browser closes it on an outside click and on <kbd>Esc</kbd>, and returns focus to the beacon, with no script at all.

## Steps that wait \{#waiting\}

A step can wait for the reader, and for its target.

- **An action.** A step with `data-advance` names an event on its target, such as `click`, that moves the tour on. Give it no Next: it asks the reader to do the thing itself, and the tour goes on once the page has answered. Skip and <kbd>Esc</kbd> still leave.
- **A target that comes later.** When a step's target is not in the page, or is not rendered, the step waits for it and opens as soon as it appears: a field that a button reveals, a row that is still loading. A target that has not appeared after three seconds, or the `wait` given to the behavior, is passed over, and past the last step the tour is complete. It does not stop on a card that points at nothing, and a step that cannot be shown should hold nothing the reader cannot find without it.

## Spotlight \{#spotlight\}

`coachmark-spotlight-dim` dims the page around the step's target with the scrim of a [dialog](/components/dialog) and leaves the target lit, 4px around it, with the corner of a control plus that inset. The scrim is a ring of shadow as wide as the screen, drawn by the spotlight where anchor positioning holds it over the target, so it follows the target as the page scrolls. It fades in as the tour starts and out as it ends; from one step to the next the lit place moves at once.

The scrim is paint, not a wall: the page under it still answers the pointer and the keys, so the reader can try what a step points at, and the tour stays out of the way of anything else they do. Use it for a first tour of a screen, where the eye needs to be led; leave it off for a tip about one new control.

## The behavior \{#behavior\}

`tour` takes the root, the element with the `coachmark-tour` class, and returns the function that detaches it. `attach()` from `@master/ui/behaviors` finds every tour on the page.

```js name=app.js
import { tour } from '@master/ui/behaviors/tour.js'

const root = document.querySelector('#deploys-tour')
const deploys = tour(root)

deploys.value = 0 // starts the tour, as the button does
root.addEventListener('end', (event) => {
  if (event.detail.completed) localStorage.setItem('deploys-tour', 'seen')
})
```

- **The value** is the index of the step the tour is on, from 0, or `null` while it is not running. Set it to open a step, or to `null` to end the tour; that dispatches nothing.
- **Events.** The root dispatches a bubbling `change` each time the tour moves to another step, with the index in `detail.value`. When the reader ends the tour, it dispatches `change` with `null`, then `end`, whose `detail.completed` is true when they went through to the last step, and false when they left before it, with Skip, <kbd>Esc</kbd> or anything else that closed the step.
- **Focus** moves into each step as it opens: to the action with `autofocus`, else to Next or Done, else to the step itself. When the tour ends, focus goes back to where it was when the tour started, unless the reader has since moved it into the page.
- **Options.** `target(step, index)` finds a step's target, for a page that holds the targets itself; `wait` is how long a step waits for its target, in milliseconds, 3000 by default.
- **Invoker commands.** Where the browser has no `command` and `commandfor`, the behavior hears the start button's click itself.

Without the behavior, nothing starts the tour; the steps stay closed and the page is whole without them. Remember that a reader has finished a tour, from the `end` event, and do not start it on their own again: offer it from a button they can find later.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Give the coachmark `role="dialog"` and label it with its title through `aria-labelledby`. It is not modal: the page stays usable around it, and in a tour, under the spotlight too.
- Put `autofocus` on the main action of a coachmark opened by its beacon, so focus moves into the card as it opens and a keyboard can go on with <kbd>Enter</kbd>. A tour's steps take focus from the behavior.
- Every step can be left: a Skip or Done action, <kbd>Esc</kbd>, and for a single coachmark a click elsewhere.
- Start a tour from something the reader does, such as a button, never as the page loads: it is help, and it should not take focus from the task they came for.
- Say nothing in a tour that cannot be found without it: a reader who skips it, or a step that is passed over, must not miss anything.
- Placed right after its beacon in the markup, the coachmark is next in the tab order after it, wherever the card is drawn.

## React, Svelte and Vue \{#frameworks\}

The `tour` component writes one step for each item of its data, each with its title, its text, its count and the actions it needs, and passes the index of the open step in and out, with the end of the tour as `onEnd`. A step's target is a selector, an element, a ref or a function that finds it. Copy it into the project with `npx master-ui add tour`; [React, Svelte and Vue](/guide/frameworks) says how the components are built and used.

## Browser support \{#browser-support\}

The coachmark is placed with CSS anchor positioning: `position-area` and `position-try-fallbacks` for the card, and `anchor()` between the card and the element it points at for the arrow and for a tour's spotlight. In a browser without anchor positioning it still opens and closes, centered in the viewport where the browser places any popover, and has no arrow; the spotlight dims nothing, since it could not leave the target lit. The `source` option of `showPopover()` gives a tour's step its anchor; without it, a step opens without one. The start button uses invoker commands, which the behavior stands in for where they are missing. The fade and the short slide use `@starting-style`; without it the card and the scrim appear and leave at once.

The card enters with a fade and slides 4px toward its anchor, and leaves with a shorter fade. It does not scale as other popovers do: a scaled card would hold its arrow inside it, out of reach of the element the arrow points at.
