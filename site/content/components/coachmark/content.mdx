A coachmark is one step of a tour: a small card of ink that points at a control and says what it does. It is a popover, so the browser holds it in the top layer and moves focus into it, and CSS anchor positioning places it against the element that opened it, with an arrow that points there. A beacon, a dot of ink that sends out a ring now and then, marks the place on the page and is the button that opens it.

The card is ink on moonstone and pearl on obsidian, the inverse surface of [sheet-inverse](/components/surface) laid flat, so it stands apart from the sheets of ordinary popovers and from the page under it, and its arrow joins it without a seam. A default [button](/components/button) on it reads as the action; a quiet one takes the card's ink, and a tint of it under the pointer.

## Anatomy \{#anatomy\}

Each part is optional except the title.

| Class | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `coachmark-count` | Where the step stands in the tour, such as 2 of 4, or a word such as New, in a quieter ink |
| `coachmark-title` | The heading of the step; give it an id and label the coachmark with it |
| `coachmark-text` | One or two sentences that say what the control does |
| `coachmark-actions` | Small buttons along the foot. The first of several stands at the start, such as Skip; the others line up at the end, such as Back and Next |

The actions sit 12px from the card's edges, so a small button's corner nests in the card's: the card's corner is the button's corner plus that inset.

## Placement \{#placement\}

A coachmark opens centered under the element that opened it, with the arrow's point 6px from that element, and flips above when there is no room below. `coachmark-top` prefers the space above, for a control near the foot of the screen or a beacon on a control's top corner, as in the first example.

The arrow points at the element right before the coachmark in the markup, so put the coachmark right after its beacon or the control it explains. While the coachmark is open, that element is named as an anchor, and the arrow follows its middle: it stays on it when an edge of the viewport pushes the card aside, keeps clear of the card's rounded corners, and moves to the other edge of the card when the card flips. One coachmark is open at a time, as in a tour.

## Beacon \{#beacon\}

`coachmark-beacon` is a 10px dot on a button, with a rim of the surface it stands on and a target of 24px, 44px on a touch screen. It opens its coachmark with `popovertarget` and needs an `aria-label` that says what it opens. The page places it: on the top corner of the control it marks, with `absolute` utilities inside a `relative` wrapper, or beside it in a row.

Its ring asks to be noticed. It rests as a halo while the beacon is under the pointer or has focus, while its coachmark is open, and with reduced motion. Remove the beacon once the reader has seen its step.

## A tour \{#tour\}

A tour is a few coachmarks opened one after another. Give each step `popover="manual"`, so a click on the page, such as trying the control a step points at, does not end the tour, and let a short script move between the steps: `showPopover({ source })` opens a step against the element before it, and the step's actions call it for the next or the previous one. A manual popover does not close on <kbd>Esc</kbd> by itself, so the script does that too, and returns focus to where the tour began.

    else start.focus()

A single coachmark opened by its beacon is `popover="auto"`: the browser closes it on an outside click and on <kbd>Esc</kbd>, and returns focus to the beacon, with no script at all.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Give the coachmark `role="dialog"` and label it with its title through `aria-labelledby`. It is not modal: the page stays usable around it.
- Put `autofocus` on its main action, so focus moves into the card as it opens and a keyboard can go on with <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
- Every step can be left: a Skip or Done action, <kbd>Esc</kbd>, and for a single coachmark a click elsewhere.
- Say nothing in a tour that cannot be found without it: a reader who skips it must not miss anything.
- Placed right after its beacon in the markup, the coachmark is next in the tab order after it, wherever the card is drawn.

## Browser support \{#browser-support\}

The coachmark is placed with CSS anchor positioning: `position-area` and `position-try-fallbacks` for the card, and `anchor()` between the card and the element it points at for the arrow. In a browser without anchor positioning it still opens and closes, centered in the viewport where the browser places any popover, and has no arrow. The `source` option of `showPopover()` gives a tour's step its anchor; without it, a step opens without one. The fade and the short slide use `@starting-style`; without it the card appears and leaves at once.

The card enters with a fade and slides 4px toward its anchor, and leaves with a shorter fade. It does not scale as other popovers do: a scaled card would hold its arrow inside it, out of reach of the element the arrow points at.
