A gradient editor makes a gradient: its stops on a bar, the stop in hand in a [color picker](/components/color-picker), and its type, angle and centre. Every control in it is the library's own: the color picker, a [slider](/components/slider) and a number for a stop's place, a [segmented](/components/segmented) control for the type, a [dial](/components/dial) for the angle, numbers for the centre. What the gradient adds is the bar of stops, the preview and the [`gradient` behavior](/guide/behaviors) that keeps them all in step.

## The value \{#the-value\}

The gradient is a value of four parts:

| Part | Is |
| --- | --- |
| `type` | `linear`, `radial` or `conic` |
| `angle` | Degrees, for a linear and a conic gradient |
| `center` | Two percentages, for a radial and a conic gradient |
| `stops` | Each with an `id` that stays with it, an `offset` from 0 to 100 and a `color` |

A color is written as the color picker writes it, as sRGB in hex with its opacity. `gradientCss(value)` writes the gradient as CSS, and both the preview and the copied text come from it: interpolated in OKLab where the browser can be told to, after a fallback for where it cannot.

```css name=copied.css
background-image: linear-gradient(90deg, #ff6a00 0%, #ee0979 100%);
background-image: linear-gradient(in oklab 90deg, #ff6a00 0%, #ee0979 100%);
```

No CSS is read back: the editor holds the value, and an application stores the value, not the text.

## The stops \{#the-stops\}

- **A stop's handle is a slider** of its offset, a disc of its color on the bar. A press takes it in hand and a drag moves it; the arrow keys move it by one, by ten with <kbd class="kbd">Shift</kbd>, and <kbd class="kbd">Home</kbd> and <kbd class="kbd">End</kbd> take it to the ends. The stop in hand is `aria-current="true"`; it stands a little forward with an ink ring.
- **Stops may cross.** A stop moved past another keeps its id and its handle keeps focus; the gradient paints them in the order of their offsets. Two stops at one offset make a hard edge, and keep their order.
- **Adding** puts a stop of the color in hand ten points after it, at 100 at most. **Removing**, with the button or <kbd class="kbd">Delete</kbd>, hands focus to the next stop on the bar, or the one before. Two stops are the least: at two, removing is disabled.
- **Reversing** turns the stops round: each offset becomes 100 less itself.

Dragging is never the only way: the offset has a range and a number beside it, and every stop is reached with <kbd class="kbd">Tab</kbd>.

## States \{#states\}

`aria-readonly="true"` on the root shows a gradient that cannot be changed, and `aria-disabled="true"` one that is not in use: the handles stay where they are, and the controls are disabled.

## The behavior \{#behavior\}

```js name=app.js
import { gradient, gradientCss } from '@master/ui/behaviors/gradient.js'

const editor = gradient(document.querySelector('.gradient'), {
  value: { type: 'linear', angle: 90, center: [50, 50], stops: [
    { id: 'from', offset: 0, color: '#ff6a00' },
    { id: 'to', offset: 100, color: '#ee0979' },
  ] },
})
editor.value                     // the value as above
gradientCss(editor.value).text   // the two lines to copy
```

The reader's moves dispatch `input` while they go on and `change` when they end; setting `value` shows another gradient and dispatches nothing. The behavior writes the handles from the value, so a framework that writes them itself gives `draw`. It publishes the gradient as `--gradient` and `--gradient-fallback` on the root, and each stop as `--gradient-offset` and `--gradient-color` on its handle: what the editor paints is its content, and the stylesheet places it.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Each handle is named with its place among the stops and says its offset and its color in `aria-valuetext`.
- The preview is a picture of the value: hide it from assistive technology, since the stops and the CSS say the same.
- In forced colors the gradient keeps its own colors, since they are its content, and the handles and the bar are outlined in system colors.
