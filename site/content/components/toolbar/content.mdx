A toolbar is a small raised sheet that holds a row of quiet controls. Its 4px inset makes the corners nest: the toolbar's corner is a small control's corner plus the inset, so small quiet [buttons](/components/button), [selects](/components/select) and [segmented controls](/components/segmented) sit in it concentrically. It is 40px tall around 32px controls.

## State in a toolbar \{#state-in-a-toolbar\}

A quiet button has no material until touched, so the toolbar's own sheet shows through. Under the pointer the button rises as a sheet of its own; pressed, or with `aria-pressed="true"`, it sinks into a well. The toolbar is where the [state order](/guide/theme#principles) is easiest to see: well, sheet, raised.

## One stop in the tab order \{#keys\}

A toolbar is one stop in the tab order: <kbd class="kbd">Tab</kbd> enters it on the control focused last and leaves it, and the arrow keys move between its controls. Give it `focusgroup="toolbar wrap"`, and where the browser has [focusgroup](https://open-ui.org/components/scoped-focusgroup.explainer/) it does that by itself. The `toolbar` [behavior](/guide/behaviors) does it elsewhere, keeping one control at `tabindex="0"`. With native focusgroup, the browser moves focus and the behavior still handles toggle buttons.

```js name=app.js
import { toolbar } from '@master/ui/behaviors/toolbar.js'

const detach = toolbar(document.querySelector('.toolbar'))
```

- **Moving.** <kbd class="kbd">ArrowRight</kbd> and <kbd class="kbd">ArrowLeft</kbd> move along the toolbar, wrapping at the ends and following the reading direction; <kbd class="kbd">Home</kbd> and <kbd class="kbd">End</kbd> go to the first and the last control. Disabled controls are skipped. A control with `aria-disabled="true"` stays reachable, as a toolbar's controls should, and its click is stopped.
- **Toggles.** A button with `aria-pressed` presses on a click: `false` turns to `true` and back. A button without it is an action and keeps no state.
- **Controls with keys of their own.** A [segmented control](/components/segmented) or another native radio group counts as one control, its checked radio: the arrows leave it, and <kbd class="kbd">Space</kbd> chooses the focused radio. A select and a text field keep their own arrows.

Without either, every control is a tab stop, which works and is only slower.

An inner toolbar handles its own toggle buttons and arrow keys. In the scripted fallback, arrows skip inert subtrees and controls disabled by a fieldset. Radio groups follow their native form owners, so two forms may each offer a group named `view`.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Give the container `role="toolbar"` and a label. Use it only for controls that act on something else, such as an editor or a canvas; navigation is a [navbar](/components/navbar).
- Name icon-only buttons with `aria-label`, and say toggles with `aria-pressed`, without changing their label as they press.
- Disable a control with `aria-disabled="true"` rather than `disabled`, so the arrow keys still reach it and it can say why it is unavailable.
