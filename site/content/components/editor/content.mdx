An editor is the frame of a rich text field: the well of an [input](/components/input), a [toolbar](/components/toolbar) laid flat in its top, and the document under it. The editing itself, the document model, the selection, the history and the keys, belongs to an engine such as Tiptap or ProseMirror, which makes `editor-document` editable and writes the editor's classes on the blocks it renders. [Rich text](/guide/rich-text) puts Tiptap in it.

Without an engine this preview's document is the browser's own `contenteditable`: it takes typing, but not structure, and its toolbar does nothing.

## The frame \{#frame\}

- **It is a field.** The well, its hover, its focus halo, its danger edge and its disabled ink are the input's, so a rich text field reads like the fields around it. The focus that raises the frame is the document's, so moving through the toolbar does not.
- **The toolbar is a band.** `editor-toolbar` on a toolbar lays it flat in the top of the well, one inset in, so its corner nests in the well's, with a hairline under it. It wraps on a narrow editor. The [toolbar behavior](/components/toolbar) gives it one tab stop and the arrow keys.
- **The document grows.** From `--editor-rows` lines when it is empty, six by default, to `--editor-max-rows`, eighteen, then it scrolls inside the frame.

## What the document holds \{#content\}

The stylesheet never selects an element: the engine writes a class on each block, and only these are styled, inside the editor and nowhere else. It is not a typography for a page.

## States \{#states\}

Each state is an attribute the engine keeps on its element, or one the page sets on the frame: focus and `aria-invalid="true"` raise the frame or give it the danger edge, `aria-readonly="true"` lays it flat with no caret, and `aria-disabled="true"` on the frame drops it to a flat well with disabled ink. An invalid document shows the field's `field-error`. Disable the toolbar's controls with the editor, and tell the engine it is not editable.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The document is a `role="textbox"` with `aria-multiline="true"`, named by the field's label with `aria-labelledby` and described by its hint and error with `aria-describedby`. A `<label for>` cannot name an element that is not a form control.
- The toolbar is one tab stop, named, with `aria-controls` pointing at the document. Name every icon button, mark a toggle with `aria-pressed`, and give `aria-keyshortcuts` the shortcut the engine handles.
- After a toolbar command, focus goes back to the document, where the reader was writing.
- Do not add key handlers of your own to the document: an input method composing a word uses Enter and the arrows, and the engine knows when it does.
- In forced colors the frame is outlined in the field's color, in the highlight while it has focus.
