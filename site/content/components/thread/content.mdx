A thread is comments and their replies: the discussion under a document, a review or an issue. A comment is an [avatar](/components/avatar) on a column and its words beside it. Replies stand under the words, and a hairline runs down from the comment's avatar and turns into each of them, so a reader follows a line, not an indent, to see what answers what.

## Structure \{#structure\}

A `thread` is a list, and each `thread-item` is one comment with three children, in this order:

1. `thread-avatar`, around an avatar. It is the column the line runs down.
2. `thread-body`, the words: a `thread-header` with the `thread-author` and a `thread-meta` beside the name, then one `thread-text` for each paragraph. Both keep no margin of their own, whatever the page gives a paragraph. Anything else may follow: a [quote](/components/quote), a row of quiet buttons.
3. `thread-replies`, when there are any: a list of `thread-item`s that answer this one.

The line appears by itself when replies follow a comment, and each reply is joined to it by an elbow that stops short of its avatar. The pieces of the line never overlap, because its ink is translucent and two layers would show as a darker stretch.

## Nesting \{#nesting\}

A reply can have replies: put a `thread-replies` inside it and the line continues one level in. A comment's avatar is 32px, the default [avatar](/components/avatar); a reply's is 24px, `avatar-sm`, at every depth.

Two or three levels are enough. Each level costs 36px of width, and a deep thread on a phone leaves no room for its words: past that, start the reply with the name of the person it answers and keep it on the same level.

## Thread or message \{#thread-or-message\}

A thread is a discussion about something: comments are equals and any of them can be answered. A [message](/components/message) is a turn of a conversation between two sides, as a bubble that points at its author. Events that nobody answers are a [timeline](/components/timeline).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Write the thread as an `<ol>` and the replies as nested `<ol>`s. The nesting is the relation: a screen reader announces the level and the number of replies, which is what the line shows the eye.
- Give each time a `<time>` with a `datetime`, since "2h" is short for something exact.
- The line is drawn by the stylesheet and is not announced. In forced colors it takes the text color.
- An avatar that shows initials or a photo beside the author's name is decoration: the name is already there in words.
