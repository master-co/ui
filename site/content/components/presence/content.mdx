Presence shows where the other people working on the same thing are: their pointers on a canvas or a board, their carets and selections in text. Each person is drawn in one identity color, with their name beside it, so a reader can tell who is where at a glance. Add `presence` and the person's [identity](#identity), `presence-1` to `presence-8`, to an element that holds a `presence-name`, inside the positioned element the pointers move over, and set where it stands with `--presence-x` and `--presence-y`.

## Where a pointer stands \{#where-a-pointer-stands\}

A pointer's tip is the point. `--presence-x` and `--presence-y` are its distance, in pixels, from the top-left corner of the nearest positioned element, usually the canvas or the board, which takes `relative` and `overflow-hidden` so a pointer near its edge is cut there. The application knows where each person is and when they leave: it adds a `presence` when someone arrives, sets the two properties as their pointer moves, and removes it when they go. The stylesheet does the drawing and the motion; there is no script of its own.

```js name=app.js
function move(person, x, y) {
  const pointer = board.querySelector(`[data-person="${person.id}"]`)
  pointer.style.setProperty('--presence-x', `${x}px`)
  pointer.style.setProperty('--presence-y', `${y}px`)
}
```

- **Motion.** A pointer glides to each new point in a straight line for 160ms, which turns the few positions a second a connection delivers into steady movement. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` it jumps.
- **Out of the way.** A pointer takes no pointer event, so it never stands between the reader's own pointer and what is under it.
- **Coordinates.** Send them in the canvas's own units and convert them to the reader's view before setting the properties, so two people with different window sizes and zoom levels point at the same thing.

## A message \{#message\}

A `presence-message` under the name holds a few words the person is writing, as cursor chat does: a question about the thing they are pointing at. It wraps at 15rem. Remove it a few seconds after they stop writing.

## Identity colors \{#identity\}

Hue in Master UI says a status, and here, a person. The theme has eight identity colors, `--color-identity-1` to `--color-identity-8`, all at one lightness: a person keeps one of them for the session, and their name always stands beside it, so the color is never the only way to tell people apart. `--color-identity-ink` is the text on every one of them, at a contrast of at least 4.8:1 in the light scheme and 9:1 in the dark one.

Choose one with a modifier, `presence-1` to `presence-8`, on a pointer, a caret, a selection or a name that stands alone. A product with colors of its own for people sets `--presence-color` instead; give it one of a lightness that `--color-identity-ink` reads on, or set that token too.

Hand the colors out in this order as people arrive, and give the color of someone who left to the next to come. Choose from a stable number, such as a hash of the person's id, when every reader must see the same person in the same color. With more than eight people, colors repeat; the names still tell them apart.

## In text \{#in-text\}

In a document edited together, another person's caret is a `presence-caret` between two letters: a 2px bar in their color, as tall as the line, with their name in a flag that stands on it. It takes no room, so the words around it do not move as it does. Their selection is a `presence-selection` around the words they selected: a tint of their color behind the words, carried across lines.

The editor puts these marks where its collaboration layer says the other people are, as decorations it renders and removes; they are not part of the document. Leave room above the first line for a name flag, or let the flag of a caret on the first line run into the padding of what holds the text.

## When to use something else \{#when-to-use-something-else\}

- Who is here, rather than where, is a pile of [avatars](/components/avatar) in the header.
- The mark at the end of text the application is still writing is a [caret](/components/caret).
- Words highlighted for the reader, such as a search match, are a [mark](/components/mark).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Pointers and carets are decoration: give each `aria-hidden="true"`, so their names are not read in the middle of a canvas or a sentence. Say who is here in words elsewhere, such as the avatars in the header with their names, and announce arrivals and departures in a polite live region if they matter to the task.
- A selection is not hidden: the words in it are the document's. The tint does not change the words' color, so they keep their contrast.
- A color is never the only clue: the name stands beside every pointer and caret.
- In forced colors the arrows and the caret are drawn in system colors, and the flags and bubbles get an outline.
