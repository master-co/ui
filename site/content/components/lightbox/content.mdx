A lightbox is a picture opened large over the page. Use `.lightbox` on a native `<dialog>`: the thumbnail's own button opens it with `commandfor` and `command="show-modal"`, so the browser supplies the top layer, the focus trap and <kbd>Esc</kbd>, and no script is involved. The dialog has no sheet of its own. It is exactly as large as the picture, which stands on a page dimmed and blurred well out of focus.

## Opening and closing \{#opening-and-closing\}

- `commandfor="id"` with `command="show-modal"` on the thumbnail's button opens the lightbox. `command="close"` on the close button closes it and returns focus to the thumbnail.
- `closedby="any"` adds light dismiss. Because the dialog is exactly as large as the picture, a click anywhere else is a click outside it, and closes it. A browser that does not know the attribute keeps <kbd>Esc</kbd> and the close button.
- In a browser without invoker commands, call `dialog.showModal()` and `dialog.close()` from a click handler. The stylesheet is the same.

The picture fades in from a slightly smaller size and leaves faster than it arrived, through `@starting-style` and discrete transitions; without them it appears and leaves at once.

## Size \{#size\}

`lightbox-media` goes on an `<img>`, a `<video>` or an `<iframe>`. Its size does not come from the file. It is 64rem wide, or as wide as the viewport's width or height allows with a 24px gutter on every side, at the proportions set in `--lightbox-ratio`: `3 / 2` unless you say otherwise. So an image, a video and a frame are sized the same way, and nothing moves when one loads.

Set `--lightbox-ratio` on the dialog to the picture's own proportions, as the example does with `16 / 9`; a picture of other proportions is cropped to fit. `--lightbox-width` sets the widest it gets, and `--lightbox-gutter` the room kept at the edges.

## Caption and close \{#caption-and-close\}

`lightbox-caption` is a floating bar under the picture, as wide as it is; the picture leaves room for it. Keep it to a line or two. `lightbox-close` goes on a small square [button](/components/button) and rests it 8px inside the picture's top corner, where it is found on any picture because it is a raised control with its own edge.

## Lightbox or dialog \{#lightbox-or-dialog\}

A lightbox holds one picture and nothing to decide. An overlay with a title, text or a form is a [dialog](/components/dialog). A picture that stays in the page with its caption is a [figure](/components/figure).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Name the dialog with `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby` pointing at its caption.
- Give the large picture a real `alt`. The thumbnail inside the button may then have an empty one, when the button's own words say what opens.
- Always include a close button with an accessible name: light dismiss and <kbd>Esc</kbd> are not found by everyone.
- Focus moves into the lightbox when it opens and returns to the thumbnail when it closes. With reduced motion the picture fades without changing size.
