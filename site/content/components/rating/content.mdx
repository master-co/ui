A rating is a score out of a few stars. It is a group of native radios, each drawn as a star in ink, so the arrow keys, the form value and assistive technology work as they do for any radio group.

## How the stars fill \{#how-the-stars-fill\}

The radios are in reading order, one to five. `:has()` fills every star that has the checked one after it, and the checked one itself: `.rating-input:has(~ .rating-input:checked)`. The same selector with `:hover` previews the score under the pointer. Before `:has()`, this needed the stars in reverse order and a reversed row, which broke the keys.

## Sizes \{#sizes\}

Stars are 20px. `rating-sm` is 16px, for a row in a list, and `rating-lg` is 24px. On a touch screen a star that can be chosen stands in a 44px target of its own: the stars part instead of swelling, and each keeps its size. Stars that only show a score keep their spacing.

## Half stars \{#half-stars\}

`rating-half` gives each star two radios, so the score moves by halves. Each radio is half a star wide and shows one half of a whole star standing on the seam between the pair, so the fill and the preview are the ones above and a score of 3.5 fills seven halves. The arrow keys move by a half, and each radio is named with its score.

## Showing a score \{#read-only\}

A score that is not a choice, such as an average of 4.3, is `rating-display`: one empty element with `role="img"`, named with the score in words, and `--rating-value` set to the number. The stars fill from the start to the score, the last one in part. `--rating-max` sets how many stars it draws, five by default. Sizes work as they do for the radios.

<ComponentPreview name="display" height={96} title="Showing a score" background="plain" html={`<div class="flex items-center gap-xs text-sm fg-text-muted">
  <span class="rating rating-sm rating-display" role="img" aria-label="Rated 4.3 out of 5" style="--rating-value: 4.3"></span>
  4.3 from 128 reviews
</div>`} />

The fill reads whole stars, half a gap and the fraction of the last star with `round()`. Where it is missing, the fill runs the score's share of the whole row and may stand a pixel off.

To show a whole-star score with the radios a reader would use, disable them: the stars keep their ink and stop answering the pointer. Say the score in words beside them either way, since a row of stars is not a number.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Name the group with `aria-label` or a `<legend>`, and each radio with its score, halves too: "3.5 stars".
- Name `rating-display` with the score in words, "Rated 4.3 out of 5". The stars are a picture of the number; `role="img"` keeps assistive technology from looking for parts in it.
- A star is cut with a mask, which would also cut its own focus ring, so the group carries the ring while one of its radios has keyboard focus.
- Stars are ink, not yellow: the interface keeps hue for status.
