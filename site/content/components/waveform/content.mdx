A waveform is a recording drawn as a row of bars: the shape of a voice message or of a short clip. Each bar is as tall as the sound is loud at that moment, and the bars fill with ink as far as the recording has played; what is still to come is a quiet tint of the same ink. `waveform` is the row: a plain element holding one empty `waveform-bar` for each slice of the sound and, to seek, an invisible native range input over the bars.

The waveform takes the ink of the text it stands in. In a received [message](/components/message) that is the strong ink on a raised sheet; in a sent one it is the inverse ink on ink, and the same bars read without a variant. The play button is the opposite material of its bubble: ink on the raised sheet, a raised sheet on the ink.

## Bars \{#bars\}

Write one `waveform-bar` for each slice of the recording, with its loudness as `--level`, from 0 to 1. A bar is that share of the row's height, centered on the middle line, and never shorter than it is wide, so a pause is a row of dots. Its height is rounded to whole steps of 2px with `round()`, which keeps the round of both ends on the pixel grid; a browser without `round()` gives the bar its exact height, and an end may lose part of its round.

Bars are 3px wide and 2px apart. In a row of other things, such as a bubble with its button and its time, the waveform is as wide as its bars need: 30 bars are 148px. Given more room, it fills it and spreads the bars with even gaps; given less, the bars grow thinner together. It never asks for less than its bars need, so in a box that sizes itself to its content, give the box a width or a maximum, as a bubble has. A recording of any length is drawn with about as many bars as fit; take the peaks of the audio in that many slices, when it is recorded or uploaded, and store them with it.

## How far it has played \{#progress\}

`--waveform-progress` on the row says how far the recording has played, from 0 to 1. The ink is measured across the whole row, not counted in bars, so it stops exactly at the playhead, even halfway across a bar, and moves as smoothly as the number does. Each bar is cut to its pill shape with a `clip-path`, which clips what the bar holds; the ink inside it is positioned against the row and runs from the row's start to the playhead. Every engine draws it, with no count of the bars and no second copy of them.

To seek, put a `waveform-seek` range input last in the row. It lies over the bars, invisible, with a thumb one pixel wide, so the value under the pointer is the bar under it. The arrow keys step it, and the row draws its focus ring. Under a pointer that hovers, the bars still to play step up a little, to say they can be touched.

The script that plays the recording owns the number. With an `<audio>` element, it sets the property and the input's value on `timeupdate`, and sets the audio's time when the input moves:

```js
audio.addEventListener('timeupdate', () => {
  seek.value = audio.currentTime
  waveform.style.setProperty('--waveform-progress', audio.currentTime / audio.duration)
})
seek.addEventListener('input', () => { audio.currentTime = seek.value })
```

`timeupdate` fires about four times a second. For a playhead that glides, read `audio.currentTime` in a `requestAnimationFrame` loop while it plays, as the preview's timer does.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The bars say nothing to assistive technology: they are empty elements. The meaning is carried by the play button, the seek input and the time.
- Give the play button an `aria-label`, "Play" or "Pause" as it is, and the seek input an `aria-label` and an `aria-valuetext` in words, such as "0:05 of 0:14", kept up to date with its value.
- Without a seek input, say how long the recording is in text, with `waveform-time` or in the message itself.
- In forced colors the bars are drawn in the system's text color as far as they have played and in its gray text after.
