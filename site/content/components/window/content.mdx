A window is the frame of an application drawn around something you want to show: a screenshot, a short demo, a few lines of a terminal. It is a lifted sheet with a bar across its top. The three marks in the bar are drawn in the ink of a line, with no hue, so the frame says "this is a window" and leaves the color to what it holds.

## The bar \{#the-bar\}

`window-bar` has three places. `window-controls` stands at the start: one empty element, drawn as three discs. In the middle goes a `window-title`, or a `window-address`, a small well like a browser's. `window-end` holds anything that belongs at the far end. Each part is optional, and the middle stays centered whatever stands beside it.

## The body \{#the-body\}

`window-body` has no padding, so a picture or a video meets the frame, and the window's own corner cuts it; give text its room with a padding utility. Put the class on the element that is the content: an `<img>`, a `<video>`, a `<pre>` or a `<div>`.

`window-flat` keeps one contour and drops the shadow, for a window on a tray or among other things on a dense page.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The frame is decoration. Hide `window-controls` with `aria-hidden="true"`: the discs are not buttons and do nothing.
- A `<figure>` suits a window that illustrates the text around it; add a `<figcaption>` after it when the picture needs explaining.
- Describe a screenshot in its `alt`. The title in the bar is part of the picture, not a replacement for that description.
- Do not build a working dialog from this. A window that opens over the page is a [dialog](/components/dialog).
