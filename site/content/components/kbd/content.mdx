## Raised \{#raised\}

`kbd-raised` catches the light like a physical key. Use it when the shortcut itself is the lesson.

## Together, or one after another \{#combinations\}

A shortcut is one of two gestures, and the keycaps alone do not say which. Keys held together stand side by side with nothing between them, and an outer `<kbd>` makes the combination one input. Keys pressed in turn are a sequence: write "then" between them. A plus sign or an arrow is one more glyph for a reader to decode, and it is read aloud as a symbol; a word is read as a word.

</dl>`} />

A modifier's glyph is not known to everyone. Where a shortcut is being taught, not just recalled, write the key's name on the keycap: `Shift`, not `⇧`.
