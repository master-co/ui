A calendar is a month of days to pick from: one day, a [range](#range) or [several days](#several-days), one month or [two side by side](#several-months). It is an ARIA grid of buttons: the stylesheet draws every state, and the `calendar` [behavior](/guide/behaviors) supplies the dates, the month buttons and the arrow keys. It has no surface of its own, so it sits in a [card](/components/card), or in a [popover](/components/popover) to make a date picker.

The markup is the header and an empty grid. The behavior writes the month's name into `calendar-title`, and the weekday names and the weeks into `calendar-grid`; it opens on the month of today. Without the script the grid stays as the markup has it, so a calendar that must read before the script runs is rendered on the server with its weeks, as the [range](#range) below is written, and the behavior replaces them when it attaches.

## Months and years \{#months-and-years\}

The title of the calendar above is a button, `calendar-title-button`, inside `calendar-title`. Clicking it turns the grid into the twelve months of the year, and clicking it again into the years of the decade, with the year before and the year after it in muted ink. Choosing a year shows its months, and choosing a month shows its days, with focus on the same day. <kbd>Escape</kbd> goes back to the days as they were.

The months and the years are `calendar-period` buttons in the same grid, three to a row and drawn in the day's material. The grid keeps the height of a six-week month, so the calendar does not jump. Throughout, the title says where the grid is: `November 2026`, `2026`, `2020 – 2029`. The month buttons turn a year in the months and a decade in the years, and take names for it from the `labels` option. In the years the title button is disabled, since the decade is as far as it goes.

A title without the button stays a plain title, and the calendar never leaves its days. The jump is for a calendar of one month.

Native month and year selects in the header were the other way to do this. They were not chosen: two selects and two month buttons do not fit over seven 36px columns in every language, a list of a hundred years is a long scroll in a select, and a styled select's list is not yet in every browser. The grid of months and years is in the calendar's own material and works with the keys the reader is already using.

## States \{#states\}

Every state is an attribute or a class on the day's button. The behavior sets them; markup written by hand or on a server sets the same ones.

| State | Mark it with | Drawn as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Today | `aria-current="date"` | A dot under the number |
| Chosen | `aria-selected="true"` | Ink, the way a checked [checkbox](/components/checkbox) fills |
| Outside the month | `calendar-day-outside` | Muted ink |
| Unavailable | `disabled` | Disabled ink |

A month or a year in the jump carries the same marks: `aria-current="date"` on the month or the year of today, `aria-selected="true"` on the one that holds a chosen day, and `disabled` on one with no day between `min` and `max`. `calendar-period-outside` marks the year before and after a decade.

A day answers the pointer at once, like a row in a [menu](/components/menu). Today keeps its dot when it is chosen, in the ink of the day.

A range is two ink days joined by a chip-toned band. The first day has `calendar-day-range-start`, the last `calendar-day-range-end` and the days between `calendar-day-range`. All of them are `aria-selected="true"`.

- Columns have no gap, so the band runs from cell to cell. Rows keep 2px apart, so each week is its own band.

## The behavior \{#behavior\}

`calendar` takes the root and an object of options, and returns the function that detaches it.

```js name=app.js
import { calendar } from '@master/ui/behaviors/calendar.js'

const root = document.querySelector('.calendar')
const picker = calendar(root, { value: '2026-11-04', min: '2026-11-01', max: '2026-12-31' })

root.addEventListener('change', (event) => console.log(event.detail.value))
```

| Option | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| `value` | The chosen day as `YYYY-MM-DD`, the two ends of a range, or the days chosen with `multiple` |
| `month` | The month to show first, as `YYYY-MM`. By default the month of the value, or of today |
| `min`, `max` | The first and the last day that can be chosen, as `YYYY-MM-DD`. A month button is disabled when there is no month to go to |
| `range` | `true` to choose two days and every day between them |
| `multiple` | `true` to choose any number of separate days; `range` wins over it |
| `labels` | The names of the month buttons in the months and the years: `previousYear`, `nextYear`, `previousDecade`, `nextDecade`. English by default |
| `weekStart` | The day in the first column, 0 for Sunday to 6 for Saturday. By default the locale's own, and Monday where the browser cannot say |
| `locale` | A BCP 47 tag for the names of months and days. By default the `lang` the calendar stands in |
| `disabled` | A function that takes a `Date` and says whether that day cannot be chosen |
| `draw` | A function that writes the grids instead of the script. It is called with the month to show, as `YYYY-MM`, and the view: `days`, `months` or `years`. In the days it writes that month and, in the next grids, the months after it; `calendarMonth(month, { offset })` gives each. In the months or the years it writes the cells `calendarPeriods(month, { view })` gives. See [a grid of your own](/guide/behaviors#a-grid-of-your-own) |

- **What it writes.** The weekday names and the weeks of each month shown, and each month's name; in the jump, the months or the years and the title for them. On the days: the date as the button's `value`, `aria-current="date"` on today, `aria-selected="true"` and the range classes on what is chosen, `disabled` on a day that cannot be chosen, a full date as `aria-label`, and `tabindex` so one day is in the tab order. With `draw`, a template or a framework writes the days and the script only marks them.
- **The events.** Choosing a day dispatches a bubbling `input`, then `change`, from the root, each with `detail.value`: the day as `YYYY-MM-DD`, `[start, end]` once both ends of a range are chosen, or the sorted list of days with `multiple`. The first end of a range dispatches `input` alone, with `[start, null]`: the value has moved, but it is not complete until the second end. A jump to the months or the years dispatches nothing.
- **The value.** It lives in the script, not in the markup. Read it from the returned function's `value`: `picker.value`. In a range the end is `null` until it is chosen; with `multiple` nothing chosen is `[]`. Set it to choose from outside: `picker.value = '2026-11-20'` shows the month of the new day and dispatches nothing.
- **Days.** They are local and Gregorian.

## Structure \{#structure\}

- `calendar-grid` declares seven columns once. Each `calendar-week` is a row on a subgrid of them, so the weekday names and the days share the same columns without a table.
- `calendar-weekday` is a column heading. Its `aria-label` is the full name.
- `calendar-day` is a `<button>` with `role="gridcell"`.
- `calendar-title-button`, when the title has one, is a `<button>` inside `calendar-title`; the behavior writes the title into it.
- `calendar-period` is a month or a year in the jump: a `<button>` with `role="gridcell"` whose value is `YYYY-MM` or `YYYY`, three to a `calendar-week`.
- `calendar-month` holds the header and the grid of one of several months.

## Keyboard \{#keyboard\}

The behavior gives the grid the keys of a date grid. A move past the edge of the month shows the neighboring month and keeps focus on the new day. A move never lands on a day that cannot be chosen, and stops at `min` and `max`. In the months and the years the arrow keys move by one and by a row of three, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> chooses and goes back down.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The grid is `role="grid"`, labelled by the month's title; each week is `role="row"`.
- One day is in the tab order: the chosen one (the first of several), or today, or the first day of the months shown when they hold neither. The rest have `tabindex="-1"`. Several months side by side are one tab stop together, each grid labelled by its own title.
- A grid in which a range or several days can be chosen has `aria-multiselectable="true"`.
- The title button is named by the title it shows, which is also the grid's label. Say what it does with `aria-description`, such as "Choose a month or a year", rather than `aria-label`, which would rename the grid with it.
- Give the title `aria-live="polite"`, so a change of month is announced.
- A day keeps its 36px on a touch screen: the grid stays the shape of a month, and a day is still more than a finger needs.
- Say near the calendar what its days mean: why days are unavailable, such as "Closed on weekends", and in a range what each end is, such as check-in and check-out. A disabled day or an ink end says neither.

## React, Svelte and Vue \{#frameworks\}

The `calendar` component draws the month from the same data as the behavior, so it is in the first HTML, and passes the chosen day in and out. With `range` the value comes out twice: as `[start, null]` when the first end is chosen, and as `[start, end]` when the second is. It takes `range` and `multiple`, `months` for several months side by side, and has the title button when it shows one month; `jumpLabel` is the button's description and `labels` the names of the month buttons in the months and the years. Copy it into the project with `npx master-ui add calendar`; [React, Svelte and Vue](/guide/frameworks) says how the components are built and used.
