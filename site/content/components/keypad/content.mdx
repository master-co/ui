A keypad is the keys of a phone, for a code or an amount typed with a thumb: twelve buttons in three columns. A key is a raised face that sinks at once under a press, with a large figure and, under it, the small letters a phone prints there. It types nothing by itself. Each key is a button, and the page puts its figure where it belongs.

## A key \{#a-key\}

`keypad-key` is a button 52px tall. It has the raised face of a [button](/components/button) and none of its travel: under a press it is in the well at once, with no transition, because a thumb entering six figures moves faster than one. Under a pointer it takes the firmer contour of a hovered control.

`keypad-letters` is the line under a figure. It is there because people remember codes as words; leave it out for an amount.

`keypad-key-quiet` is a key that is not a figure: delete, a decimal point, a sign. It has no face until it is pressed, so the ten figures read as the pad and the others as its margin. Where a corner of the pad has no key, put an empty `<span>` there to keep the grid.

## The pad \{#the-pad\}

The pad is three equal columns 8px apart, as wide as its container up to 17rem. Two things it does are easy to forget when a pad is built from plain buttons:

- It sets `touch-action: manipulation`, so the browser does not wait to see whether a second tap is a double-tap zoom. Two quick presses of the same key are two presses.
- It does not select its figures, so a press that slides a little never highlights text.

`--keypad-key` sets the height of a key and `--keypad-gap` the space between keys.

## What the page does \{#script\}

The keypad has no behavior of its own, since what a key means belongs to the page: a figure appended to an amount, a slot of an [OTP field](/components/otp) filled, a call placed. One listener on the pad is enough, as in the example: read the pressed button's `value` and write it where it goes.

Keep a real field on the page. A reader with a hardware keyboard types into it, a password manager fills it, and a screen reader hears its value change. `inputmode="none"` on that field keeps the system's own keyboard from opening over the pad on a phone.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Give the pad `role="group"` and an `aria-label`. Each key is a button whose name is its figure; a key that shows only an icon needs an `aria-label`.
- The letters under a figure are part of the button's text. If "2 abc" is noise for your readers, wrap the letters in an element with `aria-hidden="true"`.
- Announce the result, not each press: the field that receives the figures is what a screen reader should follow.
- Keys are 52px tall, above the 44px a touch target needs, at every pointer.
