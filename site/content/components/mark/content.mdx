A mark is a stroke of highlighter over the words that matter: the part of a result that matches a search, the phrase a reviewer points at. It is a wash of ink, not a hue, so it reads on every surface and never competes with a status color. Use `.mark` on a native `<mark>`.

In a [listbox](/components/listbox), a [combobox](/components/combobox) or a [command palette](/components/command), wrap the matched part of each option in a mark as the reader types.

## The current match \{#current\}

When a reader steps through matches, as find-in-page does, set `aria-current="true"` on the one they are at. It fills with ink.

## Brand and underline \{#brand-and-underline\}

`mark-brand` is a wash of the [brand color](/guide/theme#status-and-brand), for the one phrase a page is about. `mark-underline` draws the stroke along the foot of the words instead of over them; it is a thick underline, which the browser paints behind the letters and carries across line breaks.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- `<mark>` means "relevant here", not "important": for importance use `<strong>`. Most screen readers do not announce a mark, so a highlight must not be the only way a result explains itself.
- A mark keeps the color of the text it is on, and its wash is ink at about a tenth, so contrast does not change. In forced colors it uses the system's `Mark` and `MarkText`.
- A mark that wraps keeps its padding and corner on every line.
