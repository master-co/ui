A pile shows several things as one: notifications of one kind, files sent together, drafts of one document. It holds one surface, the top one, and draws two more behind it, each a step narrower, a step lower and a step fainter. They read as sheets lying under the first, not as a shadow.

## What it draws \{#what-it-draws\}

The pile is a holder: put one surface in it, a [card](/components/card), a [sheet](/components/surface), a [file](/components/file). The two sheets under it are drawn by the stylesheet, 8px in on both sides and 6px lower than the one above, and the pile keeps 12px under the top surface for them, so nothing after it has to make room.

The sheets take the corner in `--pile-radius`, a surface's corner. When the top surface has another corner, set the property to it, as the file below does. `--pile-step` and `--pile-inset` change how far each sheet shows.

## Say how many \{#say-how-many\}

The sheets under the top one are decoration. They are always two, whether the pile stands for three things or thirty, and they are not announced. Say how many there are in words on the top surface, and give the reader a way to see them all: a button that opens the group, or a link to the list.

## Pile, list or avatar group \{#pile-or\}

A pile is for members that need not all be seen. When every member matters, show them in a [list](/components/list). People are an [avatar group](/components/avatar), which overlaps their faces. Slides to step through are a [carousel](/components/carousel).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The pile adds no role and no name. The surface it holds carries the meaning.
- State the count in text, since the drawing does not.
- In forced colors the two sheets take a system outline and are fully opaque.
