A scale is one answer on a row of numbers, from one end to the other: how likely, how satisfied, how hard. Each number is a `<label>` around a native radio, so the group keeps the arrow keys and a form keeps the value. At rest a cell is a flat face on one contour; the chosen one is a mark of ink.

## The row and its ends \{#the-row-and-its-ends\}

The cells share the row's width, 4px apart, so five numbers and eleven both fill the column they are given and stay level with the fields around them: a cell is 38px tall, as a [button](/components/button) is. A cell is never narrower than 28px; in a row too narrow for them all, the cells wrap onto a second line.

A number alone says nothing. `scale-ends` is a line under the row with two names, one for each end. Put it inside the scale after the last cell. The question itself belongs in a [fieldset](/components/input)'s legend, as above, or in an `aria-label` on a scale with `role="radiogroup"`.

## Scale, segmented, rating or slider \{#scale-or\}

A scale is a degree with no names in the middle. Two to five named options that switch a view are a [segmented control](/components/segmented). A score out of a few stars is a [rating](/components/rating). An approximate value on a long range is a [slider](/components/slider). A vote that shows how everyone answered is a [poll](/components/poll).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The radios share a `name`. The browser makes them one tab stop and moves between them with the arrow keys.
- The number in the cell is the radio's label. The ends are not attached to any one cell, so say what the numbers mean in the question: "from 0, not at all likely, to 10, extremely likely".
- The focus ring is drawn around the cell. On a touch screen a cell is 44px tall; eleven cells need about 320px of width to stay comfortable to press.
- A disabled scale keeps its chosen cell visible in a quieter tone, so an answer already given can still be read.
