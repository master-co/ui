A figure is an image, a video or a drawing with its caption. The media takes a surface's corner and a hairline ring just inside its own edge, so a pale image does not dissolve into the page and a dark one does not end in a hard cut. The caption sits close under it, quiet.

<ComponentPreview name="default" height={344} title="Figure" background="plain" bodyClass="p-lg" html={`<figure class="figure max-w(360) mx-auto">
  <img class="figure-media" src="/demo/moonstone.svg" alt="A pale stone with a band of light across it" style="--figure-ratio: 3 / 2">
  <figcaption class="figure-caption">Moonstone under a north light, the stone the light theme is named for.</figcaption>
</figure>`} />

Set `--figure-ratio` on the media to hold its shape before it loads; the media then covers that shape. Without it the media keeps its own proportions. Write `width` and `height` on the `<img>` either way, so the browser reserves the space.

## Framed \{#framed\}

`figure-framed` rests the media in a tray, 4px in, like a print in a mat, and the caption moves inside the frame. The tray's corner is the media's corner plus that inset, so the gap keeps one width around the bend.

<ComponentPreview name="framed" height={288} title="Framed" background="plain" bodyClass="p-lg" html={`<figure class="figure figure-framed max-w(360) mx-auto">
  <img class="figure-media" src="/demo/moonstone.svg" alt="A pale stone with a band of light across it" style="--figure-ratio: 16 / 9">
  <figcaption class="figure-caption">Figure 2. The same stone, framed.</figcaption>
</figure>`} />

## Centered \{#centered\}

`figure-center` centers and balances the caption, for a figure that stands alone in a column of text.

<ComponentPreview name="center" height={344} title="Centered" background="plain" bodyClass="p-lg" html={`<figure class="figure figure-center max-w(240) mx-auto">
  <img class="figure-media" src="/demo/portrait-2.svg" alt="Alan Turing" style="--figure-ratio: 1">
  <figcaption class="figure-caption">Alan Turing, drawn for the avatar examples.</figcaption>
</figure>`} />

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Use `<figure>` with a `<figcaption>`: the caption is then the figure's accessible name.
- The caption does not replace `alt`. The alternative text says what the image shows; the caption says why it is here. When the caption already says everything, an empty `alt=""` avoids reading it twice.
- The ring inside the media's edge is decoration and follows the color scheme; in forced colors a framed figure takes a system outline.
