Uptime shows how a service has been, one tick to a day: the row a status page is made of. It is a list whose items say in words what each day was like. The stylesheet draws each item as a thin upright tick and moves its words out of sight, where assistive technology still reads them.

## Quiet when it is good \{#quiet-when-it-is-good\}

A day that went well is a quiet tint of ink, not a green bar. Ninety green bars say nothing, ninety times; a calm row leaves the eye free for the days that did not go well. Those take a status hue and a class:

- `uptime-degraded`, in the warning hue: the service was slow or partly failing.
- `uptime-down`, in the danger hue: the service was down.
- `uptime-none`, in the tone of a hairline: there is no data for that day, such as the days before the service existed.

The hue is a status and never stands alone: the words in each item say what happened and for how long. Under the pointer a tick stands taller at once, so a reader can tell which day they are on before a tooltip opens.

## Size \{#size\}

Ticks share the row's width, 2px apart, and are never under 2px or over 8px wide, so thirty days and ninety days both fill a card without a width written anywhere. The row is 32px tall. `uptime-sm` is 16px with ticks 1px apart, for a line of a [table](/components/table) or a [list](/components/list).

## Uptime or heatmap \{#uptime-or-heatmap\}

A tick is a state: well, degraded, down, unknown. When each cell is a number on a scale, such as requests per day, use a [heatmap](/components/heatmap), whose cells are as dark as their value. The state of a service now is a [badge](/components/badge) with a dot.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Write it as an `<ol>`, oldest day first, and name it with `aria-label`: the service and the period.
- Each item's text is the day and what happened, in words: "24 October: down for 18 minutes". It is not shown, but it is what a screen reader reads, so do not leave an item empty.
- Give the summary beside the row as text too: the share of time the service was up, and its state now.
- In forced colors a good day is the text color, a day that was not is the system highlight, and a day with no data is the system's grey.
