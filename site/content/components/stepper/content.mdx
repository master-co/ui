A stepper is a small number a reader nudges more often than types: seats, a quantity, a font size. It is an [input group](/components/input#input-group) around a native `<input type="number">`, with a [button](/components/button) at each end of the well. Add `stepper` to the group and `stepper-field` to the field.

## What the field already does \{#what-the-field-already-does\}

The field is a number input, so the platform supplies most of a stepper before any script runs:

- <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> step the value by `step`, and stop at `min` and `max`.
- The field is announced as a spin button with its value and its limits.
- A phone shows its number keys.
- `:user-invalid` reports a typed value outside the range, and the well takes the danger contour.

The stylesheet removes the browser's own spin buttons, because the two buttons replace them.

## The behavior \{#behavior\}

The `stepper` [behavior](/guide/behaviors) makes the two buttons step the number between them. It takes the root, the element with the `stepper` class, has no options, and returns the function that detaches it.

```js name=app.js
import { stepper } from '@master/ui/behaviors/stepper.js'

const root = document.querySelector('.stepper')
stepper(root)

root.addEventListener('change', (event) => console.log(event.target.value))
```

- **Stepping.** A button calls `stepDown()` or `stepUp()` on the field, the same methods the arrow keys use, so `min`, `max` and `step` are written once, on the field.
- **Limits.** A button is `disabled` while the value stands at the limit it steps toward; a disabled step lies flat in the well. An empty field has reached neither limit. Detaching gives the buttons back the `disabled` they had.
- **Holding.** A button held down steps again and again after a short delay, until the pointer lifts, leaves or is cancelled, or the limit is reached.
- **Direction.** Each button says which way it steps in its `value`, negative for down. Without one, the button before the field steps down and the button after it steps up.
- **Events.** The field dispatches `input`, then `change`, when a button changed the number, as it does when typed in. The value is the field's `value`.

The buttons carry `tabindex="-1"`: the field is the one stop in the tab order, and the keys already do what the buttons do. Without the behavior the field still does [what the platform supplies](#what-the-field-already-does), and the buttons do nothing.

## Width \{#width\}

The well is as wide as its figure. Where the browser has `field-sizing: content`, the field follows the digits typed, from three digits to nine; elsewhere it keeps the width of three. Set `--stepper-min` and `--stepper-max` for a figure of another length, or give the stepper a width and the field takes what the buttons leave.

A unit is an `input-group-addon` after the field, and a larger `step` moves a larger figure.

## Disabled \{#disabled\}

## When to use something else \{#when-to-use-something-else\}

- A number that is typed once and rarely adjusted, such as a port or a price, is a plain [input](/components/input) with `type="number"`.
- A position in a wide range, where the exact figure matters less than the feel, is a [slider](/components/slider).
- A choice among a few named amounts is a [segmented control](/components/segmented).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Label the field with a `<label>`, a [field](/components/input#field) or `aria-label`. Each button needs its own name that says what it changes: "More seats", not "Plus".
- Keep `min`, `max` and `step` on the field. They are what the keys, the buttons and assistive technology all read.
- The buttons stay out of the tab order. A reader on a keyboard steps with the arrow keys in the field; a reader with a pointer has targets of 30px, and a finger 36px inside a 44px well.
