A stepper is a small number a reader nudges more often than types: seats, a quantity, a font size. It is an [input group](/components/input#input-group) around a native `<input type="number">`, with a [button](/components/button) at each end of the well. Add `stepper` to the group and `stepper-field` to the field.

## What the field already does \{#what-the-field-already-does\}

The field is a number input, so the platform supplies most of a stepper before any script runs:

- <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> step the value by `step`, and stop at `min` and `max`.
- The field is announced as a spin button with its value and its limits.
- A phone shows its number keys.
- `:user-invalid` reports a typed value outside the range, and the well takes the danger contour.

The stylesheet removes the browser's own spin buttons, because the two buttons replace them.

## The behavior \{#behavior\}

The `stepper` [behavior](/guide/behaviors) makes the two buttons step the number between them. It takes the root, the element with the `stepper` class, and the options of a [formatted figure](#formatted) and a [scrub](#scrub), and returns the function that detaches it.

```js name=app.js
import { stepper } from '@master/ui/behaviors/stepper.js'

const root = document.querySelector('.stepper')
stepper(root)

root.addEventListener('change', (event) => console.log(event.target.value))
```

- **Stepping.** A button calls `stepDown()` or `stepUp()` on the field, the same methods the arrow keys use, so `min`, `max` and `step` are written once, on the field.
- **Limits.** A button is `disabled` while the value stands at the limit it steps toward; a disabled step lies flat in the well. An empty field has reached neither limit. Detaching gives the buttons back the `disabled` they had.
- **Holding.** A button held down steps again and again after a short delay, until the pointer lifts, leaves or is cancelled, or the limit is reached.
- **Direction.** Each button says which way it steps in its `value`, negative for down. Without one, the button before the field steps down and the button after it steps up.
- **Events.** The field dispatches `input`, then `change`, when a button changed the number, as it does when typed in. The value is the field's `value`.

The buttons carry `tabindex="-1"`: the field is the one stop in the tab order, and the keys already do what the buttons do. Without the behavior the field still does [what the platform supplies](#what-the-field-already-does), and the buttons do nothing.

## Width \{#width\}

The well is as wide as its figure. Where the browser has `field-sizing: content`, the field follows the digits typed, from three digits to nine; elsewhere it keeps the width of three. Set `--stepper-min` and `--stepper-max` for a figure of another length, or give the stepper a width and the field takes what the buttons leave.

A unit is an `input-group-addon` after the field, and a larger `step` moves a larger figure.

## A formatted figure \{#formatted\}

A price, a percentage or a large count reads better written out: `$1,250`, `12%`, `1,250,000`. Put the field and a `stepper-display` in a `stepper-value`, and give the behavior `format`, the options of [`Intl.NumberFormat`](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/Intl/NumberFormat/NumberFormat), and a `locale` if the page's is not the one to write in.

```js name=app.js
stepper(root, { format: { style: 'currency', currency: 'USD', maximumFractionDigits: 0 }, locale: 'en-US' })
```

`attach()` passes no options, so this preview calls `stepper` itself, after `attach()` has run: a second call on one root replaces the first. `MasterUI` is the bundle a preview loads; in a project, import `stepper` as [the behavior](#behavior) section shows.

- **The field keeps the number.** The display stands in the field's place while the field is not focused, and the field's own digits are clear. Focus the field and the display steps aside: the reader edits `1250`, not `$1,250`, and the arrow keys, `min`, `max`, `step`, validation and the value a form sends are the number input's as before.
- **The words.** The behavior writes the figure into the display as the value changes, from a button, a key, typing or a value set from outside, and into the field's `aria-valuetext`, so a screen reader says "$1,250" too. The display has `aria-hidden="true"`.
- **Without a format.** A stepper with a `stepper-display` and no `format` groups the digits as the locale does. `format` may also be a function of the number, for a figure `Intl.NumberFormat` does not write.
- **The width.** The figure's box is as wide as the wider of the field and the display, between `--stepper-min` and `--stepper-max`, so focusing the field does not move the buttons.
- **Without the script** the display is empty and the field shows its number as usual.

## Scrubbing \{#scrub\}

In a design tool a number is often changed by dragging its label. Give a `<label>` of the field, anywhere on the page, or a handle in the well the `stepper-scrub` class: dragged sideways, it steps the number by one step every 4px, toward the end of the line for more. Hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> to take ten steps at a time.

  <input class="input-group-field stepper-field" type="number" min="1" max="4096" value="320" aria-label="Width">
  <span class="input-group-addon">px</span>

- **A drag, not a press.** The drag starts once the pointer has moved 3px, so a press on a label still focuses the field. The click that ends a drag is swallowed.
- **Pointer lock.** With a mouse the pointer is locked while it drags, where the browser can, so a long drag never runs into the edge of the screen; it comes back where it was. A pen or a finger is captured by the handle instead, and a vertical swipe on a touch screen still scrolls the page.
- **Events.** The field dispatches `input` at each step and `change` once when the drag ends.
- **Direction.** In a right-to-left page, dragging toward the start of the line, to the left, is more.
- A scrub is a convenience for a pointer. The keys, the buttons and typing do everything it does, so it needs no key of its own and the handle stays out of the tab order.

## When to use something else \{#when-to-use-something-else\}

- A number that is typed once and rarely adjusted, such as a port or a price, is a plain [input](/components/input) with `type="number"`.
- A position in a wide range, where the exact figure matters less than the feel, is a [slider](/components/slider).
- A choice among a few named amounts is a [segmented control](/components/segmented).

## Keyboard \{#keyboard\}

The keys are the number input's own; the behavior adds none. Shift only matters to a scrub.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Label the field with a `<label>`, a [field](/components/input#field) or `aria-label`. Each button needs its own name that says what it changes: "More seats", not "Plus".
- Keep `min`, `max` and `step` on the field. They are what the keys, the buttons and assistive technology all read.
- The buttons stay out of the tab order. A reader on a keyboard steps with the arrow keys in the field; a reader with a pointer has targets of 30px, and a finger 44px targets around the same 30px buttons.
- A formatted figure is said through the field's `aria-valuetext`, and the display that shows it is hidden from assistive technology. A scrub handle in the well is hidden too: it repeats what the keys do.

## React, Svelte and Vue \{#frameworks\}

The `stepper` component attaches the behavior and passes the number in and out. Give it `format`, and `locale` if needed, for a [formatted figure](#formatted): it renders the `stepper-value` and the display, with the first figure already written, so the HTML a server sends shows it. A label of its field with the `stepper-scrub` class scrubs it as it does without a framework. Copy it into the project with `npx master-ui add stepper`; [React, Svelte and Vue](/guide/frameworks) says how the components are built and used.
