A tab bar holds the three to five main destinations of a small-screen app, along the bottom edge where the thumb rests. Each item is an icon over a short label. The current one is in ink and its icon rests on a soft pill; nothing else in the bar has a hue. The bar is a pane of frosted glass with a hairline above it, and it keeps clear of the home indicator.

The bar is `position: sticky` at the bottom of its scroll container. In an app shell that does not scroll as a whole, put it after the scrolling region instead; it draws the same.

The tab bar is the bar itself. Where it sits among the regions of a page is a layout's decision: in a [page](/layouts/page) it follows the main region, and in a [fixed shell](/layouts/app-shell#fixed) it takes the footer.

## When to use \{#when-to-use\}

Use a tab bar for destinations of equal weight that a reader moves between all day. For views of one page use [tabs](/components/tabs); on a wide screen the same destinations belong in a [navbar](/components/navbar) or a [nav](/components/nav). Keep labels to one word: a label that does not fit is cut with an ellipsis before it pushes its neighbors.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The bar is a `<nav>` with a label, and each item a link with `aria-current="page"` on the current one. If the items switch panels in place, use `role="tablist"` with `role="tab"` and `aria-selected`; both states draw the same.
- Every item keeps its label. An icon alone is a guess; the word under it is the name.
- Items are at least 56px tall and share the width equally, well over the 44px a touch target needs.
- The bar adds `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` under its items. Set `viewport-fit=cover` in the viewport meta tag for the inset to apply.
