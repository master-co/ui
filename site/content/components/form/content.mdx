A form layout sets the rhythm between [fields](/components/input): one distance down the column, short fields side by side, and the buttons that close it at the end. It is a layout, not a component: it draws no surface and holds no state. What it knows is when to stack.

The form is a size container, so its rows and sections follow the column the form is given, not the viewport. The same markup is two columns on a settings page and one column in a narrow dialog. Those thresholds are why this is a class and not a handful of utilities.

## Rows \{#rows\}

`form-row` holds fields that are read as one line: a first and a last name, a city and a postcode. From 28rem of form they share the line in equal columns; below that they stack. Keep a row to two or three short fields.

## Sections \{#sections\}

A `form-section` puts a heading beside the fields it describes. From 40rem of form the heading takes a third of the width and the fields the rest, the way a settings page reads; below that the heading sits above its fields. Sections are parted by a hairline.

## Actions \{#actions\}

`form-actions` closes the form: buttons at the end of the line, the confirming one last. Below 24rem of form they stack at full width and the confirming button comes first, where the thumb is. Write the buttons in reading order, cancel before confirm; the stylesheet reverses the stack.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The form classes are layout only. Labels, hints and errors come from [`field`](/components/input#hint-and-error); groups of related controls from `fieldset`.
- Give each section a heading and point the `<section>` at it with `aria-labelledby`, so the group has a name.
- The stacked actions change order visually, not in the document: the tab order stays cancel, then confirm.
- A container needs a width from its context. Inside a shrink-to-fit box (an inline-flex parent, a popover without a width) give the form a width.
