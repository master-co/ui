A caret is the mark at the end of text that is still being written: a fine upright bar in the ink of the words it follows, that fades out and back about once a second. It says "more is coming" where the words themselves cannot, at the end of an answer as it streams or after a prompt that waits.

## Where it goes \{#where-it-goes\}

Put the caret inside the element that holds the text, right after the last word, with nothing between them. It is an inline box as tall as the letters of the line it is in and takes their color, so it needs no size and no variant: it follows a heading, a paragraph and a line of code alike, on a page, a card and an ink surface.

Remove it when the text is complete. A caret that keeps blinking after the last word tells a reader to keep waiting.

## The block caret \{#block\}

`caret-block` is the wide caret of a terminal, half an em across. Use it after a command in a [code block](/components/code) or a [window](/components/window) that shows a shell, where the fine bar would be mistaken for a letter.

## Caret, shimmer or typing \{#caret-shimmer-or-typing\}

A caret follows words that exist and are growing. When nothing is written yet and a few words say work is under way, use [shimmer](/components/shimmer). When another person is typing in a conversation, a [message](/components/message) has `message-typing`. A wait that belongs to a control is a [spinner](/components/spinner).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The caret is decoration: give it `aria-hidden="true"`.
- Mark the region that is being written with `aria-busy="true"` and remove it when the text is complete, so a screen reader can read the finished text once and not each word as it arrives.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the caret does not blink; it stands still at the end of the text.
