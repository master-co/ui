A hotspot puts points on a picture that say what is there: the parts of a product, the places on a plan, the things worth noticing in a screenshot. The hotspot is the frame around the picture, and each pin is a button placed on it in percent. A pin opens what a button opens, so a [popover](/components/popover) needs no script.

## Placing a pin \{#placing-a-pin\}

`hotspot` makes the element around the picture as wide as the picture and the reference for its pins. Each `hotspot-pin` sets `--x` and `--y` in its style, in percent of the picture's width and height from its top left corner, and the pin's middle stands on that point. Percent keeps a pin on its place at any size of the picture.

A pin is a `<button>`, or a link when it goes somewhere. With `popovertarget` it opens a popover, which the browser places against the pin where anchor positioning is supported and in the middle of the viewport elsewhere. With `interestfor` it shows a [tooltip](/components/tooltip) on hover and focus.

## The pin \{#the-pin\}

A pin is a 24px disc of ink with a white rim and a dark shade outside the rim. The rim and the shade are the same in both themes, since a picture has no theme: between them a pin is found on a light picture and on a dark one. It may hold a number, which ties it to a list of the same points in the text, or nothing.

A ring leaves the pin every few seconds, to say it can be pressed. With reduced motion the ring rests. On a touch screen the pin keeps its size and its target grows to 44px.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Every pin needs an accessible name that says what it points at: `aria-label="The arm"`, not "Pin 1". A number inside the pin is not a name.
- Pins are reached with <kbd>Tab</kbd> in the order they are written, so write them in the order a reader would look, not in the order they were added.
- The picture's `alt` describes the whole picture. What the pins say must also be available without them: list the same points under the picture, numbered the same way.
- Keep pins at least 44px apart on a picture that may be seen on a phone, so two targets never overlap.
