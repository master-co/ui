A transcript is what a recording says, as a list of cues, each with the time it begins. It follows the recording as it plays: the cue being spoken is strong on a raised sheet, the cues already heard turn quiet and the ones still to come wait in the body's ink. Choose a cue and the recording plays from there. The `transcript` [behavior](/guide/behaviors) connects it to a [player](/components/player) or to a plain `<audio>` or `<video>`, named by its id in `aria-controls`.

## Following and scrolling away \{#following\}

As the recording plays, the behavior marks the current cue with `aria-current="true"`: the last cue whose `data-start` has passed, so the place is kept through a pause between two cues. Every cue before it is drawn as played, by the stylesheet alone.

The list keeps the current cue in its middle. It scrolls itself and nothing around it, so a page with a transcript on it never moves under the reader; it glides, or jumps for a reader who asks for reduced motion. When the reader scrolls the list with the wheel, a finger, the keys or the scrollbar and the current cue leaves the view, the list stops following, and `transcript-return`, a small pill at its lower edge, brings it back. Choosing a cue, or scrolling the current cue back into view, follows again.

The list scrolls inside `--transcript-height`, 20rem unless set. Add [`scroll-area`](/components/scroll-area) to the list for its thin scrollbar and the fades at the edges where it goes on.

## From a WebVTT track \{#from-a-track\}

When the list is empty, the behavior writes the cues from the media's first captions or subtitles track, read through the TextTrack API: the time, the words without their tags, and the speaker a voice tag names (`<v Ana>`). A track loads its cues only once it is not disabled, so the behavior sets a disabled track to `hidden`, which shows nothing over a picture. Here the transcript follows a plain `<audio>` with the browser's controls.

## Several voices \{#speakers\}

When the recording has more than one voice, put who speaks at the start of a cue's words in a `transcript-speaker`. It stands on a line of its own over the words, small and in the strong ink, so a reader who skims the list finds each turn. The behavior writes it from a voice tag when it writes the cues from a track; here the cues are written in the page.

## The behavior \{#behavior\}

`transcript` takes the root, the element with the `transcript` class, and returns the function that detaches it.

```js name=app.js
import { transcript } from '@master/ui/behaviors/transcript.js'

const detach = transcript(document.querySelector('.transcript'))
```

- **The media** is the one `aria-controls` names: a player, whose `player-media` it takes, or an `<audio>` or `<video>`. The behavior looks each time the media speaks, so a media written after the transcript, or another one named later, is followed. The `media` option names an element instead.
- **A cue** is an `li` with `data-start`, the second it begins, holding a `transcript-time` button and a `transcript-text`. A press anywhere on a cue plays from its start, unless the reader is selecting its words; <kbd class="kbd">Enter</kbd> on its time does the same.
- **Rows written elsewhere.** The behavior reads the list each time and keeps none of it, so a renderer may write the cues and change them. `draw: false` leaves an empty list to the renderer; `track` chooses the track the cues are written from. The rows it wrote are removed when it detaches.

| Option | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `media` | from `aria-controls` | The media element to follow |
| `track` | the first captions or subtitles track | The track an empty list is written from |
| `draw` | `true` | Whether to write an empty list from the track |

Without the behavior the cues are a list a reader reads; nothing is marked, nothing follows, and a cue does not seek.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Name the transcript with `aria-label` or a heading it points to with `aria-labelledby`, and name its media in `aria-controls`.
- The current cue is `aria-current="true"`; the stylesheet draws it in ink and depth, not in a hue, and in forced colors with a system outline.
- The transcript never takes focus by itself as the recording plays. Focus moves only when the reader moves it, and back to the current cue when the way back is chosen.
- A transcript is what a recording without a picture needs to be understood without sound. Write it in the page when the page has to be read without script.
