A flash shows where a link has landed. Put `flash` on anything with an id: a heading, a row, a comment, a card. When that id is the fragment of the address, a ring of ink stands around the element and fades away in two seconds, so a reader who followed a link to one thing among many sees which. Nothing is added by script: the address is the state.

## How it works \{#how-it-works\}

The browser marks the element whose id matches the fragment of the address as the page's `:target`. `flash` draws a ring around that element and lets it fade. Two things follow from the address being the state:

- A link from another page, a link on the same page and a pasted address all flash the same element.
- Following another fragment link moves the flash; a link to the same fragment again does not replay it.

The ring is an outline. It takes no room, follows the element's own corner and needs no pseudo-element, and it leaves the element's face, its shadow and its hover as they are. So the same class works on a card, a table row, a heading and a comment.

## Clear of the navbar \{#offset\}

A fragment link scrolls its target to the very top of the viewport, which is under a sticky [navbar](/components/navbar) if the page has one. `flash` also sets how far below the top edge the browser stops: `--flash-offset`, 5rem by default. Set it to the height of whatever is fixed at the top of your page, plus a little air.

## Without motion \{#reduced-motion\}

For a reader who asks for reduced motion nothing fades. A hairline stays around the target for as long as it is the target, which says the same thing without movement.

## Flash or mark \{#flash-or-mark\}

A flash marks a place for a moment. Words that are emphasized where they stand, for as long as they are there, are a [mark](/components/mark). News about something that just happened is a [toast](/components/toast).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The flash is for the eye. Give a target that is not a link or a control `tabindex="-1"`, so focus moves there with the jump and the next <kbd>Tab</kbd> starts from it.
- The ring stands 4px outside the element and covers nothing, so the text keeps its contrast.
- In forced colors the target takes a system-highlight outline and nothing fades.
