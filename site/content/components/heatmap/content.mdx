A heatmap shows many small numbers at once as a field of cells, each as dark as its value: activity by day, load by hour. Like the [chart](/components/chart), it is a list the stylesheet draws: a cell carries its number as `--value`, and its tone is ink mixed into the surface behind it. The scale is one material at different depths, with no hue.

## How a cell gets its tone \{#how-a-cell-gets-its-tone\}

Each `heatmap-cell` sets `--value` in its `style`; the heatmap sets the top of the scale with `--heatmap-max`. A cell's tone is its share of the maximum: full ink at the maximum and above, and a 7% tint for nothing, so an empty day still has its place in the field.

The cell's own text is its value in words: "Mon 2 Nov: 4 deployments". The text is not drawn; a screen reader reads it, and the same words in `title` are the tooltip under the pointer. The tone is a drawing of that sentence, never the only place the number lives.

## Rows and columns \{#rows-and-columns\}

Cells fill a column from the top and then start the next, the way the weeks of a year are laid out: `--heatmap-rows` is the length of a column, seven by default. Start the list on the first day of a week, or pad the first column with cells of no value. The heatmap is as wide as its columns; give the card or the cell around it a width.

`heatmap-lg` makes cells 20px, for a short period where each cell matters more.

## A legend \{#legend\}

A legend is a second heatmap of one row with a few values of the same scale, between the words for its ends. It repeats what the cells already say, so hide it from assistive technology with `aria-hidden`.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Label the list with what is measured and over what period.
- Write each cell's text as a full phrase: the date or the hour, the number and its unit. A bare number means nothing when read on its own.
- A reader who cannot see the tones gets a long list. When the period is long, offer the same numbers as a [table](/components/table) or a summary beside the heatmap.
- Tone is the only visual channel here, so do not ask the heatmap to carry a precise comparison. Use a [chart](/components/chart) when two values must be told apart.
- In forced colors the cells keep their tones, drawn between the system's text and canvas colors.

## Classes \{#classes\}

| Class | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `heatmap` | The list: columns of cells |
| `heatmap-lg` | 20px cells |
| `heatmap-cell` | One value; set `--value` in its `style` and say it in its text |

## Custom properties \{#custom-properties\}

| Property | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--value` | `0` | On a cell: its number |
| `--heatmap-max` | `4` | The value that is full ink |
| `--heatmap-rows` | `7` | Cells in a column |
| `--heatmap-size` | `.75rem` | Side of a cell |
| `--heatmap-gap` | `3px` | Space between cells |
| `--heatmap-color` | `var(--color-surface-inverse)` | The ink at the top of the scale |
