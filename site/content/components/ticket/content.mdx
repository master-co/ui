A ticket is a sheet with a tear line: a boarding pass, an event ticket, a voucher, a receipt and its stub. Two raised faces meet on a perforation, the body and the stub that would be torn off, and a half-round notch is punched into each edge where they meet. It is a material, like a [card](/components/card): what stands on it is yours.

## The tear line \{#the-tear-line\}

The body and the stub are two sheets, each with a corner of its own. Where the browser has `corner-shape`, the two corners of each face that lie on the tear line are scooped: a quarter circle cut into the sheet instead of rounded off it. Two scoops side by side make a half-round notch, and the contour and the shadow follow the notch, because they follow the corner. No mask is involved, so nothing is cut that should not be: a focus ring inside a ticket is whole.

Each face is cut straight along the tear line, so neither draws a contour across it. The only line there is the perforation, a row of 4px dashes that runs between the notches.

Without `corner-shape: scoop` the two faces meet square, and the perforation alone parts them.

## A stub at the end \{#row\}

`ticket-row` sets the stub at the end of the body instead of under it, the way a coupon is printed. The stub is as wide as what it holds, and the body takes the rest.

## Geometry \{#geometry\}

A ticket has the corner of a surface, 8px notches and 20px of padding in each face. `--ticket-notch` sets the radius of the notch and `--ticket-padding` the padding; a receipt that is mostly figures may want less. The sheet publishes `--surface-color`, so a badge's ring or a stack of avatars on it is cut in the right color.

## Ticket or card \{#ticket-or-card\}

A ticket says "this is yours to hand over": something issued to one person and checked, redeemed or torn. Anything else on a raised sheet is a [card](/components/card). Do not use the notches as decoration for ordinary content: the shape means something.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The shape is decoration. Use the element that fits the content: an `<article>` for a pass or a voucher, with a heading inside it.
- A code a person reads aloud or types belongs in [code](/components/code), which sets it in the mono face. Offer a way to copy it.
- In forced colors each face takes a system outline and the perforation is a solid line.
