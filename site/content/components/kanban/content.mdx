A kanban is a board of columns, each a named section that holds a list, where an item is moved to another place in its column or into another column. Each item has two small controls: a handle that carries it with the pointer or the keys, and a button that opens the mover, where a column and a position are chosen without a drag. What an item shows is the page's own: here a [card](/components/card) with a [badge](/components/badge) and an [avatar](/components/avatar). The `kanban` [behavior](/guide/behaviors) does the moving; nothing moves until a move is committed.

A [sortable](/components/sortable) puts one list in order. Use a kanban when an item also changes list, and the list it is in says something about it: its stage, its owner, its state.

## What the stylesheet draws \{#what-the-stylesheet-draws\}

- **The board** is a row that scrolls across. Give it a height, as the `h(376)` above does or as the main region of the [board](/pages/board) page does, and its columns stop there.
- **A column** is a tray: recessed by tone and a hairline, as wide as `--kanban-column-width` and never wider than the board less 2rem, so on a phone the next column shows at the edge. It is as tall as what it holds. Its list, `kanban-items`, is the one part that scrolls, under a header that stays.
- **One corner, one line.** A card the board holds takes `--kanban-radius` and `--kanban-padding`. A column's corner is that radius plus `--kanban-inset`, the room around its items, and its title starts on the line the cards' words start on.
- **The two controls** are a pair, 24px each and 44px tall to a finger, each target stopping halfway to the other so neither answers for its neighbor: the handle, which holds a grip icon, and `kanban-move`, which holds two arrows that pass, each a [Master Icons](/components/icon#master-icons) drawing in the markup. Both show only their icon until the pointer or the keys reach them. Only the handle takes `touch-action: none`, so a swipe that starts anywhere else on a card still scrolls. `kanban-controls` holds the pair at the end of an item's first line: it hangs into the card's padding by .5rem, as far as the grip's points stand in from its box, so the grip meets the edge the avatar under it ends on, and it centers on the title's first line.
- **The move button waits.** Where a pointer hovers, `kanban-move` is not drawn until the pointer or the keys are on its item, so a board shows one grip to a card; on a touch screen it is always drawn.
- **Carried**, an item keeps its place and fades, so the reader sees where it came from, and its grip sits in a well.
- **The place it would land in** is a line in the middle of the gap, drawn before an item marked `kanban-item-target` or after the last item of a list marked `kanban-items-target`. No item makes room: the board holds still under the pointer, which is what keeps the place from chasing itself.
- **An empty column** shows `kanban-empty`, a few words in a shallow well, in place of its list. As the place an item would land in, the well takes the marker's ink as a ring.
- **The carried sheet**, `kanban-preview`, is the item's name on a small raised sheet at the item's width. It is a manual popover, so it stands in the top layer, where no column's scrolling clips it, and it holds no copy of the card: no second id, field or picture. On the release it leaves where it is, in the time of a press, `kanban-preview-landing` while it does, and the item, whole again, is what moves: a sheet that holds only the name, sent on to the item's place, would be a second and smaller picture of the item sliding over the item itself.

With reduced motion nothing glides, and the carried item takes and leaves its faded look without a transition. In forced colors the columns, the well and the sheet are outlined and the line is drawn in the system's highlight.

## The behavior \{#behavior\}

`kanban` takes the root, the element with the `kanban` class, and returns the function that detaches it.

```js name=app.js
import { kanban } from '@master/ui/behaviors/kanban.js'

const board = kanban(document.querySelector('.kanban'))

board.value
// [{ value: 'backlog', items: ['emails', 'pricing', 'tour', 'translate'] }, { value: 'progress', items: ['post', 'table'] }, …]

board.move('pricing', 'review', 0)

// The board dispatches input, then change, once for each move that changed it.
document.querySelector('.kanban').addEventListener('change', (event) => {
  if (event.target === event.currentTarget) save(board.value)
})
```

- **The pointer.** A press on a handle that moves more than 4px picks the item up; mouse, pen and touch alike, and only the pointer that started the drag moves it. The column under the pointer, or the nearest one across, is where it would land, before the first item whose middle is under the pointer. The board is scrolled across, and that column's list along, while the pointer is near their ends. The release commits: the handle lets go, so the item is drawn whole again, and the item moves to its place. A release where the item was picked up changes nothing, and a release outside the board, <kbd class="kbd">Escape</kbd>, a cancelled pointer and a capture taken away all leave it where it was.
- **The whole item, for a mouse.** A mouse also picks an item up by any part of it that is not a link, a button or a field, and the item shows the hand there. A finger carries by the handle alone: a press anywhere else on an item is how the list is scrolled. A press on the item that does not move is still only a click on whatever it is over.
- **The keys.** <kbd class="kbd">Enter</kbd> or <kbd class="kbd">Space</kbd> on a handle picks the item up, as a press that does not move does. The arrow keys then choose a place, which the line shows; they do nothing until an item is picked up, so a screen reader's own arrow keys keep working. <kbd class="kbd">Enter</kbd> or <kbd class="kbd">Space</kbd> commits, <kbd class="kbd">Escape</kbd> cancels, and leaving the handle commits too.
- **The mover.** A button in an item whose `popovertarget` is the popover that holds the form `kanban-mover` opens it for that item. The browser opens and closes the popover; the behavior writes the columns into the select named `column` and the places of the chosen column into the select named `position`, and submitting the form commits. This is the way for a hand that cannot drag and has no keyboard, and for a screen reader on a touch screen.
- **One path.** The pointer, the keys and the mover all commit through `move(item, column, index)`, which is also there to call. `index` is the place among the column's other items: `0` is first, and a number past the end is the end. A commit that changes the board moves the item's node itself, with `moveBefore()` where the browser has it so a field's text and focus stay, brings the item into view, gives focus to its handle and dispatches. A commit to the place the item is in dispatches nothing.
- **The move is drawn.** Where the browser has view transitions scoped to an element, a commit starts one on the board: every item glides to the place it now has, clipped by its list, and the commit is finished a frame later, so `move` then answers with a promise. Elsewhere, and with reduced motion, the items take their places at once. The glide takes 160ms and the board takes no press while it lasts.
- **What the page moves.** `move` called by the page does not bring the item into view or move focus, does not move into a hidden column, and is refused while an earlier move is still being drawn. An item whose handle is `disabled` stays where it is by every way, the mover included.
- **The value** is the arrangement: one `{ value, items }` to a column, in the order of the columns. It is what has been committed, never the place being chosen. Set it to arrange the board; that dispatches nothing. A value that names a column or an item the board does not have, or names one twice, is refused and the board stays as it is, so a card is never dropped on the way. An item the value leaves out keeps its column, after the ones it names.
- **Under a move.** Setting the value, calling `move`, or anything else that rearranges the board while an item is carried cancels that move first.
- **What it says.** Each pick, place, drop and cancel is said in the `kanban-status` region with the item's name, the column and the place: "Price the annual plan: In review, position 1 of 1." Pass `messages` to say it in the page's language. An item is called by its `aria-label`, or by the first words it holds, and a column by its title.

The order of the columns is the markup's. A column with `hidden` takes no item and the keys pass over it.

Fields inside the cards and the mover's own selects send `input` and `change` through the board too, as they would through a form. A move's events are the ones whose `target` is the board.

### What this behavior measures \{#measuring\}

A [behavior](/guide/behaviors) moves attributes and leaves geometry to the stylesheet. A drag across scrolling columns cannot be found that way, so this one behavior measures, within limits that are part of its contract: it reads where the pointer is among the columns and the items, scrolls the board and a list near their ends, and writes three custom properties on the carried sheet, `--kanban-x`, `--kanban-y` and `--kanban-width`. It sets no color, no shadow and no other style, and the marks of a place are classes the stylesheet draws.

## Without the script \{#without-script\}

The board is sections, headings and lists, so without the behavior it reads in order, column by column, and scrolls. Nothing can be moved: moving needs the behavior, or a page that writes the order itself. A handle and a move button that do nothing are worse than none, so a page that may run without script renders them once the script is there.

Where a browser has no popover API, nothing follows the pointer during a drag; the faded item and the line still say what is carried and where it would land.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Each column is a `<section>` named by its title, so it is a region a screen reader can jump to, and its items are a native list that says how many it holds. The board is a `role="group"` with a name.
- Dragging is never the only way. The keys do everything the pointer does, and the mover does it with two selects and a button, which is what [WCAG 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html) asks for: a keyboard alternative alone does not reach a reader who uses touch and cannot drag.
- Name both controls with what they move: "Drag Price the annual plan", "Move Price the annual plan".
- Keep the status region in the page from the start, empty: a region added at the moment it speaks is often not heard.
- After a move focus is on the handle of the item that moved, and the item is in view. Leaving the handle with <kbd class="kbd">Tab</kbd> commits and does not pull focus back.
- Save the arrangement the way the page saves anything else, on `change`; say so near the board when a move has an effect a reader cannot see.

## React, Svelte and Vue \{#frameworks\}

The `kanban` component writes the columns and their items from `columns`, `items` and the arrangement it holds, hands an item's markup the attributes of its two buttons to spread, and attaches the behavior. When a move is committed the behavior gives the component the arrangement to draw and waits for that render before it returns focus and dispatches; an owner that does not take the arrangement leaves the board as it was. Copy it into the project with `npx master-ui add kanban`; [React, Svelte and Vue](/guide/frameworks) says how the components are built and used.

An item that changes column is rendered again in its new list, as a framework renders any item that changes parent. Keep what a card shows in your data rather than in state inside the card.
