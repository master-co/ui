A carousel is a row of slides that scrolls and snaps. It is built from CSS alone: the track is a scroll container with scroll snapping, so touch, a trackpad, the keys and assistive technology all work as scrolling does. Where the browser supports them, the stylesheet adds previous and next buttons and a dot for each slide, which the browser generates as real, focusable controls.

## The controls \{#the-controls\}

Nothing in the markup stands for the buttons or the dots. They are pseudo-elements:

- `::scroll-button(inline-start)` and `::scroll-button(inline-end)` scroll the track by a page. They are raised like every button, lift under the pointer and sink when pressed. The browser disables one at each end, and the stylesheet fades it out and takes it away, so it no longer covers the slide under it.
- `::scroll-marker` on each slide is a dot; `::scroll-marker-group` gathers them under the track. The dot of the slide in view matches `:target-current`, lengthens and takes ink. Each dot is padded into a larger target, larger still on a touch screen, so a finger does not have to find six pixels.

They are placed with anchor positioning against their own carousel, whose anchor name is scoped, so several carousels on a page do not share controls. In a browser without these pseudo-elements, the carousel is a swipeable, scrollable row and loses nothing else.

## Several in view \{#several-in-view\}

## A slide that peeks \{#peek\}

Set `--carousel-slide` under the track's width and the next slide shows its edge. That edge says there is more before any dot does, and in a browser that draws no dots it is the only thing that says so. The pictures are [figures](/components/figure): `--figure-ratio` on the track gives every picture one shape before it loads, so the row never jumps.

## Centered \{#centered\}

`carousel-center` keeps the slide in view in the middle, with a neighbor's edge on either side, and leaves room before the first slide and after the last so both reach the middle too. A slide takes 80% of the track; set `--carousel-slide` for more or less of the neighbors. The room at the ends is two empty flex items rather than padding, so a slide's width stays a share of the whole track.

## As many as fit \{#as-many-as-fit\}

Narrow the preview to see three slides become two, then one.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Give the track `role="region"`, a label and `tabindex="0"`, so the arrow keys can scroll it.
- The generated buttons are named "Previous slide" and "Next slide" by the stylesheet. Each dot takes the name of its slide's content.
- A carousel does not move by itself. Content that advances on a timer needs a pause control, which is a script's job.
- With reduced motion the track jumps between slides instead of gliding.
- In forced colors the buttons are drawn with an outline in the button color, and the dots keep their system colors, the one in view in the highlight.
