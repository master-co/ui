A carousel is a row, or a column, of slides that scrolls and snaps. It is built from CSS alone: the track is a scroll container with scroll snapping, so touch, a trackpad, the keys and assistive technology all work as scrolling does. Where the browser supports them, the stylesheet adds previous and next buttons and a dot for each slide, which the browser generates as real, focusable controls.

## The controls \{#the-controls\}

Nothing in the markup stands for the buttons or the dots. They are pseudo-elements:

- `::scroll-button(inline-start)` and `::scroll-button(inline-end)` scroll the track by a page. They are raised like every button, lift under the pointer and sink when pressed. The browser disables one at each end, and the stylesheet fades it out and takes it away, so it no longer covers the slide under it.
- `::scroll-marker` on each slide is a dot; `::scroll-marker-group` gathers them under the track. The dot of the slide in view matches `:target-current`, lengthens and takes ink. Each dot is padded into a larger target, larger still on a touch screen, so a finger does not have to find six pixels.

They are placed with anchor positioning against their own carousel, whose anchor name is scoped, so several carousels on a page do not share controls. In a browser without these pseudo-elements, the carousel is a swipeable, scrollable row with a thin scrollbar, which is how a mouse that cannot swipe gets across, and it loses nothing else. The scrollbar steps aside wherever the buttons and the dots are drawn, and in a `carousel-view`.

## A slide that peeks \{#peek\}

Set `--carousel-slide` under the track's width and the next slide shows its edge. That edge says there is more before any dot does, and in a browser that draws no dots it is the only thing that says so. The pictures are [figures](/components/figure): `--figure-ratio` on the track gives every picture one shape before it loads, so the row never jumps.

## Centered \{#centered\}

`carousel-center` keeps the slide in view in the middle, with a neighbor's edge on either side, and leaves room before the first slide and after the last so both reach the middle too. A slide takes 80% of the track; set `--carousel-slide` for more or less of the neighbors. The room at the ends is two empty flex items rather than padding, so a slide's width stays a share of the whole track.

## Read and set the slide in view \{#view\}

A page that needs to know which slide is in view, or to show one, puts the track in a `carousel-view` with previous and next buttons and a row of dots of its own. Inside a view the generated controls step aside, so there is one set of buttons. The [`carousel` behavior](/guide/behaviors) drives them:

- An `IntersectionObserver` tracks how much of each slide is in view. When scrolling settles at `scrollend`, or after a short pause in browsers without it, the most visible slide becomes current. When slides are equally visible, the current one stays selected if it is among them. Otherwise the first one in document order wins. A native snap target can be a different slide when several fit in view.
- Previous, next and a dot ask the platform to scroll the slide into view at its own snap alignment, at once under reduced motion. The behavior never sets a position.
- The current slide's dot is `aria-current="true"`, and previous and next are disabled at the ends. Several slides may be in view; there is still one current slide, and the others are not hidden.
- A slide stands for its `data-value`, or its `id`, so the current slide stays current when a slide is added before it. A change the reader makes dispatches `input` and `change`; the function it returns has a `value` to read and to set, and setting it dispatches nothing.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Give the track `role="region"`, a label and `tabindex="0"`, so the arrow keys can scroll it.
- The generated buttons are named "Previous slide" and "Next slide" by the stylesheet. Name them in the page's language with `--carousel-previous-label` and `--carousel-next-label`, quoted strings set on the carousel or an ancestor such as the root. Each dot takes the name of its slide's content.
- A carousel does not move by itself. Content that advances on a timer needs a pause control, which is a script's job.
- With reduced motion the track jumps between slides instead of gliding.
- In forced colors the buttons are drawn with an outline in the button color, and the dots keep their system colors, the one in view in the highlight.
- In a `carousel-view`, name the buttons and the dots in the page's language; a dot says which slide of how many it goes to.

## React, Svelte and Vue \{#frameworks\}

The `carousel` component writes the slides from items, each with a `value` that stays the same when the items change, and the view's buttons and dots beside them; its value is the current slide. Copy it into the project with `npx master-ui add carousel`; [React, Svelte and Vue](/guide/frameworks) says how the components are built and used. A carousel nobody reads needs no component: the markup above is enough.
