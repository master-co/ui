A checklist is a short list of things to do: the steps of a setup, the points of a review. Each step is a native [checkbox](/components/checkbox) in a label with a title and a line of help. A step that is done steps back, with a hairline through its title, and the list counts itself.

## The list counts itself \{#the-list-counts-itself\}

Two CSS counters do the arithmetic. Every `checklist-item` adds one to the total, and an item whose checkbox is checked, which the item knows through `:has()`, adds one to the number done. `checklist-count` prints both.

Counters run in document order, so the count comes after the steps in the markup: that is where both numbers are final. Its own text follows the numbers, which keeps the words yours to translate. `--checklist-of` is what stands between the two numbers, `' / '` by default; set it to `' of '` for "2 of 4".

The count is generated content. It is right at every moment, but a change to it is not announced: when finishing the list matters, say so in a status message of your own.

## A step \{#a-step\}

The label is the whole row, so a press anywhere on the words checks the box. `checklist-title` is an inline element on purpose: the stroke through a finished title is a background that grows along the text, and it stays one line however the title wraps. `checklist-end` holds something at the end of a step, such as how long it takes.

Rows part on hairlines that start at the text, as a [list](/components/list)'s do. The checklist has no surface: put it in a [card](/components/card).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Use a `<ul>`, or an `<ol>` when the steps must be done in order.
- A checkbox here is state the reader sets. For steps the system completes, show them with [steps](/components/steps) instead: a checkbox that a reader cannot change is a disabled control, not a record.
- The struck title keeps the muted ink at a readable contrast; the meaning is carried by the checkbox, not by the stroke.
