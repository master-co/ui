Expand shows the first lines of a long text and opens the rest on request: a description, a review, a changelog entry. The closed text fades out at its foot, and the toggle under it turns over as it opens to its full height.

It is a native `<details>`, and the toggle is its `<summary>`: the keyboard, find-in-page and assistive technology already know how it works. Where the browser exposes `::details-content`, the closed state is a preview instead of nothing. Elsewhere the text is hidden while closed, as in any details, and the toggle still opens it.

The summary comes first in the markup, as `<details>` requires, and is drawn under the text.

## Lines \{#lines\}

`--expand-lines` sets the height of the preview in lines of the text's own line height. The default is four.

## The toggle's words \{#words\}

Put the two wordings in `expand-more` and `expand-less`; the stylesheet shows the one that fits the state. Say what opening gives when you can ("Read the full review") instead of a bare "More". With one wording that suits both states, write it as plain text in the summary.

## When to use \{#when-to-use\}

Use expand for prose that most readers skim. For a list of questions and answers use an [accordion](/components/accordion), where each row is closed to its heading. Keep links and controls out of the hidden part: while closed, the keyboard can still reach what the preview has clipped.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The summary is a button to assistive technology and reports whether the text is expanded. Find-in-page opens the details when a match is inside it.
- The fade at the foot is a mask over the text, so the last visible line dims; the first lines keep full contrast.
- With reduced motion the text opens at once.
