A texture is one drawing repeated across the face of a surface. Use `.texture` on any element for room that holds no content and still has to say what it is. It is a background image in one quiet ink, so it stands on whatever color the element already has: a well stays a well, with dots across it.

## Drawings \{#drawings\}

There are five, and each says one thing about the room it fills. Choose by that meaning, not by which looks best: a reader who meets stripes in two places should be able to assume the same thing about both.

| Class | Says | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `texture` | An open stage | The field a specimen, a canvas or a diagram is seen against |
| `texture texture-grid` | Measured room | A canvas where position matters: an editor, a floor plan, a layout lesson |
| `texture texture-stripes` | Room kept empty | A gutter, a clear space, a slot nothing fills, a placeholder for a picture |
| `texture texture-crosshatch` | Room that cannot be used | A blocked time, a day that is closed, a range out of bounds |
| `texture texture-checker` | Nothing at all | The ground under a transparent picture or a color with alpha |

Words never stand on a texture. The marks run through the letters and make them harder to read, so put a caption beside the textured room or on a sheet over it.

## Kept and unavailable \{#kept-and-unavailable\}

Stripes and a crosshatch part two kinds of empty. A striped slot is kept free and may be filled later; a crosshatched one cannot be chosen. Say the same in words, since a drawing is not read by everyone: here the closed hours are also disabled and named.

## Size, weight and ink \{#size-weight-and-ink\}

Three custom properties tune a drawing where it stands. `--texture-size` is the side of one tile, `--texture-weight` the radius of a dot or the width of a rule, and `--texture-ink` the color of the marks. A theme sets the ink for every texture at once with `--color-texture`.

Keep a texture quieter than what stands on it. On a stage that holds a specimen, a sparse, fine drawing is enough for the eye to find an edge against; a dense one competes with the specimen.

Stripes turn 45° in a square tile, so they stand the tile's side over √2 apart: 5.7px in the default 8px tile. Keep the tile a whole number of pixels, or the lines drift from one tile to the next.

Tiles are counted from the middle of the room, so a drawing is cut the same way at opposite edges, whatever the room's size. Set `background-position` to start it from a corner instead.

## Fade \{#fade\}

`texture-fade` thins the drawing out toward the edges, for a field behind a heading. It is a mask, and a mask fades what the element holds too, so put it on an element of its own behind the content and hide that element from assistive technology.

## Layering \{#layering\}

A texture takes the place of the face's own image, and the element keeps its color. To keep another image as well, or to cut a shape with the drawing, read `--texture-image` on the element that carries the class: the drawing is published there.

```css
.texture.stage {
  background-image: var(--texture-image), linear-gradient(var(--color-surface-raised), var(--color-surface-raised-low));
  background-size: var(--texture-size) var(--texture-size), auto;
}
```

A crosshatch is two layers, so give it two sizes.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

A texture is a drawing, and it is removed in forced colors. Never let it carry a meaning alone:

- Room that is only decorated needs nothing. An element that exists just to hold a texture takes `aria-hidden="true"`.
- Room whose texture means something (kept free, closed, transparent) says so in words: text beside it, or `role="img"` with an `aria-label` on the room itself.
- A control that cannot be used is `disabled` first. A crosshatch only repeats what the attribute says.
