A poll is a question, a few answers, and how the votes fell. Each answer is a `<label>` around a native radio, so the group keeps the arrow keys and a form keeps the value. Before a vote the answers are flat faces on one contour. Once one is chosen, every answer fills from its start with a tint as long as its share and its figure appears at the end. The checked input is all that drives it: there is no script.

## How the result shows \{#how-the-result-shows\}

Each `poll-option` carries its share of the votes as `--value`, from 0 to 100, and the same figure as text in `poll-value`. While no input is checked, the shares have no width and the figures are hidden, from assistive technology too. When an answer is checked, `:has()` sees it on the group: every share grows to its width and every figure fades in.

The chosen answer takes an ink contour, a stronger tint and a check after its words. Choosing another answer moves all three; the shares stay.

The page supplies the numbers. If the result should include the vote just cast, update `--value` and the text when the vote is recorded: the shares ease to their new widths.

## A poll that is over \{#closed\}

Disable the inputs when voting has ended. The result shows from the start, nothing can be chosen, and the answers stay as legible as before, since a result is there to be read. Keep `checked` on the answer the reader chose.

## Several answers \{#several-answers\}

Use checkboxes when a reader may choose more than one. The result shows as soon as one is checked, each chosen answer takes the contour and the check, and the shares no longer add up to 100.

## Poll or choice \{#poll-or-choice\}

A poll asks a question whose answer is everyone's: choosing shows the result. A [choice](/components/choice) is an option of a form that explains itself in a sentence, and shows nothing afterwards. A result nobody votes on is a [chart](/components/chart) with `chart-horizontal`.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Use a `<fieldset>` with the question in its `<legend>`, so the question is announced with each answer. Radios in it share a `name`.
- Each answer is a `<label>` around its input, so the whole face is the target, 38px tall and 44px on a touch screen. The focus ring is drawn around the answer.
- The figures are text. They are hidden with `visibility` until the result shows, so they are not read early, and they are in the document for a reader who cannot see the tint.
- The check is the sign of the chosen answer for a reader who does not see the contour; in forced colors the chosen answer takes the system highlight.
