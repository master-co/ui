Bullets are a list in running text: points in an article, what a plan includes, steps written as prose. Each marker hangs in a column of its own and stands on the first line of its item, so however the text wraps, every item starts on one edge. It is a native `<ul>` or `<ol>`.

## Markers \{#markers\}

The marker is a small dot. Two classes on the list change it:

- `bullets-check` draws a check in the ink of the text, for what is included: the features of a plan, the things a release brings.
- `bullets-numbered` draws each item's number in tabular figures, set to the end of the marker column so the points line up from 9 to 10. Use it on an `<ol>`, where the order is part of the meaning.

## What is not included \{#not-included\}

In a list of what a plan includes, the lines it does not include are part of the answer. `bullets-off` on an item draws a short dash in place of its marker and sets its words a step quieter. The dash and the tone are for the eye: start the item with words such as "Not included:" in a visually hidden span, as the example does with the `sr-only` utility, so the same is said to a reader who sees neither.

## Nesting and rhythm \{#nesting\}

A `bullets` list inside an item is indented to the edge of the item's text and stands 8px under it. Items are 8px apart, set by `--bullets-gap`; `--bullets-marker` is the width of the marker column. The list takes the size, the line height and the ink of the text it is in, and the marker follows the first line. So the same list works in a card at 14px and in an article at 17px, and on an ink surface its markers turn with its words.

The numbers are the browser's own, so the list's attributes work: `start` on the `<ol>` resumes a sequence, `value` on an item skips ahead and `reversed` counts down. A list inside an item chooses its marker for itself, so a numbered step may hold plain points.

## Bullets, list or checklist \{#bullets-list-or-checklist\}

Bullets are sentences. Rows of things, each with media, a title and something at the end, are a [list](/components/list). Things to do that a reader ticks off are a [checklist](/components/checklist). Terms and their values are a [data list](/components/data-list).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Use a `<ul>` or, when order matters, an `<ol>`, with an `<li>` for every item. Decimal markers come from the native ordered list. Dots and checks are visual cues.
- Safari with VoiceOver does not announce a list whose markers are removed unless it has `role="list"`. Add it when the count of items matters to understanding.
- A check says "included" and a dash says "not included" only to the eye. Say it in words for the items that are off.
