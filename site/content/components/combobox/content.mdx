A combobox is a text field that opens a [listbox](/components/listbox) of matches: a select you can type into. It is made of parts you already have, an [input group](/components/input#input-group) and a floating listbox, inside a `combobox` wrapper that ties the two together. The stylesheet places and draws it; the `combobox` [behavior](/guide/behaviors) filters the options, moves through them and writes the chosen one into the field.

## What the stylesheet does \{#what-the-stylesheet-does\}

- **Placement.** The `combobox` wrapper names itself as an anchor and scopes the name to itself. Its listbox opens 6px under the whole field, not under the caret or the button, and takes at least the field's width through `anchor-size()`. Nothing is measured, and several comboboxes on a page do not interfere.
- **The chevron.** `combobox-toggle` is a small button at the field's end that holds a chevron icon. The chevron turns over while the field says `aria-expanded="true"`.
- **The empty state.** `listbox-empty` appears by itself once every option is `hidden`.
- **The field's states.** Hover, focus, invalid and disabled come from the input group.

In a browser without anchor positioning the listbox still opens, directly under the field where it stands in the markup, at its own width.

## The behavior \{#behavior\}

`combobox` takes the root, the element with the `combobox` class, and an object of options, and returns the function that detaches it. It finds the field by `role="combobox"` and the list by `role="listbox"`. Its `filter` option is true by default; `filter: false` leaves the options to the page, as [Autocomplete](#autocomplete) shows. `name`, `removeLabel` and `draw` concern [several values](#several-values).

```js name=app.js
import { combobox } from '@master/ui/behaviors/combobox.js'

const box = combobox(document.querySelector('.combobox'))
box.value // what the chosen option stands for, or null
box.query // the text in the field
```

The function it returns has two properties, read and set from outside. Setting either dispatches nothing.

- **`value`** is what the chosen option stands for, its `data-value` or its text, and null while the field holds text no option was chosen for. Setting it checks that option and writes its text into the field.
- **`query`** is the text in the field, what the reader is looking for. It is not the value: it changes with every key, and once an option is chosen it is that option's text. Setting it writes the field and filters the options as typing does.

- **Typing** sets `hidden` on the options whose text does not hold what is typed, whatever its case, opens the list and makes the first match the active option.
- **The active option** has `aria-selected="true"` and is named by `aria-activedescendant` on the field. Focus stays in the field.
- **Choosing**, with <kbd>Enter</kbd> or the pointer, writes the option's text into the field, without its `listbox-description` and `listbox-end`, marks the option `aria-checked="true"` and the others `false`, and closes the list. Typing over a chosen value takes its check away.
- **Opening and closing.** The listbox is `popover="manual"`, so typing and clicking in the field do not dismiss it. The behavior opens it on a click in the field, on the toggle and on the arrow keys, closes it on <kbd>Esc</kbd> and when focus leaves the combobox, and keeps `aria-expanded` in step.
- **Events.** The field dispatches `input`, then `change`, when an option is chosen, as it does for typing. The value is the field's `value`.

Disabled and hidden options are skipped. While an input method composes, <kbd>Enter</kbd> and the arrows belong to it.

Without the behavior the field is a text input: the reader can type a value, and the list does not open.

## Several values \{#several-values\}

A listbox with `aria-multiselectable="true"` holds several values. The field is then an input group with `input-group-wrap`, as in a [tags field](/components/tags), and each chosen option is a [chip](/components/chip) before it. Choosing checks or unchecks an option, empties the field and keeps the list open, so the next value is a few keys away. The options keep their checks, so the list says what is chosen as well as the chips do.

- **The field only searches.** Typing filters the list and never takes a check away; the chips keep what is chosen.
- **The chips.** The field is the one tab stop. <kbd class="kbd">Backspace</kbd> in an empty field and the arrow key that points back reach the chips, and the keys there are those of the [tags field](/components/tags#keyboard). Removing a chip, with its button or <kbd class="kbd">Backspace</kbd>, unchecks its option.
- **The form.** A chip the behavior writes carries a hidden input with the value when the chips already carry one with a `name`, or when `name` is given. `removeLabel` names its remove button in the page's language.
- **The value** is the list of chosen values, in the order they were chosen.

When the values are typed freely rather than chosen from a list, use a [tags field](/components/tags).

## Autocomplete \{#autocomplete\}

When the matches come from a server, or from a search of the page's own, attach the behavior with `filter: false`. It hides nothing: on each `input` of the field, read `query`, look for it and write the matches as the list's options. Options that arrive after the typing that asked for them still get an active option, so <kbd>Enter</kbd> and the arrows work on them.

<ComponentPreview name="autocomplete" height={360} title="Autocomplete" background="plain" bodyClass="content-start p-lg min-h(360)" behaviors html={`<div class="field max-w(320) mx-auto">
  <label for="city">City</label>
  <div class="combobox" id="city-combobox">
    <div class="input-group">
      <input class="input-group-field" id="city" role="combobox" aria-expanded="false" aria-controls="city-list" aria-autocomplete="list" autocomplete="off" placeholder="Start typing a city">
      <button type="button" class="combobox-toggle" tabindex="-1" aria-label="Show cities"><svg class="icon" viewBox="0 0 16 16" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M6 3.5L10.3586 7.8586A0.2 0.2 0 0 1 10.3586 8.1414L6 12.5" transform="rotate(90 8 8)"/></svg></button>
    </div>
    <div id="city-list" popover="manual" class="listbox" role="listbox" aria-label="Cities">
      <div class="listbox-empty">No cities start with that.</div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
<script>
addEventListener('DOMContentLoaded', function () {
  var root = document.getElementById('city-combobox')
  var list = document.getElementById('city-list')
  var cities = ['Amsterdam', 'Athens', 'Auckland', 'Bangkok', 'Barcelona', 'Berlin', 'Bogotá', 'Boston', 'Brussels', 'Buenos Aires', 'Cairo', 'Cape Town', 'Chicago', 'Copenhagen', 'Dublin', 'Frankfurt', 'Helsinki', 'Hong Kong', 'Istanbul', 'Lisbon', 'London', 'Los Angeles', 'Madrid', 'Melbourne', 'Mexico City', 'Montreal', 'Mumbai', 'Nairobi', 'New York', 'Oslo', 'Paris', 'Prague', 'Rome', 'San Francisco', 'Santiago', 'São Paulo', 'Seoul', 'Singapore', 'Stockholm', 'Sydney', 'Taipei', 'Tokyo', 'Toronto', 'Vienna', 'Warsaw', 'Zurich']
  var box = MasterUI.combobox(root, { filter: false })
  var asked = 0

  // Stands for a request to a server: the cities that start with the query, a moment later.
  function search(query) {
    return new Promise(function (resolve) {
      setTimeout(function () {
        var text = query.trim().toLowerCase()
        resolve(cities.filter(function (city) { return city.toLowerCase().startsWith(text) }).slice(0, 6))
      }, 120)
    })
  }

  function write(matches) {
    list.querySelectorAll('.listbox-option').forEach(function (option) { option.remove() })
    matches.forEach(function (city) {
      var option = document.createElement('div')
      option.className = 'listbox-option'
      option.setAttribute('role', 'option')
      option.setAttribute('aria-checked', 'false')
      option.textContent = city
      list.insertBefore(option, list.querySelector('.listbox-empty'))
    })
  }

  root.addEventListener('input', function (event) {
    // A choice says input too; its text is the option's, with nothing more to look for.
    if (event.target.getAttribute('role') !== 'combobox' || box.value !== null) return
    var ticket = ++asked
    search(box.query).then(function (matches) {
      // An answer to an older query that arrives late is dropped.
      if (ticket === asked) write(matches)
    })
  })
  search('').then(write)
})
</script>`} caption="Type a few letters: the page searches for the query and writes the options a moment later. Choose one with the arrow keys and Enter." />

- **Read `query`, not the event.** The query is the field's text; `query` reads it the same way whether the reader typed it or a framework wrote it.
- **A choice dispatches `input` too.** Its text is the chosen option's, and `value` is no longer null: there is nothing to look for, and writing new options then would take the chosen one away.
- **Answers arrive out of order.** Keep only the answer to the newest query.
- **The empty state is the stylesheet's.** `listbox-empty` shows by itself while the list holds no option.

In React, Svelte and Vue, the component's `query` and `onQueryChange` (a bindable `query` in Svelte, `v-model:query` in Vue) carry the same text: search for it, and give the matches as `options` with `filter` set to false.

An option hidden in the markup stays hidden when its label matches a query and when the search is cleared. Use `filter: false` when the application supplies the matching options itself.

## Choosing a control \{#choosing-a-control\}

For up to a dozen plain options, a [select](/components/select) does this with no script and the platform's own type-ahead. Reach for a combobox when the list is long enough to search, loads as the reader types, or has rich rows.

## Keyboard \{#keyboard\}

The keys are those of the behavior. With the list closed, and with <kbd>Shift</kbd> held, <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> move the caret as in any field. <kbd>Enter</kbd> without an active option is left alone, so it still submits a form.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The input has `role="combobox"`, `aria-controls` pointing at the listbox, `aria-autocomplete="list"` and `aria-expanded`.
- Focus stays in the input; the active option is announced through `aria-activedescendant`.
- Take the toggle button out of the tab order with `tabindex="-1"`: the arrow keys already open the list from the field.
- Set `autocomplete="off"` so the browser's own suggestions do not cover the list.

## React, Svelte and Vue \{#frameworks\}

The `combobox` component renders this markup from a list of options, attaches the behavior and passes the chosen value in and out. With `multiple` it writes the chips from the value, which is then a list. An option with a `group` is written under that caption, in a `listbox-group` with `role="group"` labelled by its `listbox-label`; a group whose options are all filtered out hides with them. Apart from the value it carries the query: `query` and `onQueryChange` in React, a bindable `query` with `onQueryChange` in Svelte, `v-model:query` in Vue. The query comes out as the reader types and once they choose, after the value; a query the owner gives is written into the field and filters the options. Copy it into the project with `npx master-ui add combobox`; [React, Svelte and Vue](/guide/frameworks) says how the components are built and used.
