A dialog is the highest surface on the ladder: an overlay sheet over a scrim that dims and softens the page. Use `.dialog` on a native `<dialog>`. A button with `commandfor` and `command="show-modal"` opens it, so the browser supplies the top layer, the focus trap and <kbd>Esc</kbd>, and no script is involved.

## Opening and closing \{#opening-and-closing\}

Three attributes replace the script a modal used to need:

- `commandfor="id"` with `command="show-modal"` on a button opens the dialog as a modal. `command="close"` closes it and returns focus to the button that opened it.
- `closedby="any"` on the dialog adds light dismiss: a click on the scrim closes it, as <kbd>Esc</kbd> always does. Leave it out when a stray click must not discard work. A browser that does not know the attribute keeps <kbd>Esc</kbd> and the close buttons.
- A `<form method="dialog">` inside the dialog closes it on submit and reports which button was used through the dialog's `returnValue`.

While a modal dialog is open, the page behind it is inert and does not scroll.

## With a form \{#with-a-form\}

`dialog-body` holds the content between the header and the footer. It takes the remaining height and scrolls inside the sheet when the content is longer than the viewport allows. `dialog-close` puts a quiet square button in the corner: give it an accessible name.

## Sizes \{#sizes\}

A dialog is 28rem wide. `dialog-sm` is 22rem, for a confirmation of one sentence, and `dialog-lg` is 40rem. Each keeps 1rem clear of the viewport on a narrow screen. The footer answers to the dialog's own width through a container query: under 22rem its buttons stack at full width, the main action first.

## Sheet \{#sheet\}

`dialog-sheet` rises from the bottom edge, for a short task or a list of choices on a small screen, where the thumb is. It spans the width up to its `--dialog-width`, squares its lower corners against the edge and clears the home indicator with `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`.

## Swipe \{#swipe\}

Add `dialog-swipe` to a drawer or a sheet, and it follows a finger, a trackpad or a mouse and is let go of to close. The dialog becomes the track the panel moves on: transparent and as large as the viewport. Put everything the dialog held in a `dialog-panel`, which wears the material, and give a sheet a `dialog-handle`, a short groove at its top edge that shows where to take hold. Attach the `drawer` behavior; `attach()` finds every `dialog-swipe`.

What a swipe is made of:

- **The browser moves the panel.** The track is a scroller with snap points: the open rest, and a stretch past the edge as large as the viewport where the panel goes when it is let go of. A finger, a trackpad and a wheel scroll it, so following the hand, the momentum of a flick and the choice of where to settle are the browser's own. The track is a reversed flex line with the panel at its end, so it starts at the open rest with no script.
- **The behavior does what scrolling cannot.** It closes the dialog once the panel has left the track, by whatever way it left. It gives `closedby="any"` back: the track covers the scrim the browser listens on, so the behavior closes the dialog on a press beside the panel. It lets a mouse, which cannot scroll by dragging, take the panel by its handle or by any part that is not a control or the body, and lets it go to the rest it was flung toward or the nearer one; while a fine pointer holds the panel the track stops snapping. It writes how much of the scrim is left to `--dialog-scrim`, so the scrim thins as the panel leaves.
- **The dialog stays a dialog.** <kbd>Esc</kbd>, the close buttons, the focus trap, the focus that returns to the trigger and the slide in and out are the same as for any drawer or sheet.
- **A body that scrolls keeps its own scroll.** With a mouse or a wheel a drag in `dialog-body` scrolls it or selects text. On a touch screen a drag that reaches the top of the body goes on to move the sheet.

Swiping is a shortcut: keep a close button or an action that closes the dialog, and keep <kbd>Esc</kbd>.

### Detents \{#detents\}

Add `dialog-detents` to a swiped sheet and it rests at two heights: half the viewport, where it opens, and full height. A drag or a flick moves it between them; a flick down from full height stops at half, and only one from half height lets it go. Make the handle a `<button>` with an accessible name: a press, <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> moves the sheet to the other height, <kbd>↑</kbd> grows it to full height and <kbd>↓</kbd> shrinks it to half. Its `aria-expanded` says whether the sheet is at full height.

At half height the lower half of the sheet waits below the edge, its footer too: detents suit a sheet to browse, a list, comments or a map, whose actions sit at its top. A sheet shorter than half the viewport has one rest, full height, and its handle says so.

### Without a feature \{#swipe-fallbacks\}

- Without scripting, the stretch past the edge is not a rest (`@media (scripting: enabled)`): a panel let go of springs back, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> and the close buttons close the dialog. Attach the behavior wherever scripts run, or a panel let go of rests off screen while the page stays inert.
- Without the behavior the scrim stays whole until the dialog closes. It is not a scroll-driven animation: in a reversed scroller Chromium and WebKit run scroll and view timelines in opposite directions.
- Where `scroll-initial-target` is supported (Chromium), a sheet with detents opens at half height before any script runs; elsewhere it opens at full height until the behavior moves it.

Swiping was verified in Chromium and WebKit, with a mouse, a wheel and, in Chromium, touch. Scroll snapping, reversed flex scrolling and `scrollend` are in every current browser.

## Motion \{#motion\}

The dialog fades in from 97% of its size and half a rem below, over 280ms; it leaves in 160ms. The stylesheet writes both directions in CSS: `@starting-style` gives the first rendered frame, and `display` and `overlay` transition with `allow-discrete`, so the exit plays before the dialog leaves the top layer. With reduced motion only the fade remains.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Name the dialog with `aria-labelledby` pointing at its `dialog-title`.
- Opened with `show-modal`, focus moves into the dialog and returns to the invoking button on close. Put `autofocus` on the field a reader should start in. For a destructive confirmation, put it on the safe action.
- `command` and `commandfor` are supported in current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. In an older browser, call `dialog.showModal()` from a click handler; the stylesheet is the same.
- A swipe is never the only way to close: keep a close button or an action that closes, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> still does. In a sheet with detents the handle is a button with a name, such as "Sheet height", whose keys and press change the height a drag changes; without detents it is a groove hidden from assistive technology. Its target is 64 by 24px, 44px tall on a touch screen.

## React, Svelte and Vue \{#frameworks\}

The `dialog` component opens and closes the dialog from the application's state and says when the browser did. The trigger and the parts inside stay the page's own markup. It attaches the `drawer` behavior to every dialog, so a drawer or a sheet given `dialog-swipe` and a `dialog-panel` is let go of to close and says so like any other close; on a dialog without `dialog-swipe` the behavior does nothing. Copy it into the project with `npx master-ui add dialog`; [React, Svelte and Vue](/guide/frameworks) says how the components are built and used.
