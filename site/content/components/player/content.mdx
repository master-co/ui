A player puts controls of the page's own over a native `<audio>` or `<video>`. The media stays the element that plays: it keeps its semantics, its place in the browser's media controls and its `controls` attribute, which is the fallback. The `player` [behavior](/guide/behaviors) takes that attribute away as it attaches, and from then on mirrors the media into the row: the play button is pressed while it plays, the seek track is the [slider](/components/slider)'s own range input with what has loaded drawn under what has played, and the times are small tabular figures that keep their width.

## Video \{#video\}

`player-video` makes the player the frame of a video. The picture fills a dark field with a surface's corner and the same row stands over its lower edge, on a shade that rises from the edge. The row is drawn in the dark scheme in either theme, as a screen is dark. A video adds three controls, each shown only where it can work: captions when the video has a captions or subtitles track, picture in picture where the browser has it, full screen where the browser allows it. A click on the picture plays and pauses it, and a double click fills the screen.

While the video plays and the pointer has been still for two and a half seconds, or has left the frame, the row fades out of the picture and the pointer with it. Any movement brings it back. It never fades while a control has keyboard focus or the speed menu is open, and never for a reader who asks for reduced motion. On a touch screen, a tap on a picture whose controls have faded only brings them back.

## The behavior \{#behavior\}

`player` takes the root, the element with the `player` class, and returns the function that detaches it and gives the media its controls back.

```js name=app.js
import { player } from '@master/ui/behaviors/player.js'

const detach = player(document.querySelector('.player'))
```

The media is the one source of truth. The behavior listens to it and mirrors what it says into attributes the stylesheet reads, and every control asks the media to change rather than changing itself, so a recording played from elsewhere (the system's media keys, another script) is drawn the same.

- **Toggles.** The play button is `aria-pressed="true"` while the media plays, the mute button while it is muted, the captions button while the first captions or subtitles track is showing, the picture-in-picture and full screen buttons while the video is in either. A toggle keeps one name, Play or Mute, and its `aria-pressed` says the state. With a `player-off` and a `player-on` face it shows the one for its state; with one face it sinks into a well while it is on.
- **Time.** The seek track's `max` is the duration and its `value` the current time, with an `aria-valuetext` in words. The elapsed time and the duration are written into their parts, with hours once the recording is an hour long. While the reader drags the track, the media does not move the thumb under the finger.
- **Loaded.** What has loaded around the current time is `--player-buffered` on the root, from 0 to 1, drawn on the track under what has played.
- **Volume and speed.** The volume slider holds the volume, and keeps it, a step quieter, while the media is muted; it is hidden on a device whose volume only its own buttons set. The speed button opens the menu its `popovertarget` names; each `menu-input` radio in it is a rate, and the behavior writes the rate on the button and checks its radio.
- **Captions.** Turned off, a track is `hidden`, not `disabled`, so a [transcript](/components/transcript) that reads the same track keeps its cues.
- **Full screen** is the player's, not the video's, so the row stays over the picture. On an iPhone, which lets only a video fill the screen, it is the system's own player.
- **The value.** The function it returns has a `value`, the current time in seconds; set it to seek.

| Option | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `step` | `5` | Seconds the arrow keys seek by |
| `jump` | `10` | Seconds <kbd class="kbd">J</kbd> and <kbd class="kbd">L</kbd> seek by |
| `volumeStep` | `0.1` | How much <kbd class="kbd">ArrowUp</kbd> and <kbd class="kbd">ArrowDown</kbd> change the volume |
| `idle` | `2500` | Milliseconds of a still pointer before a playing video's controls fade |
| `valueText` | English | The seek track's `aria-valuetext`, from the time and the duration, in the page's language |
| `rateText` | `1.5×` | What the speed button says for a rate |

## Keyboard \{#keyboard\}

The keys work wherever focus is in the player, and after a click on a video's picture. A key a control needs stays the control's: <kbd class="kbd">Space</kbd> presses a focused button, the arrows move the volume slider, and the speed menu has the keys of a [menu](/components/menu).

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Give the root `role="group"` and an `aria-label` that names the recording, so the controls are heard as the controls of that recording.
- Name every button for what it does, in the page's language, and keep the name when the state changes: the state is `aria-pressed`.
- A video with speech needs captions: add a `<track kind="captions">`. A recording without a picture needs a [transcript](/components/transcript).
- Never start a recording with sound by itself. The player plays when the reader asks.
- The buttons are 32px at every pointer; on touch screens a target reaches 44px above and below each. The times keep their width as the digits change, so the track does not shift while it plays.

## React, Svelte and Vue \{#frameworks\}

The `player` component renders the media and its row from `src`, `video`, `poster` and `tracks`, attaches the behavior, and passes the current time and whether it plays in and out: `time` and `playing` with `onTimeChange` and `onPlayingChange` in React, bindable props in Svelte, `v-model:time` and `v-model:playing` in Vue. A time set from outside seeks; `playing` plays and pauses. Copy it into the project with `npx master-ui add player`; [React, Svelte and Vue](/guide/frameworks) says how the components are built and used.
