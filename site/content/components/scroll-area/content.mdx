A scroll area is a region that scrolls inside the page: a long list in a panel, a wide table, a log. It keeps the platform's own scrolling, with a thin quiet scrollbar, and adds one thing: where content continues past an edge, that edge fades out, and where the content ends, the edge is clean.

## How the fade knows \{#how-the-fade-knows\}

The fade is a mask whose two stops are custom properties, `--scroll-area-start` and `--scroll-area-end`. Two animations on the region's own scroll timeline drive them: one runs over the first 1.5rem of scrolling and opens the fade at the start edge, the other runs over the last 1.5rem and closes the fade at the end edge. There is no script and there are no sentinel elements. While nothing overflows, the timeline is inactive and both edges stay clean.

In a browser without scroll-driven animations the region scrolls the same and has no fades.

## Along the inline axis \{#inline\}

`scroll-area-inline` scrolls sideways and fades the start and end edges instead: a row of cards, or a [table](/components/table#wide-tables) wider than its column.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- A region that scrolls must be reachable by the keys. Give it `tabindex="0"`, a `role` such as `region` and a label, unless it already holds focusable content.
- The mask fades the region's own scrollbar at the same edges. In forced colors the mask is removed.
- Give the region a height or a maximum height; without one it grows with its content and never scrolls.
