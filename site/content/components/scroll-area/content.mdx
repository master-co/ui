A scroll area is a region that scrolls inside the page: a long list in a panel, a wide table, a log. It keeps the platform's own scrolling, with a thin quiet scrollbar, and adds one thing: where content continues past an edge, that edge fades out, and where the content ends, the edge is clean. A region that grows at its end while it is read, such as a chat or a build log, can also [follow what arrives](#follow).

## How the fade knows \{#how-the-fade-knows\}

The fade is a mask whose two stops are custom properties, `--scroll-area-start` and `--scroll-area-end`. Two animations on the region's own scroll timeline drive them: one runs over the first 1.5rem of scrolling and opens the fade at the start edge, the other runs over the last 1.5rem and closes the fade at the end edge. There is no script and there are no sentinel elements. While nothing overflows, the timeline is inactive and both edges stay clean.

In a browser without scroll-driven animations the region scrolls the same and has no fades.

## Scrolling past it \{#scrolling-past-it\}

The page goes on scrolling past a scroll area along the block axis: a wheel or a swipe that starts over a region with nothing left to scroll moves the page, as it would over any other content. Only the inline axis is contained, so a sideways swipe that reaches the end of a row stops there instead of turning into the browser's back gesture.

## Along the inline axis \{#inline\}

`scroll-area-inline` scrolls sideways and fades the start and end edges instead: a row of cards, or a [table](/components/table#wide-tables) wider than its column.

## Following what arrives \{#follow\}

A chat, a build log or a streamed answer grows at its end while it is read. Give the region `role="log"` and attach the `follow` [behavior](/guide/behaviors): it keeps the end in view while content arrives, stops following the moment the reader scrolls away from the end, and follows again when they come back to it. Put a `scroll-area-latest` button after the content, with `hidden`: it appears only while the region does not follow, and pressing it scrolls to the end and follows again.

How it decides:

- **Following.** The region follows from the moment the behavior is attached: it is scrolled to its end, and each time what it holds grows, whether a row is added, a streamed word lands in the last message or an image finishes loading, it is scrolled to the end again.
- **Stopping.** Scrolling toward the start stops following, whatever did it: a wheel, a finger, the scrollbar, <kbd>↑</kbd>, <kbd>Page Up</kbd> or <kbd>Home</kbd>. A wheel turned toward the start stops it at once, before the region has moved, so a word arriving at that instant cannot pull the reader back.
- **Following again.** Reaching the end again, with <kbd>End</kbd>, the scrollbar or the button, follows again. A few pixels short of the end still counts as the end.
- **Events.** The region dispatches `change` each time the reader makes it follow or stop. The behavior's `value` says whether it follows; set it to `true` to bring the reader to the end, as an application does when the reader sends a message. Setting it dispatches nothing.

```js name=app.js
import { follow } from '@master/ui/behaviors/follow.js'

const log = document.querySelector('.scroll-area[role="log"]')
const following = follow(log)

form.addEventListener('submit', () => { following.value = true })
```

Without the behavior the region is an ordinary scroll area: it stays where the reader left it, and the button stays hidden. The button stands sticky at the foot of the region, over the content and above the faded edge, and lays itself over the last row instead of after it, so showing it never makes the content taller.

## Keeping the reader's place \{#keeping-the-place\}

Content inserted above what the reader is looking at, such as older messages loaded at the top, would push what they are reading down. The browser prevents that by itself with scroll anchoring: the region moves with the content above, so the message being read stays where it was. The stylesheet keeps `overflow-anchor: auto` on the region and `none` on its button, which is never the element the browser holds still. Where a browser has no scroll anchoring, the `follow` behavior does the same itself, by as much as the content above moved. A line at the top that rows are inserted under, such as a note that more is loading, takes `overflow-anchor: none` too, so the browser holds a row still instead of the line.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- A region that scrolls must be reachable by the keys. Give it `tabindex="0"`, a `role` such as `region` and a label, unless it already holds focusable content.
- The mask fades the region's own scrollbar at the same edges. In forced colors the mask is removed.
- Give the region a height or a maximum height; without one it grows with its content and never scrolls.
- `role="log"` is a polite live region: what is added to it is announced without moving the focus. While an answer streams into a message, give that message `aria-busy="true"` and remove it when the answer is complete, so it is read once, whole, instead of word by word.
- The `scroll-area-latest` button is a real button in the tab order while it shows. Pressing it hides it, and the focus moves to the region, which then scrolls with the keys.
- Following never moves the focus, and stops as soon as the reader scrolls away, so content that keeps arriving never takes the page out from under someone reading it.
