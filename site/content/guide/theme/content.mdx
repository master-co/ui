Master UI has one material language. Light mode is **moonstone**, a clean zinc pearl. Dark mode is **obsidian**, a matte zinc glass whose surfaces step apart and keep a legible edge. Both are lit by one source above and slightly to the left, so every raised face catches a line of light on its top edge and settles on a short shadow that fades out.

The character is restrained: ink rather than color, half-pixel contours, corners that nest and depth that changes with state. Detail comes from light, not decoration, and no edge is harder than it needs to be to be found.

## Principles \{#principles\}

1. **Monochrome interface.** Controls use ink and pearl only. Hue marks status, always beside words that say the same thing; the brand color is kept for one moment per view.
2. **Zinc neutrals.** Every neutral is the preset's zinc, with a hue of 265 at almost no chroma. Lines and shadows carry no hue of their own.
3. **One material ladder.** Base, tray, raised, floating; an inset sits below its surroundings. Never invent a surface color.
4. **State is depth.** A control lifts by half a pixel on hover and sinks into a well when pressed. Do not express state by changing hue.
5. **Soft edges.** A contour is a half-pixel ring: one device pixel on a dense screen, finer than any border. An edge is found by its light and its shadow before its line.
6. **Continuous corners.** A circular arc meets a straight edge with a sudden change of curvature, which reads as a hard shoulder. Where the browser can draw them, corners are squircles: the curve starts earlier and turns gradually, like a polished stone.
7. **Concentric radii.** Radii nest by the inset between surfaces, so an outer corner is the inner one plus the space between them: a 10px control, a 16px sheet, a 20px tray 4px around it.
8. **Short shadows.** Every shadow layer doubles its offset, blurs by twice that offset and pulls its spread in by the offset. A shadow stays under its object and fades out; there is no single large blur and no hard lower edge.

## Surfaces \{#surfaces\}

Surfaces form one ladder. Each step is a role, not a color: pick the step by what the surface does, and the theme resolves its value in light and dark.

## Lines \{#lines\}

Lines are neutral ink alphas on moonstone and white alphas on obsidian. Two of them are 1px rules. The other three are contours, drawn as half-pixel rings with a zero-blur `box-shadow`, which takes no layout space; their alphas are tuned for that width, so a contour reads as fine rather than faint.

| Token | Moonstone | Obsidian | Drawn at | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--color-line-hair` | 6.5% | 7.5% | 1px | Quiet separators inside a surface |
| `--color-line-divider` | 12% | 13% | 1px | Rules between sections, tab rows |
| `--color-line-contour` | 12% | 14% | .5px | The outline of every surface |
| `--color-line-strong` | 20% | 23% | .5px | Hovered contours, switch tracks, keycaps |
| `--color-line-mark` | 32% | 36% | .5px | Checkboxes, a focused field's contour |

A control that must be found at a glance takes a firmer contour than a surface does: a field stacks two contour rings, and a checkbox uses the mark.

## Ink \{#ink\}

Ink is a face filled with the inverse color: a primary button, a checked checkbox, a sent message, the one [inverse sheet](/components/surface) of a page. Every ink face catches the same line of light on its top edge and takes the same hairline around it, so they read as one material at any size.

| Token | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `--color-surface-inverse-high`, `--color-surface-inverse-low` | The fall of light on a sheet of ink |
| `--color-ink-high`, `--color-ink-low` | The rounder fall on a control of ink, at rest |
| `--color-ink-high-hover`, `--color-ink-low-hover`, `--color-ink-pressed` | The same control under the pointer and under a press |
| `--color-ink-highlight` | The line of light on the top edge of any ink face |
| `--color-ink-edge` | The hairline around it |
| `--color-surface-tip` | A tooltip: ink on moonstone, a lighter slab on obsidian |
| `--shadow-ink`, `--shadow-ink-hover`, `--shadow-ink-pressed` | The depth of a control of ink in each state |
| `--shadow-ink-mark` | A small checked fill: a checkbox, a radio, a chosen day |
| `--shadow-ink-well`, `--shadow-thumb-on-ink` | A filled track, and the thumb that rides it |
| `--shadow-tip` | A tooltip and a chart's value |

A hued control (brand, a destructive confirmation) mixes its face, its edge and its glow from its own hue; `--color-tone-highlight` and `--color-tone-highlight-soft` are the light it catches.

## Shape \{#shape\}

Radii are roles, like surfaces. A chip is a small mark, a control is something pressed or typed in, and a surface is a sheet. Because the tokens are in `@theme`, they are utilities too: `r-control` gives a custom element the corner of a button.

| Token | Value | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--radius-chip` | 6px | Badges, keycaps, checkboxes, skeletons |
| `--radius-control-sm` | 8px | Small buttons and fields, menu rows, options, segmented thumbs |
| `--radius-control` | 10px | Buttons, fields, wells |
| `--radius-control-lg` | 12px | Large buttons and fields, alerts, choices, toasts |
| `--radius-surface` | 16px | Sheets, cards |

A holder has no radius of its own. It derives its corner from what it holds: the inner radius plus the inset between them. A tray is a sheet's corner plus 4px, a menu is a row's corner plus 4px, a segmented track is its thumb's corner plus its inset, and a button inside a field is the field's corner minus 4px. The gap therefore keeps one width around the bend. Only pills, dots and avatars are rounder.

### Continuous corners \{#continuous-corners\}

`border-radius` draws a quarter circle. Where a quarter circle meets a straight edge, the curvature jumps from zero to its full value at once, and the eye reads that joint as a shoulder. A squircle, drawn with `corner-shape: squircle`, starts turning earlier and tightens gradually, so the edge flows into the corner.

Components draw squircles where the browser supports `corner-shape`, and quarter circles elsewhere. A squircle hugs its corner more closely than a circle of the same radius, so a component multiplies its radius by `--corner-scale` to keep the same apparent roundness: 1.6 with squircles, 1 without. Both values are set once in the theme, and every component reads them.

| Property | With `corner-shape` | Without |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--corner-shape` | `squircle` | `round` |
| `--corner-scale` | `1.6` | `1` |

Pills, dots, avatars and thumbs stay true circles. To keep circular corners everywhere, set both properties back:

```css name=app.css
:root {
  --corner-shape: round;
  --corner-scale: 1;
}
```

## Type \{#type\}

Components inherit the page's font family. Master UI is designed with [Geist](https://vercel.com/font) and its stylistic sets, at a light regular weight for running text:

```css name=app.css
@theme {
  --font-sans: 'Geist';
  --font-feature-sans: 'ss02', 'ss03', 'ss04', 'ss06', 'ss07', 'ss08';
}

body {
  font-weight: 350;
}
```

Control labels use `--font-weight-control` (460), a light medium that reads clearly without looking heavy. Headings stay regular: size and ink carry the steps.

## Motion \{#motion\}

One easing family keeps interaction quick and explanation calm, and one length sets how far a control travels: `--lift` is the half pixel a raised control rises under the pointer and sinks under a press. A card rises twice as far. Set it to `0px` for a theme without travel; reduced motion already does.

| Token | Value | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--easing-glide` | `cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)` | Every state change |
| `--duration-press` | 60ms | Pressing a control |
| `--duration-quick` | 160ms | Hover and focus; popovers, menus and tooltips; any exit |
| `--duration-base` | 280ms | Thumbs and panels that move; a dialog arriving |
| `--lift` | .5px | How far a raised control rises and sinks |

Overlays write both directions in CSS. `@starting-style` gives the frame an element arrives from, and `display` and `overlay` transition with `allow-discrete`, so the exit plays before the element leaves the top layer. An exit is shorter than its entrance. With reduced motion, travel and scale are removed and the fade stays.

## Status and brand \{#status-and-brand\}

`--color-info`, `--color-success`, `--color-warning` and `--color-danger` are the only hues the interface uses to speak. Each is tuned to read as text on its theme.

The brand color is the one hue that is not a status. It appears on [`btn-brand`](/components/button#variants), once per view.

| Token | Value | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--color-brand` | `var(--color-indigo-50)` | The face of a brand surface |
| `--color-brand-ink` | near white | Text on a brand surface |
| `--color-brand-text` | indigo-60 on moonstone, indigo-30 on obsidian | Brand-colored text on the page |

A brand surface mixes its edge, its light and its glow from `--color-brand`, so overriding that one token retunes it. Set `--color-brand-ink` to a dark ink if your brand color is light.

## Color schemes \{#color-schemes\}

Every color token resolves with `light-dark()`, so the theme follows the element's `color-scheme`. The Master CSS preset sets `color-scheme: light dark` on the root and lets you pin a scheme for the page or for one subtree:

```html
<html data-theme="dark">
  …
  <aside data-theme="light">A moonstone panel on an obsidian page</aside>
</html>
```

There is no second stylesheet for dark mode and no dark-only class on components.

## Customize \{#customize\}

Override tokens after importing Master UI. Components read them through `var()`, so one change reaches every component:

```css name=app.css
@import '@master/css';
@import '@master/ui';

@theme {
  /* Rounder controls */
  --radius-control: .75rem;
  /* A warmer canvas */
  --color-surface-base: light-dark(oklch(98.4% .004 80), oklch(19.4% .004 60));
}
```

Because the tokens live in `@theme`, they are also utilities: `bg-surface-tray`, `shadow-lifted` and `b-line-contour` paint custom elements with the same materials.

A component stylesheet never writes a color of its own, so the tokens on this page are every decision of the material. [A theme of your own](/guide/theming) lists them by decision and shows a second theme built from them alone.

Importing Master UI tunes the preset's semantic tokens, such as `--color-surface-raised`, `--color-line-divider` and `--color-text-body`, to moonstone and obsidian, so the rest of your markup matches the components.
