Master UI draws its own charts. The [chart](/components/chart) is one SVG that draws more than twenty forms in the theme's tokens, from a line to a sunburst; each form has a page in [Charts](/charts). Beside it stand smaller components for a single figure, a trend or a share. This guide says which to reach for, shows several charts working together under one choice of period, and says when a charting engine is the better tool.

## Which one \{#choose\}

Start from what the reader has to see, not from a shape. Each of these draws from markup, follows the theme with no script and says its numbers in words.

| The reader has to see | Use |
| --- | --- |
| How values go on over time | A [line](/charts/line), or an [area](/charts/area) for one or two series |
| What rose and what fell between two moments | A [slope](/charts/slope); for two values of each thing in a list, a [dumbbell](/charts/dumbbell) |
| How ranks changed | A [bump chart](/charts/bump) |
| Amounts compared with each other | [Bars](/charts/bar); [across](/charts/bar#across) when the names are long |
| How one figure became another | A [waterfall](/charts/waterfall) |
| Spans between two values | A [range chart](/charts/range) |
| What totals are made of, over time or across groups | [Stacked bars](/charts/bar#stacked) or [stacked areas](/charts/area#stacked); a [marimekko](/charts/marimekko) when the groups differ in size |
| What one whole is made of | A [donut](/charts/donut) or a [pie](/charts/pie), of five parts at most; a [waffle](/charts/waffle) to the percent; a [treemap](/charts/treemap) for many parts; a [sunburst](/charts/sunburst) for two levels; along one bar, a [breakdown](/components/breakdown) |
| How much of a flow reaches each stage | A [funnel](/charts/funnel) |
| A few goals, each with a whole of its own | [Rings](/charts/rings) |
| A few things on the same measures | A [radar](/charts/radar) |
| How readings are spread | A [histogram](/charts/histogram); for several groups at once, a [box plot](/charts/box) |
| A pattern across two scales | A [heatmap](/charts/heatmap) |
| Two measures against each other | A [scatter](/charts/scatter), with [bubbles](/charts/scatter#bubble) for a third |
| One figure and how it moved | A [stat](/components/stat) |
| Which way a series went, in a sentence or a row | A [sparkline](/components/sparkline) |
| One reading and how near its limit it is | A [gauge](/components/gauge) or a [meter](/components/meter) |
| A pattern across many small values, with no scales beside it | A [heatmap](/components/heatmap) of cells |
| The exact numbers, compared in columns | A [table](/components/table) |

When a figure says it, do not draw it: a stat with its change reads faster than a chart of two bars.

## A dashboard \{#dashboard\}

Three figures and three charts under one choice of period. Choosing a period changes all of them together, from the same numbers, so what the figures say and what the charts show always agree.

<PagePreview name="dashboard" title="Chart dashboard" example="chart-dashboard" caption="Choose a period, point at a day, or hide a series from a legend." />

- **One control scopes everything under it.** The period stands in one row above the figures and the charts, never inside a card. A chart that needs a period of its own belongs to another view.
- **A chart is written again from its data.** `chart(element, options)` on an element that already holds a chart replaces it, and the function it returns stops it. The period's change calls it with the period's numbers.
- **The figure leads, the chart explains.** Each card names what it shows in its title; the chart's `label` says the same with its unit for a reader who does not see the drawing.
- **Each form does one job.** The stacked areas show how the total moved and what it was made of, the donut what the whole is made of now, and the bars a ranking with its values written out, since a ranking is read by its numbers.

## Colors from the theme \{#colors\}

A chart of one series is ink. It needs no color to be read, and it stands beside the monochrome interface around it without asking for attention.

Several series take a [category](/components/category) each. Left to itself a chart gives them in a fixed order, blue, orange, teal, violet, amber, pink, green and sky, in which neighbors stay apart for the common kinds of color blindness in both schemes. When a color follows a thing across a product, name the series' `category` yourself: Tokyo is blue in the chart, in its legend, in its badge and in its row of a table, wherever it stands in the list.

- **A hidden series does not repaint the others.** A series keeps its category when another leaves the chart.
- **A category is not a status.** Success, warning and danger say how something is; a series that is failing says so in words, in a [badge](/components/badge) or a sentence beside the chart. The one form that takes the status inks is the [waterfall](/charts/waterfall#inks), whose steps up and down are a change with a sign, as a stat's is.
- **Text is never in a series' color.** Names and values take the text tokens, and a mark of the color stands beside them: a block for a bar or a slice, a stroke for a line.
- **Steps of one color are for one measure.** A heatmap's cells, a funnel's stages and the outer ring of a sunburst take a share of one color, never a second hue.
- **The theme reaches every mark.** Marks read the category's `--color-category-<name>-mark` token and the ink reads `--chart-color`, so the color scheme, a theme and an override scoped to one region all apply, with nothing drawn again.

## One scale \{#scale\}

A chart has one scale. Two measures of different size, visitors in thousands and a rate in percent, are two charts one above the other on the same steps of time, never one chart with a scale on each side: a reader cannot tell which line belongs to which side, and the place where two lines cross means nothing.

A bar's scale starts at zero, since a bar's length is its value. A line may start higher when the change, not the amount, is the point; say so in the chart's `label`.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- **A chart is a list.** Each point is a list item named with its values, so a screen reader reads "Thu: New 1,420, Returning 820" with no script, and the arrow keys walk the same points for a reader who sees them.
- **The legend is controls.** Each entry is a label around a real checkbox, reached with Tab and toggled with Space.
- **The readout is a convenience.** It appears under the pointer, with the focus and under a finger, and never holds a value that is nowhere else. Where the exact numbers are the point, write them: `values: true` on a few bars, or a [table](/components/table) beside the chart.
- **Color is never alone.** A series is named in the legend and in the readout.
- **Motion follows the reader.** With reduced motion the charts appear drawn.

## When to use an engine \{#engine\}

The chart is drawn for a few hundred marks, each an element a reader can reach with the keys. Reach for a charting engine when the data is past that:

- thousands of points, or values that stream in many times a second;
- a zoom, a pan or a brush over the plot;
- a shape the chart does not draw: a map, a network or a tree, a candlestick, a Sankey diagram, or a density that needs a kernel, such as a violin or a ridgeline.

An engine that draws in SVG takes the theme's tokens as they are, in `fill` and `stroke`. One that draws on a canvas cannot read a custom property, and a token is written with `light-dark()`, so resolve each color where the chart stands: a hidden probe inside the chart's root takes the token as its color, and one pixel painted with it gives the color as `rgba()`. Resolve again when the color scheme or the theme changes, and keep the same rules as above: one scale, categories in a fixed order, a legend that names every series and the numbers as text.

```js name=theme.js
const probe = document.createElement('span')
probe.style.cssText = 'position:absolute;visibility:hidden'
root.append(probe)

function resolve(token) {
  probe.style.color = `var(${token})`
  pixel.fillStyle = getComputedStyle(probe).color
  pixel.fillRect(0, 0, 1, 1)
  const [r, g, b, a] = pixel.getImageData(0, 0, 1, 1).data
  return `rgba(${r}, ${g}, ${b}, ${a / 255})`
}
```
