A careers block lists the roles that are open: what the job is, which team it is in, where it is done and on what terms, with the way to apply. A candidate decides from those four facts whether to read on, so each row carries all of them. The roles are a [list](/components/list) on a [sheet](/components/surface#sheet).

Blocks are markup to copy. They take the page's own text color and size; the previews set `fg-text-body text-sm` on the body. Each block is a size container, so it reflows by its own width.

## What is in it \{#anatomy\}

- **The number of roles in the lead.** "Five roles are open" tells a visitor how long the list is before they scroll it, and agrees with the rows under it.
- **One reading column**, `max-w(720)`: a list of roles is scanned down its start edge, and a wider row would only put the terms farther from the title they belong to.
- **Each role is a link.** The list is `list list-interactive` and each row is an `<a class="list-row">`, so the whole row is the target, answers the pointer and is 44px tall at least on a touch screen. The sheet's `p-xs` is the room the rows' rounded faces stand in.
- **The title is the job; the line under it is the team and the place**, parted by a middle dot. Both keep one line, so a place is written as short as it is said: "Taipei or remote".
- **The terms are a neutral [badge](/components/badge)** at the end of the row. "Full-time" and "Part-time" are words, and a role's terms are not a status, so the badge takes no hue.
- **A chevron after the badge** says the row goes somewhere. It is decoration, `aria-hidden`.
- **A way in for the role that is not listed**: one muted line under the sheet, with an address.

## By team \{#by-team\}

When the roles are many, or a candidate looks for a team first, group them. Each team is a row of an [accordion](/components/accordion): native `<details>`, so every role is in the page for search and for find-in-page, and no script opens one. The rows share a `name`, which keeps one team open at a time.

The count beside a team's name is what lets a closed row be skipped: a candidate sees "1 role" without opening it. Inside a row the team is already said, so the line under a title is the place and the terms, and "Apply" is the one link of the row. Where the browser does not know the `name` attribute, the rows open and close on their own, and nothing is lost.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The block is a `<section>` named by its `<h2>`.
- In the first list each role is one link whose name is everything in the row: the title, the team, the place and the terms, read in that order.
- In the grouped list "Apply" repeats, so each link has an `aria-label` that starts with the same word and names the role: "Apply for Product designer".
- A `<summary>` is a button the browser already makes: Enter and Space open it, and its state is announced. The count is text inside it, so it is part of the row's name.
- The middle dot between two facts is punctuation in a sentence here; where it is read aloud awkwardly, write "in" or a comma instead.
- Keep a closed role out of the list. A row that leads to "this position has been filled" costs a candidate a click and some hope.
