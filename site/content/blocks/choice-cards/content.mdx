Choice cards ask for one decision that needs a sentence to make: which plan, who reviews, how to pay. Each option is a [choice](/components/choice), a `<label>` around a native radio or checkbox, so the whole card is the target and the input keeps the state, the keys and the form value. No script is needed.

Blocks are markup to copy. They take the page's own text color and size; the previews set `fg-text-body text-sm` on the body. Each block is a size container, so it reflows by its own width: in a wide page, a sidebar's worth of room or a dialog.

## What is in it \{#anatomy\}

- **A `<fieldset>` with a `<legend>`.** The legend is the question and names the group of radios, so each option is read as an answer to it. It takes the heading's type with `heading heading-sm`.
- **Each option is a [choice](/components/choice).** The radio sits at its start; a chosen card rises and its contour turns to ink, which a reader sees without hue, and the radio's own mark says the same.
- **The words carry the decision.** A title, a [price](/components/price) where there is one, and one sentence on what the option gives. What applies now, the current plan or the default method, is a neutral [badge](/components/badge) at the end of the first line.
- **The cards reflow by the block's width.** `auto-grid auto-grid-fit` with `--auto-grid-min` sets the plans side by side in a wide room, sharing the whole row, and one under another in a narrow one; no breakpoint is written.
- **The summary agrees with the choice.** The line before the action says what the chosen option costs with the seats there are, and the action names it.

## People \{#people\}

Checkboxes when several can be chosen: who reviews, who is notified. Each card holds the person's [avatar](/components/avatar), their name and their role. A line under the cards says how many are chosen and what that means, and the fieldset is described by it.

## Payment methods \{#payment-methods\}

Saved methods to pay with, one at a time. A method is named by what a reader recognizes, the brand and the last four figures, and what makes it differ: when it expires, how long it takes, how much credit is left. The icon in an [icon frame](/components/icon-frame) is decoration; the title says what it is.

The block chooses among methods that are already saved. It never asks for a card number: adding a method is the payment provider's own form or page, which holds the number so the application never does.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- Each block is a `<section>` named by its legend's id, and the legend names the fieldset for every option in it.
- The input inside each card is a real radio or checkbox: the arrow keys move within a group of radios, Space toggles a checkbox, and a form submits the value.
- The whole card is the target, at least 44px tall on a touch screen; the focus ring is drawn around the card, not the small input.
- What is chosen is told by the input's own mark and the card's ink contour, never by color alone. Badges say "Current" and "Default" in words.
- An avatar beside a name is decoration: its `alt` is empty, and initials are `aria-hidden`.
