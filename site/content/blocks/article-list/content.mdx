An article list shows what there is to read: a picture, a title that is the way in, a sentence that says what the piece holds, who wrote it and when. The same block lists a blog's posts, a library of guides or a set of case studies, since all three are a title, a summary and a source. Each piece is a [card](/components/card).

Blocks are markup to copy. They take the page's own text color and size; the previews set `fg-text-body text-sm` on the body. Each block is a size container, so it reflows by its own width.

## What is in it \{#anatomy\}

- **A heading group with the way to the rest.** The title stands at the start and "All articles" at the end of the same row, `flex flex-wrap items-end justify-between`; in a narrow block the button wraps under the title. Three cards are a sample, and the button says where the others are.
- **Each piece is a `card card-split` on a list item.** From 64rem of block the three stand side by side, `grid-cols(3)@container(--5xl)`, with the picture on top. Below that they stack, and each card sets its picture beside its words while it has 28rem of its own, then on top again in a phone's width. The card decides that from its own width; the block only decides the columns.
- **The title is the way in.** It is an `<a class="card-link">`, whose target covers the whole card, so the picture and the padding follow the link too. The summary and the author's line stand above that target, and their words can still be selected.
- **A summary that says what the piece holds**, two sentences at most, in `card-description`. A summary that repeats the title has wasted the line.
- **What kind of piece it is** is a neutral [badge](/components/badge) in `card-action`, at the end of the header. It is text, and it takes no hue: a kind is not a status.
- **Who and when, in one quiet line.** An [avatar](/components/avatar), the name in ink and the date in a `<time>`. `mt-auto` holds the line to the foot of the card, so three cards of different lengths end on one line.

Order the pieces newest first, and write the year into the date.

## A featured piece \{#featured\}

When one piece should lead, as a case study leads a library of guides, give it half the block: a [figure](/components/figure), a larger title and its summary. The three beside it are a title and a line each, parted by hairlines. From 64rem of block the two halves stand side by side; below that the featured piece comes first.

The featured piece has no surface: a figure and a column of words on the page are enough to lead, and a card beside three bare titles would shout. The hairline between two titles, `bt(1) bt-solid b-line-hair`, is the one line utilities draw.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The block is a `<section>` named by its `<h2>`; each piece's title is an `<h3>`, so the list of headings is the list of pieces.
- A title is one link, and its words say where it goes. There is no "Read more": the title already is the way in, and three links named "Read more" tell a screen reader's list of links nothing.
- The pictures in the example are atmosphere, so their `alt` is empty. A picture that shows what the piece is about gets a description.
- A portrait beside a name has an empty `alt`: the name is already there.
- The date is a `<time>` with a `datetime`, written with its month as a word. "10/11" is two different days to two readers.
- The dots between the facts are `aria-hidden`.
