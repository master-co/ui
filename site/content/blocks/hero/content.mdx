A hero is the first screen of a page: what this is, why it matters, and one thing to do about it. The words are a [heading](/components/heading) at its display size, which follows the width of the block, and a lead under it. Everything else is optional.

Blocks are markup to copy. They take the page's own text color and size; the previews set `fg-text-body text-sm` on the body. Each block is a size container, so it reflows by its own width.

## What is in it \{#anatomy\}

- **One measure, centered.** `max-w(760) mx-auto text-center` on a column with `gap-lg`. The lead has its own, narrower measure, so its lines stay short under a wide title.
- **A display heading.** `heading heading-display` is sized in container units: 2.5rem in a phone's column and 4.25rem in a wide one, with balanced lines at every size. The section is the container.
- **Two actions at most.** The one you want is a `btn-brand` or a `btn-primary`; the other is a default or a quiet button. `flex flex-wrap justify-center` lets them stack on a narrow screen without a query.
- **A small line of reassurance.** What it costs to try, in `text-xs fg-text-muted`.

The brand button is the system's one hued control. With a hero that uses it, nothing else on the first screen should.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The hero's title is the page's `<h1>`. A page has one.
- An eyebrow is a `<p>`, not a heading: it says what kind of thing follows, and a screen reader's list of headings should start with the title.
- Buttons that go somewhere are links, `<a class="btn">`. Buttons that submit are `<button>`.
- Describe the picture in `alt` when it shows the product; leave it empty when it is atmosphere.
