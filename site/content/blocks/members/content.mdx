A members block is where a workspace's owner sees who can get in and as what, changes a role, and asks someone new to join. The people are a [list](/components/list) on a [sheet](/components/surface#sheet), each role a native [select](/components/select), and the invitation a form in a [dialog](/components/dialog). The invitations nobody has answered yet stand under the people, apart from them, because an address that was written to is not yet a member.

Blocks are markup to copy. They take the page's own text color and size; the previews set `fg-text-body text-sm` on the body. Each block is a size container, so it reflows by its own width: in a wide page, a sidebar's worth of room or a dialog.

## What is in it \{#anatomy\}

- **The count, and what it costs.** The line under the title says how many people there are and that each takes a seat. Whoever adds a person here is spending money, and should know it before the dialog opens.
- **One action for the page.** "Invite a member" is the one primary [button](/components/button), at the end of the header. It opens the dialog with `commandfor` and `command="show-modal"`, so no script is involved in opening or closing it.
- **People are rows of a `list`.** An [avatar](/components/avatar) is the `list-media`, the name and the address are the `list-body`, and the role and the row's menu are the `list-end`. The media and the end center on the two lines of words.
- **A role is a native `<select>`.** `select-sm` keeps it the height of the small button beside it. A row has no label to spare, so each select is named for its person: "Role of Po-han Chen".
- **The owner's row has no control.** The person signed in cannot demote or remove themselves, so their role is words in muted ink, and a [badge](/components/badge) in the title says "You". Ownership moves from another row's [menu](/components/menu), "Make owner".
- **What cannot be undone is behind the menu**, in the danger ink, and names where the person is removed from. It does not sit in the row as a button: a row of five red buttons makes the page about leaving.
- **Invitations are a second list.** An invitation has an address and no person yet, so its row has no avatar. Its description says the role, who sent it and when it stops working, each date in a `<time>`.
- **The dialog says what the choice costs.** The hint under the role says what each role may do in a sentence, and the last line says that the seats are full and what one more adds. The figures agree with the [billing summary](/blocks/billing-summary): five seats, $20 each.

The block shows the state a server holds. Changing a role, resending and revoking are requests your application sends; after each, write the result to a `role="status"` region so it is announced, and move focus to the next row when a row leaves.

## What each role can do \{#roles\}

Before choosing a role, a reader wants to compare them. That is a [table](/components/table): the roles are the columns, what can be done the rows, and each cell says yes or no. It goes under the members, or on a page of its own that the dialog's hint links to.

- **A check and a dash, each with a word.** The check is an [icon](/components/icon) hidden from assistive technology and the dash a character; beside each is a `sr-only` "Yes" or "No", so a cell is read as an answer and not as "image" or nothing.
- **The rows run from what everyone may do to what one person may.** Read down a column and a role ends where its checks end; read along a row and the dashes begin where the permission stops.
- **It scrolls sideways, it does not restack.** A matrix read as cards is no longer a comparison. In a narrow room the `scroll-area` scrolls along the inline axis and fades the edge where columns continue; it has `tabindex="0"`, a role and a name, since nothing inside it takes focus.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The block is a `<section>` named by its `<h2>`. The pending invitations are a section of their own under an `<h3>`, so a reader moving by headings finds both lists.
- A control in a row is named with its row: "Role of Seo-yeon Kim", "More for Seo-yeon Kim", "Revoke the invitation to yuna@atlaslabs.dev". Five controls all called "Role" cannot be told apart in a list of form fields.
- The portraits repeat the names beside them, so their `alt` is empty.
- The dialog is named by its title and described by the sentence under it, and focus goes to the email field when it opens. When it closes, the browser returns focus to the button that opened it.
- The dialog's submit button stands in the footer, outside the form, and is tied to it with `form`. Enter in the email field submits, and the browser's own validation holds the dialog open on an address it cannot send to.
