A billing summary answers what an account's owner comes to check: which plan the workspace is on, what it costs, how much of it is used and when it renews, with the way to change any of it. The amount is a [price](/components/price), the usage a [meter](/components/meter) and the rest a [data list](/components/data-list), on one [sheet](/components/surface#sheet).

Blocks are markup to copy. They take the page's own text color and size; the previews set `fg-text-body text-sm` on the body. Each block is a size container, so it reflows by its own width: in a wide page, a sidebar's worth of room or a dialog.

## What is in it \{#anatomy\}

- **The plan and its state, in words.** The plan's name is the block's heading, and a [badge](/components/badge) beside it says "Active". The tone is a status here, and the word says the same.
- **The amount is a `price`**: the figures, the currency mark hung small from their top, and the period in muted ink on their baseline. "Before tax" is part of the period, because a figure that leaves the tax out has to say so where it is read.
- **How the amount is made** in one muted line: five seats at twenty dollars. A reader should be able to do the sum.
- **The two ways to change it** at the end of the header, as links drawn as small buttons. Neither is primary: nothing on a summary is the one thing to do. In a narrow block they wrap under the price.
- **Usage is a native `<meter>`.** Its `low`, `high` and `optimum` say where the reading stops being comfortable, 1,500 and 1,800 of 2,000 minutes, so the fill turns to the warning ink and then the danger ink without a class. The meter prints no number: the reading stands beside its label, and the hint says what is left and when it resets.
- **The dates and the rest are a `data-list`**, a term and a value to a row. Under 20rem of list each term moves above its value.
- **The figures agree.** Five seats at $20 are $100; five seats at 400 minutes are 2,000; 1,240 used leave 760.

No card number, expiry or address is shown or asked for here. Where a product shows the payment method, it is the brand and the last four digits the payment provider returns, never a field of the block.

- **Three facts in a row** are a `<dl>` laid out with utilities, each term over its value, three columns from 36rem of block. The next invoice is a `price price-sm`; the other two are words.
- **The invoices appear newest first.** Each amount has two decimal places. `table-numeric` aligns the ends and uses equal-width digits, which lines up the decimal points at that precision. September’s $80 covers four seats.
- **An invoice's number is its link.** It is the row header, so each cell is announced with the invoice it belongs to.
- **The table scrolls sideways in a [scroll area](/components/scroll-area)** if its room is narrower than its columns, inside the sheet, which clips the corners.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The summary is a `<section>` named by its heading. In the second example the `<h2>` names the block and the table has a `<caption>`, so the table is announced by name.
- The meter has a `<label>`, and the reading is text beside it. A meter's color is drawn from `low`, `high` and `optimum`; the words must say the same thing the color does once it changes.
- A status is a word with a dot, "Active" and "Paid". The hue repeats it; it never carries it.
- Dates are written with the month as a word, and each is a `<time>` in the table.
- "Change plan" and "Manage seats" go to other pages, so they are links drawn as buttons.
- Write the currency once in words. A "$" alone is several currencies.
