Blocks are markup to copy. They take the page's own text color and size; the previews set `fg-text-body text-sm` on the body. Each block is a size container, so it reflows by its own width.

## What is in it \{#anatomy\}

- **A grid by the block's width.** One column, two from 42rem and three from 64rem: `grid-cols(2)@container(--2xl) grid-cols(3)@container(--5xl)`. Six people fill every one of those without a card left alone on a row; with five or seven, drop to the count that divides.
- **Each person is a `card` on a list item.** The avatar comes first, then the card's header with the name as `card-title` and the role as `card-description`, then the sentence in `card-body`.
- **The face is an `avatar avatar-xl`**, 56px: large enough to recognize, small enough that six of them are not a wall of portraits. It keeps its circle whatever the photograph's shape.
- **A role says what the person does for the reader.** "Build systems" tells a customer whom a question about builds reaches; a rank does not.
- **One sentence each, about the work.** Keep them within a line of each other in length, as the cards share a row.
- **One quiet link, held to the foot** with `mt-auto`, so the links line up across a row. It is a `link-quiet` in muted ink: there to be found, not to compete with the name.

## A short list \{#list\}

Where the people are an aside, as beside a contact form or in an account page, three rows are enough. A [list](/components/list) on a [sheet](/components/surface#sheet): the avatar as `list-media`, the name over the role, and the way to write at the end of the row.

A list's title and description each keep one line and end in an ellipsis, so write a role that fits a phone: a function and a place, not a sentence. The hairlines start under the names, which leaves the avatars as one unbroken column.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The block is a `<section>` named by its `<h2>`. In the grid each name is an `<h3>`, so a reader can move from person to person by heading.
- A portrait beside a name has `alt=""` and a `width` and `height`: the name is already in the text, and a screen reader would otherwise say it twice.
- A link that repeats across people is told apart by its name. "GitHub" and "Email" carry an `aria-label` that starts with the same word and adds whose it is: "GitHub profile of Po-han Chen", "Email Mei-ling Wang".
- The role and the sentence are text, never a picture of text or a tooltip over the face.
- Ask before you publish a person. Show the name and the photograph they chose.
