A changelog says what shipped and when, newest first, so a visitor can see that a product moves and a customer can find what changed under them. Releases are moments in order, which is what a [timeline](/components/timeline) draws; what each one changed is [bullets](/components/bullets).

Blocks are markup to copy. They take the page's own text color and size; the previews set `fg-text-body text-sm` on the body. Each block is a size container, so it reflows by its own width.

## What is in it \{#anatomy\}

- **One reading column**, `max-w(720)`. A changelog is read down, and its lines should stay short enough to scan.
- **The rhythm in the lead.** "Every three weeks" tells a reader how fresh the newest entry is likely to be; the dates agree with it.
- **An `<ol class="timeline">`, newest first.** The order is the content. Each release is a `timeline-item` with an empty marker, and a hairline joins it to the one before.
- **The newest release is `aria-current="true"`**: its dot takes ink and a faint halo. A `badge badge-ink` beside its name says "Latest" in words, since a dot is not a sentence.
- **A version, then its date**, on one row that wraps: the name as `timeline-title` on an `<h3>`, the date in `timeline-time` on a `<time>`, written with its month as a word.
- **One sentence that says why the release matters**, in `timeline-description`, before the list of what changed.
- **Three changes each, as `bullets`**: whole sentences about what a customer can now do, in the customer's words. The commit log is somewhere else.
- **A link to the full notes** under each release, named with its version, and one button to all of them at the foot.

Three releases are a sample of a changelog, not the changelog. Keep the block to the last few and send the rest to their own page.

## One release \{#one-release\}

Inside a product, or beside an article, one release is enough: what is new, on a [sheet](/components/surface#sheet). The changes are in an [expand](/components/expand), a native `<details>` that shows their first lines and opens to the rest, so a long release does not push the page down.

- **The toggle says how much there is.** "Show all five changes" is a promise a reader can weigh; "Show more" is not.
- **Nothing to press is clipped.** The two links stand after the `<details>`, outside it: a link inside the part that fades cannot be seen but can still take focus.
- **`--expand-lines: 5`** sets how much shows while it is closed: the sentence and the first change or two, enough to decide whether to open it.
- **Without `::details-content`** the text is hidden while closed, as in any `<details>`, and the toggle still opens it.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The block is a `<section>` named by its `<h2>`; each release is an `<h3>`, so the versions are the list of headings.
- The timeline is an ordered list, and its order, newest first, is the one a screen reader reads.
- `aria-current="true"` marks the newest release for assistive technology as the dot marks it for the eye, and "Latest" says it to everyone.
- Each date is a `<time>` with a `datetime`.
- "Read the 4.2 notes" names its release. Three links that all say "Read more" cannot be told apart in a list of links.
- The expand's `<summary>` is a button the browser makes: Enter and Space open it, and its state is announced. Its two wordings swap with the state.
