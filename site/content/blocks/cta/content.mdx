Blocks are markup to copy. They take the page's own text color and size; the previews set `fg-text-body text-sm` on the body. Each block is a size container, so it reflows by its own width.

## What is in it \{#anatomy\}

- **[`sheet sheet-inverse`](/components/surface#inverse).** Ink on moonstone and pearl on obsidian, with the same fall of light as a primary button. Use one on a page.
- **Inverse ink on the words.** The heading and the lead take `fg-text-inverse`; a utility comes after the heading's own color.
- **A default button as the action.** On ink, the ordinary raised button is the brightest thing in view. A primary button would be ink on ink.
- **One sentence and one action**, with a quiet second for the visitor who is not ready.

## Quiet \{#quiet\}

When the page already ends strongly, or the same ask repeats on every page, use the ordinary materials: a [sheet](/components/surface#sheet) in a [tray](/components/surface#tray), words at the start and buttons at the end. `flex flex-wrap justify-between` drops the buttons under the words when they no longer fit.

## With a field \{#field\}

When the action is an address, put the field and its button in a wrapping row. `grow(1) basis(200)` lets the field take the row and gives it 200px before the button drops below.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The inverse sheet keeps full text contrast in both schemes: its ink and its text are the theme's own inverse pair.
- A field needs a name. Here it is `aria-label="Email"`; a visible label is better when there is room.
- Say what happens after the click: "Start deploying" is a promise, "Submit" is not.
