A centered layout puts one task in the middle of the viewport and nothing else: signing in, confirming an address, an error page. The frame is a [page](/layouts/page), so it is as tall as the viewport in `dvh`; two utilities center what is in it.

## How it is built \{#how-it-is-built\}

- **`page items-center justify-center`.** The page is a column as tall as the viewport; the two utilities center its content on both axes. `p-lg` keeps the card off the edges of a phone.
- **`w-full max-w(380)` on the card.** It fills a phone and stops at a comfortable width for one column of fields.
- **The tray under the sheet.** `bg-surface-tray` on the frame and a [sheet](/components/surface#sheet) for the card: the card is the one raised thing on the page.
- **When the card is taller than the viewport, the page scrolls.** A minimum height, not a fixed one, is what makes that work. A phone with its keyboard open is this case.

The card is also a [form](/layouts/form), which sets the distance between its fields.

## Beside a panel \{#split\}

From the `md` breakpoint the frame becomes two equal columns: the task in one, and a panel in the other for the brand, a quotation or a picture. Below it the panel leaves, and the task is alone again.

`grid grid-cols(2)@md` splits the frame; the panel is `hidden flex@md`. This one measures the viewport, with `@md`, not a container: the frame is the viewport.

The panel is ink, `bg-surface-inverse` with `fg-text-inverse`. Keep it to a few words: it is the first thing that leaves, so nothing a reader needs may live there.

## Accessibility \{#accessibility\}

- The page has one `<h1>`, in the card.
- The panel is an `<aside>`. When it holds only decoration, hide it from assistive technology with `aria-hidden="true"`; when it holds a quotation, it is content and stays.
- Fields carry `autocomplete` values, so a password manager can fill them.
