A histogram shows how readings are spread: where most of them lie, how far they reach, whether they lean to one side. The readings of the first series are sorted into bins between round numbers, and each bin is a bar as tall as the count of readings in it. The bins touch, since the scale under them runs on without a break.

Give the raw readings as the series' `values`, not counts: the chart counts them. `bins` asks for about that many bins; left out, their number follows the count of readings, between five and twenty. The edges are round numbers, so a bin reads as "200 to 250". A reading on an edge belongs to the bin that begins there.

A histogram is not a bar chart of names: its bars are ranges of one measure, in order. Amounts by name are [bars](/charts/bar), and five figures that sum a spread up are a [box plot](/charts/box).

## Options \{#options\}

| Option | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `label` | none | What the chart shows and in what unit: the plot's accessible name |
| `series` | `[]` | Each with a `name` and what its form reads: `values`, a `value`, `points`, `ranges` or `children`; optionally a `category`, a `type`, `low` and `high`, and a ring's `max` |
| `bins` | from the count | About how many bins a histogram sorts its readings into |
| `min`, `max`, `ticks` | fit, `4` | The ends of the scale and about how many steps mark it; `xMin`, `xMax` and `xTicks` for a scatter's scale across |
| `format`, `tickFormat` | the locale's | How a value and a tick are written; `xFormat` for a scatter's scale across |
| `current` | none | The index of the point marked `aria-current` |

The readout, the keys, the legend, the colors and the scale are the same in every form: [Chart](/components/chart) holds them, with every option, class and custom property.
