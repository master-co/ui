A heatmap shows a pattern across two scales at once: the hours of a week, the months of several years. Each series is a row and each label a column, and the cell where they meet is as dark as its value between the lowest and the highest. Tone is the only channel, so a heatmap says where the high and the low values lie, not what they are.

The lowest value keeps a trace of ink, so a cell that holds a value is told from one that holds none; a value of `null` leaves its cell out. `min` and `max` hold the ends of the range, so two heatmaps side by side share one. A row's cells take its series' `category` when it names one; otherwise they are ink.

This is the heatmap with two named scales. A field of many small cells with no scales beside it, such as a year of days, is the [heatmap](/components/heatmap) component, which is a list the stylesheet draws with no script.

## Options \{#options\}

| Option | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `label` | none | What the chart shows and in what unit: the plot's accessible name |
| `labels` | `[]` | The names along the scale, around a radar or over a heatmap's columns |
| `series` | `[]` | Each with a `name` and what its form reads: `values`, a `value`, `points`, `ranges` or `children`; optionally a `category`, a `type`, `low` and `high`, and a ring's `max` |
| `min`, `max`, `ticks` | fit, `4` | The ends of the scale and about how many steps mark it; `xMin`, `xMax` and `xTicks` for a scatter's scale across |
| `format`, `tickFormat` | the locale's | How a value and a tick are written; `xFormat` for a scatter's scale across |

The readout, the keys, the legend, the colors and the scale are the same in every form: [Chart](/components/chart) holds them, with every option, class and custom property.
