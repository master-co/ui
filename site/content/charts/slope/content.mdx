A slope chart sets two moments side by side and draws a straight line for each series from one to the other. What rose, what fell and what overtook what is read from the slopes alone. There is no scale to read: each line's value is written at its first end and its value and name at its last, so the chart needs no legend.

A series is one point of the chart, not a step of the scale: under the pointer, or with the arrow keys, a whole line is chosen and the others step back. Labels that would meet at an end are nudged apart, each staying as near its line as the ones above it allow. Past six or seven series the ends crowd; fold the rest into one line or draw a [line chart](/charts/line) with a legend.

More than two labels are allowed, and each line then turns at every one of them. With many labels a [line chart](/charts/line) reads better, since a slope's strength is the single stroke from one moment to the other.

## Options \{#options\}

| Option | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `label` | none | What the chart shows and in what unit: the plot's accessible name |
| `labels` | `[]` | The names along the scale, around a radar or over a heatmap's columns |
| `series` | `[]` | Each with a `name` and what its form reads: `values`, a `value`, `points`, `ranges` or `children`; optionally a `category`, a `type`, `low` and `high`, and a ring's `max` |
| `min`, `max` | fit | The ends of the scale both columns share |
| `format` | the locale's | How a value is written |
| `current` | none | The index of the point marked `aria-current` |

The readout, the keys, the legend, the colors and the scale are the same in every form: [Chart](/components/chart) holds them, with every option, class and custom property.
