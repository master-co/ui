A radar compares a few things on the same measures at a glance: two releases on six scores, a profile. Each series is an outline with a wash of the same color, so two show through each other. The measures share one scale, so give `max` when they have a known top. Point at a spoke to read every series on that measure.

A radar reads well with two or three series and five to eight measures. The exact scores belong in a [table](/components/table) beside it.

## Options \{#options\}

| Option | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `label` | none | What the chart shows and in what unit: the plot's accessible name |
| `labels` | `[]` | The names along the scale, or around a radar |
| `series` | `[]` | Each with a `name` and `values`, a `value` for a slice or a ring, or `points` for a scatter; optionally a `category`, a `type` and a ring's `max` |
| `min`, `max`, `ticks` | fit, `4` | The ends of the scale and about how many steps mark it; `xMin`, `xMax` and `xTicks` for a scatter's scale across |
| `format`, `tickFormat` | the locale's | How a value and a tick are written; `xFormat` for a scatter's scale across |
| `legend`, `toggle` | with two series, `true` | Whether a legend is written, and whether its entries are checkboxes |

The readout, the keys, the legend, the colors and the scale are the same in every form: [Chart](/components/chart) holds them, with every option, class and custom property.
