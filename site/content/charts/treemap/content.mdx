A treemap shows the parts of a whole as tiles whose areas are their shares. It holds more parts than a pie can, and a small part is still a tile a reader can point at. The tiles are laid out largest first and kept as near to squares as their sizes allow, since a square's area is easier to judge than a sliver's.

A tile says its name where it has room for it, and its value under the name where it has room for both; every tile says both in the readout and in its point's name, so nothing is lost on a small tile. Tiles are a wash of ink. A series that names a `category` takes that category's surface, for parts a product already colors.

## Options \{#options\}

| Option | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `label` | none | What the chart shows and in what unit: the plot's accessible name |
| `series` | `[]` | Each with a `name` and what its form reads: `values`, a `value`, `points`, `ranges` or `children`; optionally a `category`, a `type`, `low` and `high`, and a ring's `max` |
| `format`, `tickFormat` | the locale's | How a value and a tick are written; `xFormat` for a scatter's scale across |
| `current` | none | The index of the point marked `aria-current` |

The readout, the keys, the legend, the colors and the scale are the same in every form: [Chart](/components/chart) holds them, with every option, class and custom property.
